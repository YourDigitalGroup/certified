# Getting Ready to Sell — Process Script
**Pillar:** Process · Block 2 of 11
**Audience:** Owners, managers, and account executives at partner groups — internal
**Format:** Motion graphics over real screen capture and real photography · AI voice read (two voices) · animated text · green callout boxes
**Runtime:** 1457 counted spoken words ≈ **9:43** at 150 wpm
**Spine — the one sentence somebody must repeat cold:** *Tell all of them. And put digital in every pitch.*

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES — READ BEFORE RECORDING

**Two voices.** **V1 — Guide** carries all teaching. **V2 — Rep** says the thing out loud. Distinctly different ElevenLabs voice.

**Written for an AI read.** Short sentences. No semicolons, no parentheticals.

**The humour is recognition, not jokes.** Nothing depends on a pause or a dry delivery.

**This is the second half of the launch.** Block one settled what you are called and how you describe us. This block is everything you do before you talk to a client. **It depends on block one being done** — the announcement in Segment 3 assumes the partnership decision is already made.

**Tone.** This block is mostly good news. A website they do not have to build, content written for them, training with somebody sitting alongside. Lead with that. **Never scare anybody about launching.**

**Visuals.** Real partner websites and listings, real screen capture of the certification and training schedule, real photography for the client-announcement beats. **No illustrations, no icon cards, no mascots.**

## SEGMENT 2 — WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

**ON-SCREEN:** Two paths build as text — *announced and ready* versus *announced and guessing* — with a **green box** on the first. Then a real partner website on a phone.

**V1 — Guide:** So why is there an order to this?

**V1 — Guide:** Picture what happens if you skip ahead. You announce the new agency, a client gets curious and asks a perfectly reasonable question, and the person in front of them does not know the answer.

**V1 — Guide:** Nothing dramatic happens. That is the problem. It just quietly becomes a press release instead of a business, and the next time that client thinks about digital they think about somebody else.

**V1 — Guide:** Same with your own house. If you are pitching a website while your own site loads slowly on a phone, you will know it while you are talking. Clients rarely check. It still shows.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So get ready first, then announce."*

**V1 — Guide:** Ready, then announce. And getting ready takes less work than you would think, because most of it is already handled.

## SEGMENT 3 — THREE THINGS TO DO

**ON-SCREEN:** Three numbered items animate in as text, then each gets its own visual sequence.

### One — get your own house in order

**ON-SCREEN:** A real partner website on a phone, a real Google listing, a real review profile. Then a **green box** on a live blog post on a partner agency site.

**V1 — Guide:** Look at your own. Your website. Your Google listing. Your reviews. Your social pages.

**V1 — Guide:** Because here is the uncomfortable truth. **You cannot sell a website with a bad website.**

**V1 — Guide:** So here is the best news in this whole program. **Every group gets a website. We build it. You do not lift a finger.**

**V2 — Rep:** *"Wait. That is included?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Automatically, at onboarding. Not a project you have to schedule, not something you have to budget for. It is the first thing that happens.

**V1 — Guide:** And there is a second piece that comes with it, which surprises people. **We write a blog for your agency every other week, and we post it on your site for you.** No extra charge.

**V1 — Guide:** Two things that does. Your website stops looking abandoned, which is exactly what a business owner will notice if they go looking. And you get something worth sending — take that article, drop it into an email, and send it to your client database. You are now doing content marketing and email marketing for your own agency, and somebody else wrote it.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So we are actually doing the thing we are selling."*

**V1 — Guide:** That is the whole idea. Walk the walk.

**V1 — Guide:** And if you want to go further, there are **walk-the-walk add-ons** available. We can manage your **social media.** We can take over your **Google Business Profile** and optimise it. We can build **targeted landing pages** for your agency. And we can set up an **automated program that identifies businesses visiting your website** and never reaching out, so you can follow up.

**V1 — Guide:** Those are paid add-ons, unlike the website and the blog. Take all of it or none of it. But the website and the every-other-week blog are yours either way.

### Two — get your team trained

**ON-SCREEN:** Real screen capture of the certification program and the weekly training schedule with Zoom links. **Green box** on the schedule.

**V1 — Guide:** Somebody has to be able to have the conversation. Ideally everybody who talks to clients.

**V1 — Guide:** And this is where we would push hardest, because it is the clearest difference between the groups that do well with this and the groups that struggle. **The successful ones get certified.** All of them. It is not a coincidence.

**V1 — Guide:** Two ways to do it, and they work together.

**V1 — Guide:** **First, the guided certification course.** You can work through it on your own, at your own pace. But you do not have to, and we would rather you did not. **Your account manager will meet with your team every week** and walk you through it until your people are certified.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So somebody is actually going to sit with us."*

**V1 — Guide:** Every week, for as long as it takes. A real person who knows this business, answering the questions your team actually has. Nobody is handing you a login and wishing you luck.

**V1 — Guide:** **Second, supplemental sales training every Wednesday on Zoom.** Separate from certification, and it keeps going. Live and focused on selling — what is working, what to say, how to handle the objection you got yesterday.

**V1 — Guide:** So certification gets your team competent. Wednesdays keep them sharp. Do both.

### Three — tell everybody

**ON-SCREEN:** Large animated text: *100%.* Then a client list with every entry highlighted green. Then the Digital House graphic, then a billing statement with it inserted.

**V1 — Guide:** Now the part that decides whether any of this works.

**V1 — Guide:** **Tell every single client. All of them. One hundred percent.**

**V1 — Guide:** Not the big ones. Not the ones you think are interested. Not the ones who happen to ask. Every client you have, told deliberately, by you.

**V2 — Rep:** *"Even the ones who only buy one thing from us?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Especially those. The client you assume is not interested is the one quietly paying somebody else for digital work. You just never asked.

**V1 — Guide:** And the message is simple. You can now handle their digital work too. One partner instead of three. One conversation instead of chasing vendors. And everything they already buy from you stays exactly as it is.

**V1 — Guide:** Then the second half of the rule. **Digital goes in every pitch.** Every one. Not when it seems relevant, not when there is budget left over.

**V1 — Guide:** And the easiest way to do that is the **Digital House graphic.** It shows a client, on one page, everything you can do for them — and it explains that you are not selling one thing. You are selling a whole house.

**V1 — Guide:** There are dozens of tactics in there, organised into three areas. **Online visibility.** **Content marketing.** And **targeted digital display.** A client does not need to understand all of it. They need to see how much of it there is.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is a tip from groups who have done this well. If you still mail statements, **put the Digital House in the billing statement.** Every client, every month, holding a page that shows everything you now do. That is the cheapest and most persistent announcement you will ever make.

**V1 — Guide:** Then make it visible everywhere else. Your website. An announcement through your own channels. Business cards and signage. Social pages. And a Google Business Profile of your own, properly set up.

## SEGMENT 5 — WHERE THIS GOES WRONG

**ON-SCREEN:** Each mistake appears as text with a real-world visual — a half-finished website, a client list with only a few entries marked, a proposal with digital at the back.

**V1 — Guide:** Five things go wrong here. Every one of them is avoidable.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. Announcing before anybody can answer a question.** A client gets curious, asks something reasonable, and gets a blank look. Now the launch feels like a press release. Train first, announce second.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Telling some clients and assuming word travels.** It does not. You will find out eighteen months later that a client you see monthly never knew.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Trying to do the certification alone when you do not have to.** Groups take the login, mean to get to it, and never do. Your account manager will sit with your team weekly. Let them.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four. Leaving digital at the back of the proposal.** If it is the last page, it is the first thing cut. Put it in the middle where the conversation actually happens.

**V1 — Guide:** **Five. Not looking at your own house.** The website is built for you. The blog is written for you. All you have to do is check your listing and your reviews, which takes about ten minutes.

**V1 — Guide:** And the handoff. Once your house is in order, your team is training, and your clients know — everything after this happens inside one place. Your resource site. That is where every form, tool, presentation, and report lives, and it is the next block.

## TRAINER NOTES *(not narrated)*

**Where this sits.** Block 2 of 11. **Depends on block one** — the client announcement assumes the partnership model is already decided. Feeds into the Resource Section.

**What's included versus what's an add-on — get this right in conversation:**
| Included, no extra charge | Paid add-ons |
|---|---|
| The agency website, built by us | Social media management |
| A blog written every other week and posted to their site | Google Business Profile management |
| | Targeted landing pages for their own agency |
| | Automated website-visitor identification marketing |

**The blog program is under-appreciated and worth selling internally.** It keeps a partner's own site from looking abandoned, and it gives them a reason to email their client database every other week. A partner doing content marketing and email marketing on their own agency is a partner who can sell both credibly.

**Certification is the strongest predictor of partner success.** Push the weekly account manager meetings hard. Groups that take the self-paced route without support are the ones that stall.

**The mistakes, with the symptom somebody would notice:**
| Mistake | What it looks like when it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Announcing before training | A client asks a basic question and gets a blank look |
| Telling only some clients | Eighteen months later, a long-standing client says they never knew |
| Going it alone on certification | Login taken, never used, and nobody can answer a question |
| Digital at the back of the proposal | It gets cut first, every time, and stays an add-on forever |
| Own listing and reviews ignored | The rep knows it while presenting, and it shows |

**Suggested work product for this block.** A completed launch checklist: **the date the client announcement goes out, the list of who needs certifying, and a ten-minute self-check of their own listing and reviews.** Plus confirmation the Digital House graphic is in the standard presentation.
