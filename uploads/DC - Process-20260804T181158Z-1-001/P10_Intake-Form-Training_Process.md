# Intake Form Training — Process Script
**Pillar:** Process · Block 10 of 11
**Audience:** Account executives and agency staff — internal
**Format:** Motion graphics over real screen capture · AI voice read (two voices) · animated text · green callout boxes
**Runtime:** 1182 counted spoken words ≈ **7:53** at 150 wpm
**Spine — the one sentence somebody must repeat cold:** *Fill in what you know. We will get the rest.*

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES — READ BEFORE RECORDING

**Two voices.** **V1 — Guide** carries all teaching. **V2 — Rep** says what the viewer is actually thinking. Distinctly different ElevenLabs voice.

**Written for an AI read.** Short sentences. No semicolons, no parentheticals.

**⚠️ Read this before writing or editing a word of it. The tone mission for this block is different from every other one.**

Intake forms generate a recurring complaint: *"I just sold something and now there is more paperwork."* That complaint is the reason this block exists, and **the job of this script is to take that feeling away** — not to add instruction on top of it.

So every beat should reduce effort rather than describe it. **The instruction is permission**, not requirement: fill in what you know, leave what you do not, ask for help freely. A rep who finishes this block feeling like they have less to do has understood it correctly.

**This block is deliberately shorter than the others.** A long video about forms proves the complaint right.

**No illustrations, no icon cards, no mascots.**

**ON-SCREEN:** Real screen capture of the insertion order at the services step, with a **green box** on an intake form panel opening inside it.

**V2 — Rep:** *"I just sold it. Why is there more paperwork?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Fair question, and it is the most common thing we hear at this stage. So let us deal with it first, honestly.

**V1 — Guide:** **This is not paperwork and it is not a test.** Nobody is checking whether you asked the right questions. These are a handful of basic facts our team needs in order to start building the thing you just sold.

**V1 — Guide:** And they are not a separate job any more. **The intake forms live inside the insertion order**, attached to whatever services you selected. You are already in there. There is nothing extra to go and find.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is the line for the whole block, and it is genuine permission: **fill in what you know. We will get the rest.**

## SEGMENT 2 — WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

**ON-SCREEN:** Two paths build as text — *campaign starts building* versus *campaign sits waiting* — with a **green box** on the first.

**V1 — Guide:** So why do we need anything at all?

**V1 — Guide:** Because a campaign is not a service you switch on. Somebody has to build it, and to build it they need a few things that only exist in the world you just came from.

**V1 — Guide:** Where should the traffic go. Who are we trying to reach. What creative are we using, and do we have it. That is roughly the shape of it — not an interrogation, just the difference between a campaign that starts on Monday and one that sits waiting on an email.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So this is what stops things stalling."*

**V1 — Guide:** Exactly that. And here is the honest version of the consequence. Nothing dramatic happens if these are thin. Your campaign just quietly does not start, because somebody is waiting on an answer nobody has, and you find out two weeks later when the client asks how it is going.

**V1 — Guide:** Ten minutes here saves that conversation.

## SEGMENT 3 — THE WALKTHROUGH

**ON-SCREEN:** Real screen capture of the intake panel inside the insertion order, following each option as it is named.

**V1 — Guide:** Three things to know. Your three options, what happens after, and the products that always come with a call.

### Your three options

**ON-SCREEN:** Three options build as text with a **green box** on each in turn, then on the *Need AM Help* button.

**V1 — Guide:** You have three ways to handle any intake form, and **all three are fine.**

**V1 — Guide:** **One. Fill it out completely.** You just had the needs analysis and you probably know most of this already. If you can, do it — it is the fastest route to launch.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Fill out what you know and leave the rest.** Genuinely acceptable. A blank field is easy to follow up on. A wrong answer looks exactly like a right one and gets built into the campaign.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Ask your account manager to help.** There is a **need help** button right there in the form. Press it and somebody picks it up with you.

**V2 — Rep:** *"And that is not going to look bad?"*

**V1 — Guide:** No. It is the button working as intended. Nobody is keeping score on which reps used it, and the ones who ask early tend to have campaigns that launch on time.

### What happens after

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the kick-off call scheduling step, then on the account manager's calendar.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is the part that makes the options work, and it happens on its own.

**V1 — Guide:** If you fill everything in, you are done. If you leave anything blank **or** press the help button, **the system will prompt you to book a kick-off call with your account manager.**

**V1 — Guide:** So the gap does not become your homework. It becomes a fifteen-minute conversation with somebody who does this all day, and between you the form gets finished properly.

**V1 — Guide:** Book that call when it prompts you. Do not skip past it meaning to sort it out later — that is the one thing that genuinely does slow a launch down.

### Three products that always come with a call

**ON-SCREEN:** Three product names animate in as text with a **green box** on each.

**V1 — Guide:** And three products always include a kick-off call, no matter how completely you filled the form in.

**V1 — Guide:** **Website development. Social media management. And email marketing.**

**V1 — Guide:** Those three have too many decisions in them to capture in a form — how the site should be structured, what the brand sounds like, who is on the list and what we are allowed to send them. Those are conversations, so we treat them as conversations.

**V1 — Guide:** For website development in particular, **your account manager handles that intake with the client directly.** That one is not yours to fill in at all.

### What they actually ask

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box moving across three groups of fields — the basics, the access items, the campaign details.

**V1 — Guide:** The forms vary by product, but they are all asking about the same three things.

**V1 — Guide:** **The basics.** Business name, website, and where you want the traffic to land. That last one matters more than it looks — if an ad sends somebody to a homepage instead of the page about the thing they clicked on, the campaign works harder for less.

**V1 — Guide:** **A couple of access items.** Whether we can put tracking on their site, so we can tell them what the campaign actually produced. And what creative we are using — whether they are providing it, or we are making it. If they have brand standards, fonts, colours, a proper logo file, that is the moment to mention it.

**V1 — Guide:** **And the campaign itself.** What they want out of it, who we are trying to reach, and where.

**V1 — Guide:** That is it. Nothing in there requires research. It is all things you either heard in the room or can ask in one text message.

## SEGMENT 5 — WHERE THIS GOES WRONG

**ON-SCREEN:** Each mistake appears as text beside the real screen element it relates to.

**V1 — Guide:** Four things, and none of them are about working harder.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. Guessing instead of leaving it blank.** This is the only one that really costs anything. A guess reads as a fact to whoever builds the campaign, and it gets built that way. Blank is safe. Blank triggers a conversation.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Not booking the kick-off call when prompted.** Meaning to do it later is how a campaign sits for a fortnight. It takes thirty seconds while the prompt is in front of you.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Sending the client a list of questions.** Do not turn this into homework for them. If you need answers, pick up the phone, or bring your account manager in and let them ask.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four. Not asking for help because it feels like you should know.** You will not know everything, especially early on. That is expected, and it is exactly what the button and the account manager are there for.

**V1 — Guide:** And the handoff. Once the intake information is in — however it got there — our team builds the campaign, and your account manager picks up anything outstanding. From there it goes live, and the next thing you will see is a report.

## TRAINER NOTES *(not narrated)*

**Where this sits.** Block 10 of 11. The intake forms live inside the insertion order, so this block goes deeper on content that block 9 introduces. Feeds fulfillment and then reporting.

**⚠️ The tone is the deliverable in this block.** The recurring complaint is *"I sold something and now there is more paperwork."* **Every beat of this script is built to reduce that feeling.** If a trainer reinforces it as a compliance requirement, the block will do more harm than not running it. Frame it as: three options, all acceptable, and any gap becomes a fifteen-minute call rather than the rep's homework.

**The three options, and all three are legitimate:**
1. Complete it
2. Complete what they know, leave the rest
3. Press the **need help** button

**Partial completion or a help request automatically prompts a kick-off call** with the account manager. That is the mechanism that makes "fill in what you know" safe rather than sloppy — the gap becomes a conversation.

**Three products always include a kick-off call regardless:**
- **Website development** — and the account manager handles that intake with the client directly
- **Social media management**
- **Email marketing**

Those three carry decisions a form cannot hold: site structure, brand voice, list permissions.

**The mistakes, with the symptom somebody would notice:**
| Mistake | What it looks like when it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Guessing rather than leaving blank | Campaign built to the wrong specification; discovered after launch |
| Not booking the prompted kick-off call | Campaign sits for a fortnight; client asks how it is going |
| Sending the client a list of questions | Client feels they are doing our work; relationship cost |
| Not asking for help | Slow launches and avoidable errors, usually from newer reps |

**What the forms ask, in three groups** — useful if a trainer is asked for detail:
- **Basics:** business name, website, and the landing destination for traffic
- **Access and creative:** permission to place tracking on the site so results can be reported, and whether the client is supplying creative or we are producing it. Brand standards, fonts, colours, and a proper logo file if they exist
- **Campaign:** the goal, the audience, the geography

**The landing destination is worth emphasising in the weekly meeting.** It is the single field most often left as a homepage, and it quietly costs the campaign performance for the whole flight.

**Suggested work product for this block.** None beyond the real thing — the next insertion order a rep submits. **The check is behavioural: did they leave a field blank rather than guess, and did they book the prompted call?** Both are visible to the account manager.

**Writer's notes:**
- Content draws on the original Intake Forms script, but its job has changed. The original taught **how to find and submit separate forms**. They now live inside the online insertion order, so this block teaches **what they ask, the three ways to handle them, and how a gap becomes a call.**
- **The original rule "fill them out yourself, do not email the form to the client" is preserved in spirit** but reframed. The rep no longer has a form they could send. The mistake is now sending the client a list of questions, which creates the same problem.
- **Deliberately kept shorter than other blocks.** A long video about forms proves the complaint right.
- **⚠️ One reconciliation to confirm.** The original script listed **SEO** among the products requiring a kick-off call. Current direction names three — website development, social media management, and email marketing. **This script uses the three. If SEO still requires a call, it needs adding.**
- **Cross-module dependency:** block 9 introduces the intake panel, the help button, and the locked kick-off call fields. If the insertion order changes, check both.
