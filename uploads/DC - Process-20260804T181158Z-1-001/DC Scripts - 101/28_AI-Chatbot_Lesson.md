# AI Chatbot — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Online Visibility (a website capability — see the note in Section B)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1269 counted spoken words ≈ **8:28** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics, no vendor named.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *Most people who leave your website had exactly one question.*

## SECTION B — AI Chatbot: Full Script

> **Note for the writer.** This is a website capability rather than a media tactic — there's no targeting, no impressions, and no cost-per-thousand. It's also included with our managed website product, so for many clients this is a feature to *activate and set up well* rather than a separate sale. The seven segments and quiz blueprint are unchanged.
>
> **No vendor is named anywhere in this script**, per direction. The technology is described generically.

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real screenshot of a business website at night with a small chat bubble in the corner — **green box** on the bubble. Then a real chat exchange: a visitor asking a question, an answer coming back, then a name and phone number being given. Green box on the contact details.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Do they take kids as young as five? …It's nine-thirty at night, I'll just look somewhere else."*
**V1 — Guide:** There it is. A parent with a credit card, ready to sign her kid up, stopped cold by one question nobody was awake to answer. And she didn't call in the morning — she looked somewhere else, found a place that answered, and that business never knew she existed.
**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: an **AI chatbot** is a small chat window on a business's website that answers visitors' questions using artificial intelligence — trained on that specific business — and collects the visitor's contact information so somebody can follow up.
**V1 — Guide:** Here's the analogy to use in a meeting: it's the employee who works the shift nobody wants. There at nine-thirty at night, at six in the morning, on Sunday. Never calls in sick, never gets tired of the same question.
**V1 — Guide:** And the line to carry, because it's the whole argument: **most people who leave your website had exactly one question.**

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Three statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a clock face showing 9:30pm beside a dark storefront and a lit-up phone. Then a warning line in text: *an unattended chat window is worse than none at all.*

**V1 — Guide:** So why does a business need this? Three reasons.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: the customers show up when nobody's there.** Well over forty percent of high-intent inquiries arrive evenings and weekends — when somebody finally has ten quiet minutes. And most local businesses answer none of them. Friday evening to Monday morning is a sixty-hour window where their most motivated prospects hit voicemail.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: people will not wait.** Around eight in ten say they expect an immediate answer when they open a chat window, and roughly six in ten abandon it if nothing happens within about a minute. A minute — that's the whole window. And what happens next isn't "I'll call tomorrow." It's a competitor.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three — and this one's a warning: an unattended chat window is worse than none at all.** Put a chat bubble up that nobody answers and you've built a machine for disappointing people. Which is the honest case *for* an AI chatbot: a small business can't staff chat at nine-thirty at night, but it can have something reliable there.
**V1 — Guide:** One more honest note: this doesn't replace a human. It answers the straightforward questions, captures the information, and then somebody real follows up. It's there to get the customer to a human faster, not to keep them away from one.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a martial arts or dance studio, a service business van, a clinic or salon front desk. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses that get the same handful of questions constantly.** Studios, gyms, salons, clinics, daycares, trades. What ages, what hours, what's it cost, do you service my area. Predictable, answerable, and answering them at midnight costs nothing.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses where nobody's at a desk.** Contractors, mobile services, one-person operations, anybody whose "office" is a truck. They miss inquiries all day, not just at night, and they know it.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses whose customers research privately.** Medical, dental, legal, financial. People look into those alone, at night, and would often rather type a question than call and talk to somebody.
**V1 — Guide:** Honest flip side: a business that answers its phone all day with very little website traffic won't get much from this. And one unwilling to keep the information current ends up with a bot confidently giving the wrong hours — worse than silence.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) A setup screen filling with business information — services, hours, policies, common questions — **green box** on it → *it knows what you tell it*. (2) A chat exchange where an answer is given and then a name and number collected → *answer, then capture*. (3) The chat handing off with a note going to the owner's phone → *know when to hand off*. Closes on text: *Trained on the business · Answers and captures · Hands off.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: it's trained on the business.** Be clear about this, because people misunderstand it. The chatbot doesn't sit there knowing things about the category in general — it knows what it's been *told* about that specific business. Services, hours, service area, policies, the questions that come up constantly.
**V1 — Guide:** So the setup is the product. Good information in, good answers out. Vague information in and it answers vaguely, which is nobody's idea of a first impression. And somebody has to keep it current, or it'll keep confidently giving the old hours.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: it answers, and then it captures.** The first half is obvious — somebody asks whether classes are open to five-year-olds and gets an answer.
**V1 — Guide:** The second half is where the money is. During that conversation it gets a name and a way to reach them. A visitor who'd have closed the tab becomes a person on a list, question already answered, interest already established — a warm lead created at nine-thirty at night with nobody in the building.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: it knows when to hand off.** No chatbot should be the last word on everything, and this is where reps get clients into trouble if they oversell it.
**V1 — Guide:** When a question goes past what it's been taught — something unusual, something sensitive, somebody upset — it hands off to a human and passes along what's been said. And limits get set on what it can commit to. A business can be held to what its own website says, so quoting prices or promising outcomes is an owner's deliberate decision, not something a bot improvises.
**V1 — Guide:** Set that up properly and it's an asset. Set it up carelessly and it's a liability with a friendly greeting.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on the chat bubble, the setup screen, and a captured contact record.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.
**V1 — Guide:** **AI chatbot** — a chat window on a website that answers questions automatically, using artificial intelligence trained on that business's own information.
**V1 — Guide:** **Training** — the setup step where the business's information is loaded in: services, hours, policies, common questions. What it's taught is what it knows, and nothing more.
**V1 — Guide:** **Lead capture** — collecting a visitor's name and contact details during the conversation, so somebody can follow up. This is the part that turns a chat into business.
**V1 — Guide:** **Escalation** — handing a conversation off to a human when it goes past what the bot should handle. Every good setup has one, and a client should always ask where those conversations go.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *Answers when nobody's there · Turns questions into leads · Never gets tired of the same question · Hands off to a person.* Payoff: a real martial arts studio photo beside a nighttime chat exchange ending in a phone number. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? A chat window on the client's website. Setup and training on their own information — services, hours, service area, policies, common questions. Automatic answers around the clock. Contact capture inside the conversation. Handoff to a human when needed. And it's included with our managed website product — worth saying out loud, because a lot of clients have this available and switched off.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? Something that **answers when nobody's there.** Something that **turns questions into leads** instead of letting them evaporate. Something that **never gets tired** of the twentieth person asking about hours. And a **handoff to a real person** when it matters.
**V1 — Guide:** So picture a martial arts school. Great instructors, wonderful with kids — and here's how their inquiries actually work: parents research at nine or ten at night, after their own kids are in bed. That's the only time they have.
**V1 — Guide:** Before: those parents landed on a website, wondered whether five-year-olds were welcome and whether there was a trial class, found no answer, and closed the tab.
**V1 — Guide:** After: the chat window answered both questions in about four seconds, took a name and a mobile number, and the owner called back at nine the next morning — to a parent who already had her questions answered.
**V2 — Scene:** *"I'd honestly already decided by the time you called. I just needed to know you took his age."*
**V1 — Guide:** — and that's the whole thing. Not a sales pitch. One answer, at the moment somebody needed it. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: trained on the business · 2: answers and captures · 3: hands off. *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: it's trained on the business, so good information in means good answers out — and somebody has to keep it current. Two: it answers the question and captures the contact, turning a visitor into a lead. Three: it hands off to a human when a conversation needs one, with limits on what it's allowed to promise.
**V1 — Guide:** And two things to carry in. **Most people who leave your website had exactly one question.** And the warning that's also the argument: **an unattended chat window is worse than none at all** — which is exactly why this is worth setting up properly.
**V1 — Guide:** That's the AI chatbot. Quick ten-question check. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION D — AE Field Notes *(not narrated)*

**This is a website capability, not a media buy.** No impressions, no targeting, no cost-per-thousand. And it's **included with our managed website product** — which means for a lot of existing clients this isn't a sale at all, it's a switch that hasn't been flipped.

**The highest-value activity here: audit your existing website clients.** Any of them without the chatbot active is leaving after-hours inquiries on the floor with a tool they're already paying for. That's a goodwill call, not a pitch, and it's the kind of thing that gets an AE trusted.

**How it's sold.** Ask one question: *"What are the three questions you get asked most?"* Owners answer instantly and without hesitation. Then: *"Who answers those at nine-thirty at night?"* The silence is the sale.

**Setup is the product — treat it that way.** A chatbot with thin information is worse than none, so the setup conversation matters more than the sale does. Get from the client: services, hours, service area, pricing policy (what can and can't be quoted), what happens with an unhappy visitor, and where captured leads should go. **If a client won't spend twenty minutes on that, flag it — the result will disappoint them and they'll blame the tool.**

**Bundles with:**
- **Managed website** — it's part of that product. Mention it during every website conversation.
- **Every traffic tactic** — search ads, SEO, social ads, display. All of them send people to a website, and this catches the ones who arrive with a question. Worth pointing out: paying to send traffic and then not answering it is expensive.
- **Targeted landing pages** — a chat window on a specific offer page catches the visitor at peak intent.
- **CRM targeting and email** — captured contacts feed both, which turns a nighttime chat into a list.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"I don't want a robot talking to my customers."* → Fair, and don't fight it. Frame it as an answering service rather than a salesperson: it handles the simple questions and gets the person to you faster. It isn't there to close anybody.
- *"What if it says something wrong?"* → Good question and don't dodge it. It only knows what it's taught, limits get set on what it can commit to, and unusual questions get handed to a person. Also be honest: the information has to be kept current, and that's a shared responsibility.
- *"My customers would rather call."* → Some will. This is for the ones who won't — and for the hours when calling isn't an option.
- *"Isn't this just a form?"* → No. A form makes somebody wait for an answer. This gives the answer and *then* takes the contact, which is why the person on the other end is warmer.

**Lines for the room:**
- *"Most people who leave your website had exactly one question."*
- *"What are the three questions you get asked most? Now — who answers those at nine-thirty at night?"*
- *"It's the employee who works the shift nobody wants."*
- *"An unattended chat window is worse than no chat window at all."*
- *"You're paying to send people to your website. This answers them when they get there."*

**Retention note.** The failure mode is neglect. A bot set up once and never updated slowly becomes wrong — old hours, discontinued services, a price that changed. That degradation is invisible until a customer complains. **Build a periodic information review into the relationship**, which is both good practice and a natural recurring conversation.

**Writer's notes:**
- **No vendor named**, per direction. All technology described generically.
- **Statistics sources:** the finding that over 40% of high-intent inquiries arrive evenings and weekends is from 2026 speed-to-lead benchmark reporting (Blazeo data via industry compilation). The expectation figures — roughly 8 in 10 expecting an immediate response on chat, and around 6 in 10 abandoning within about a minute — are HubSpot and Comm100 respectively, widely reproduced in 2026 live-chat compilations. The "unattended chat is worse than none" finding comes from 2026 customer-service response benchmarks. **Attribute as "research shows" and re-verify annually.** All three prove one idea: *the question doesn't wait for business hours, and neither does the customer.*
- **Deliberately excluded:** conversion-rate multiples and speed-to-lead ROI figures, which circulate widely but read as promised results and often trace to vendor marketing. The script makes the case on behavior and expectation instead.
- **⚠️ Two genuine risk items built into the script, both worth keeping.** First, **accuracy** — the bot can state something wrong if its information is stale, and a business may be held to what its own website says. The script tells reps that price quotes and outcome promises are a deliberate owner decision, not something a bot improvises. Second, **escalation** — the script insists a human takes over for anything unusual or sensitive, and tells reps to have the client ask where those conversations go.

## APPENDIX — Confirmations Needed & Prompt Used

**Five things to confirm before recording.** No sales sheet was supplied for this module, so the following are described in general terms and should be tightened:

1. **Does the chatbot disclose that it's automated?** Increasingly this is expected, and some jurisdictions have moved toward requiring disclosure in commercial contexts. The script doesn't claim either way. **Worth confirming, and worth doing regardless** — a visitor who works out mid-conversation that they've been talking to software feels tricked.
2. **Appointment booking** — can it schedule directly, or only capture contact details? The script says it captures contacts and stays deliberately silent on booking. If it can book, that's a significant addition to Mechanic 2.
3. **Where captured leads land** — email, text alert, a CRM, a dashboard? Reps are told to ask, but they should be able to answer.
4. **Channels beyond the website** — does it also handle text messages, social messages, or missed-call follow-up? The script covers the website chat window only.
5. **Who does the training and updating** — us, the client, or both? The script says "somebody has to keep it current" without assigning it, which is a gap a client will ask about immediately.
