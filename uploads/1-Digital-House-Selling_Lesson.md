# Digital House Selling — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Online Visibility (Room #00 — **this one comes first.** It is the frame every other module sits inside)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1436 counted spoken words ≈ **9:34** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no rates, no competitor names.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *Nobody builds a house from the roof down.*

## SECTION B — Digital House Selling: Full Script

> **Note for the writer.** This module is not a product. It is **the sales system**, and it is the frame every other module in this course sits inside. It should be watched first. There is no payment model, no targeting, and no rate — what it teaches is *the order things are sold in and why.*
>
> It is also the bridge between the two halves of this certification. The diagnostic tools taught in the process training — the needs analysis, the audit, the recommendation — exist to serve this model. **This module is where a trainee finds out those two things are one thing.**

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a business owner across a desk, unimpressed. Then the **Digital House graphic** assembles on screen — slab, walls, roof — with **green boxes** naming each tier as it lands.

**V2 — Scene:** *"The last outfit sold me a pile of impressions. Numbers looked great. Phone never rang."*

**V1 — Guide:** You are going to hear versions of that sentence for the rest of your career. And here is the uncomfortable part — the campaign probably delivered exactly what was sold. The impressions were real. The clicks were real.

**V1 — Guide:** The business still did not grow, because the traffic arrived somewhere that could not do anything with it.

**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: **Digital House Selling** is our sales system. Not a product — a **method** for deciding what a business needs and in what order.

**V1 — Guide:** And the model is a house with three levels. **The foundation is online visibility** — a fast, findable website and the work that makes it show up. **The walls are content marketing** — the things that keep a customer close once they have found the business. **The roof is targeted digital** — the advertising, all the tactics you are about to learn.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is the whole argument in one line: **nobody builds a house from the roof down.**

**V1 — Guide:** Every other media company calling on that business is going to walk in with a rate and a tactic. You are going to walk in with a plan and an order of operations. That is a completely different conversation, and it is not one they can copy.

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Three statistics build as large animated numbers. Then the house graphic with the roof sitting on nothing, tilting. Then text: *roof-first drives clicks. Foundation-up drives revenue.*

**V1 — Guide:** So why does the order matter this much? Three reasons, and they are all about where the money leaks out.

**V1 — Guide:** **One: if the site is slow, the traffic evaporates.** Around fifty-three percent of mobile visitors leave a page that takes more than three seconds. So a business with a slow site can buy all the traffic it wants — half of it is gone before the page opens. That is not a bad campaign. That is a good campaign pouring into a bucket with a hole in it.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two: if their reputation is thin, people stop anyway.** Most consumers will not seriously consider a business rated below four stars. So somebody clicks the ad, checks the reviews, and quietly goes elsewhere. The campaign gets the credit for the click and none of the blame for the loss.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three: most people who arrive do nothing.** Roughly ninety-five percent of visitors to a small business website leave without contacting anybody. That is normal — but it means the *quality of what they land on* decides how much of that traffic was worth buying.

**V1 — Guide:** Put those together and you get the idea to carry: **you cannot advertise your way out of a broken foundation.** You can only spend faster.

**V1 — Guide:** Which is why we build in this order. **Roof-first programmes drive clicks. Foundation-up programmes drive revenue.** And the second one is what keeps the account, because it actually works.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a business owner looking skeptical, a business with a visibly dated site on a phone, a brand-new storefront. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** This approach works with almost anybody, but three kinds of prospect it works on especially well.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses who have already been burned.** They bought digital, it did nothing, and now they are cynical. That cynicism is your best asset, because you are about to explain **why** it did nothing — and it will be the first explanation anybody has offered them.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses with an obviously weak foundation.** A slow site, a listing with the wrong hours, four reviews. You do not have to convince them anything is wrong. You just have to show them, in order.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses that have never bought digital at all.** These are the best long-term accounts you will ever build, because you get to start them in the right place instead of undoing somebody else's roof.

**V1 — Guide:** And the honest flip side: occasionally you will meet a business whose foundation is genuinely solid — fast site, strong reviews, listings clean, showing up in search. **Then go straight to the roof.** The model is not a rule that every client must buy three tiers. It is a sequence for deciding where to start.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** The Digital House graphic, with a **green box** illuminating each tier in turn and the relevant tactics naming themselves beside it. Then the diagnostic tools appear beneath the house.

**V1 — Guide:** Three levels, built bottom to top. And one thing that runs through all of them, which we will come to.

**V1 — Guide:** **Tier one — the foundation. Online visibility.**

**V1 — Guide:** This is whether the business can be found and whether the place people land actually works. A website that loads fast on a phone and gives somebody an obvious next step. The search and answer-engine work that gets them found. Their Google Business Profile, properly set up. Landing pages for the things they actually sell.

**V1 — Guide:** Nothing above this floor matters until this is right. **Everything you sell in the rest of this course sends people here.**

**V1 — Guide:** **Tier two — the walls. Content marketing.**

**V1 — Guide:** Now that people can find them, does the business look like somewhere you would spend money? This is reputation and reviews. Listings that say the same thing everywhere, so they look real and credible. Social media that looks alive rather than abandoned. Email that keeps them in touch with people who already bought.

**V1 — Guide:** Walls are what keep a customer once you have found them. A business can be findable and still lose people here.

**V1 — Guide:** **Tier three — the roof. Targeted digital.**

**V1 — Guide:** Now, and only now, the advertising. Search ads, display, geofencing, streaming television, video, audio — every tactic in the second half of this course.

**V1 — Guide:** And here is why it works better up here. **The roof finally pays for itself, because there is something underneath to send people to.** Same tactic, same money, completely different result.

**V2 — Scene:** *"So you're telling me to spend less with you first?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Sometimes, yes. And that sentence is the most trustworthy thing you will say all year.

**V1 — Guide:** **And running through all three: how you find out where they are.** We do not guess. There is a needs analysis to understand the business, an audit that examines how they actually show up, and a recommendation built from both. Those tools exist to find the **deficiencies** — and they work bottom-up, same as the house.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with **green boxes** on the relevant tier of the house graphic.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.

**V1 — Guide:** **The Digital House** — our model for how a business's online presence gets built. Foundation, walls, roof, in that order.

**V1 — Guide:** **Foundation-up** — building in sequence, starting with visibility. The opposite is roof-down, which is what most digital programmes do and why most of them disappoint.

**V1 — Guide:** **Deficiency** — a specific, provable gap in a business's online presence. Not an opinion. A slow load time, a wrong phone number, four reviews. Every deficiency has a solution attached to it, and finding them is what the needs analysis and audit are for.

**V1 — Guide:** **The three areas** — **online visibility**, **content marketing**, and **targeted digital**. Learn those three names in that order. Dozens of tactics live inside them, and every one you will study belongs to one of the three.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** The Digital House graphic beside a real presentation deck and a real audit on a laptop. Four benefit lines animate in. Payoff: a real landscaping truck photo beside a fast site on a phone. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** So what do you actually get with this? A **model** you can draw on a napkin. The **Digital House graphic** for your presentations. A **diagnostic process** — needs analysis, audit, recommendation — that finds the deficiencies rather than guessing at them. And **dozens of tactics** organised so you always know which one comes next.

**V1 — Guide:** What does the client get? A **reason** for the order things happen in. **Money spent in the sequence that makes it work.** A partner who will occasionally tell them to wait, which almost nobody does. And advertising that performs, **because it is landing somewhere built to receive it.**

**V1 — Guide:** So picture a landscaping company. Twelve trucks, good crews, and last spring somebody sold them a big display campaign for the season.

**V1 — Guide:** It ran beautifully. Enormous number of impressions. Their website took nine seconds to open on a phone, their listing had a disconnected number on it, and they had six reviews. The campaign ended and the owner concluded digital does not work for landscaping.

**V1 — Guide:** After: nobody sold them advertising for four months. We fixed the site, cleaned up the listings, and got a review programme running. **Then** we ran the display campaign again — same tactic, similar money.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Same ads. Completely different spring."*

**V1 — Guide:** Same ads. The difference was the house underneath them. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge
**ON-SCREEN:** The Digital House graphic assembling bottom to top, then the three area names. *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three levels. **Foundation — online visibility**, so they can be found and the landing place works. **Walls — content marketing**, so they look worth choosing and stay in touch. **Roof — targeted digital**, which now pays for itself because there is something underneath it.

**V1 — Guide:** And the line to carry into every meeting: **nobody builds a house from the roof down.**

**V1 — Guide:** Here is what you can say to almost any prospect, and it fits on one breath. *"You need three things. To be found. To look worth choosing. And then to advertise. Do them in that order and everything you spend works harder."*

**V1 — Guide:** Everything else in this course is a room in that house. From here on, every time you learn a tactic, ask yourself one question — **which floor is this on?**

**V1 — Guide:** Quick ten-question check. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION D — AE Field Notes *(not narrated)*

**This module is not a product and has no payment model.** It is the sales system, and **it should be the first thing a trainee watches.** Everything else in the certification is a room inside it.

**⚠️ It is also the bridge between the two halves of this course.** The process training teaches the needs analysis, the audit, and the recommendation. This module is where a rep learns *why those exist* — they are the instruments that find deficiencies, bottom-up. **If a rep finishes the process pillar without understanding the Digital House, they will run the tools as paperwork.**

**The competitive argument — say this out loud in training.** Every other media company in the market is calling on the same businesses with a **rate on a tactic.** They lead with a number for impressions. That is a price conversation, and price conversations are won by whoever is cheapest.

**This is not a price conversation.** It is a diagnosis, a sequence, and a reason. It cannot be matched by somebody quoting a rate, and it is why we do not compete at that level. **Do not name competitors in the field** — the argument works better as "everybody else walks in with a number."

**The three tiers, and what lives in each:**
| Tier | Area | Rooms |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Foundation** | Online visibility | The website platform · accessibility · SEO and AEO · Google Business Profile · targeted landing pages |
| **2 — Walls** | Content marketing | Reputation management · local listing optimisation · social media marketing · email marketing |
| **3 — Roof** | Targeted digital | Search ads · display · dynamic display · geofencing · event · CRM · addressable · social ads · social display · streaming TV · YouTube · Prime Video · Hulu · programmatic video and audio · native · mobile audience |

**The single most useful habit to build:** every time a rep learns a new tactic, they should ask **"which floor is this on?"** Reps who do that stop pitching roof tactics to foundation problems.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"I just want the advertising."* → "You can have it. I'd be doing you a disservice if I didn't show you what it's going to land on first. Give me ten minutes and then decide."
- *"The last company sold me digital and it did nothing."* → "I believe you, and I can probably tell you why. Can I show you your own website on my phone?"
- *"This sounds like you're trying to sell me more."* → Honest answer: it is usually the opposite. "The first thing I'd recommend is smaller than what you were about to buy. It just has to come first."
- *"Everybody else quotes me a price. What's yours?"* → "I'll get to a number. But if I quote you before I know what's broken, I'm guessing — and you've already paid for somebody's guess once."

**Lines for the room:**
- *"Nobody builds a house from the roof down."*
- *"You need three things. To be found. To look worth choosing. And then to advertise."*
- *"You can't advertise your way out of a broken foundation. You can only spend faster."*
- *"The last campaign probably worked exactly as sold. The problem was where it landed."*
- *"Same ads. The difference is the house underneath them."*

**Retention note.** This model is itself a retention mechanism, because it builds a sequence of sales rather than one. A client who buys the foundation has a reason to buy the walls, and a client with walls is ready for the roof. **Each tier makes the next one easier and the last one more effective.** A rep who sells roof-first has one sale. A rep who sells foundation-up has a three-year account.

- **Deliberately excluded:** all rates, including the competitor cost-per-thousand figure that prompted this module. The argument is that competitors **lead with a number** — the specific number is not needed and quoting it invites a price discussion.
