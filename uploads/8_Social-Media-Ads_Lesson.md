# Social Media Ads — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Targeted Digital Marketing (Room #15 in the Digital House — the paid companion to Social Media Marketing, Room #9)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1399 counted spoken words ≈ **9:20** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *Boosting talks to the people who already follow you. Ads go find the ones who don't.*

## SECTION B — Social Media Ads: Full Script

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real phone screenshot of a feed with a **Sponsored** ad from a local business the viewer clearly doesn't follow. **Green box** on the Sponsored label. Then a real boosted post beside a real built ad campaign, boxed and labeled. Then four real ad formats in sequence — single image, carousel, video, vertical story.

**V2 — Scene:** *"I don't even follow this place. How did they find me?"*
**V1 — Guide:** Good question, and the answer is the whole tactic. Somebody built an audience she fit into — her age, where she lives, what she's interested in — and paid to put an ad in front of exactly that group.
**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: **social media ads** are built advertising campaigns that run inside social platforms, aimed at a chosen audience whether or not those people have ever heard of the business.
**V1 — Guide:** In the management lesson we covered **boosting** — money behind a post that's already doing well. Useful, and not this. Boosting amplifies to people who mostly already know the business. This builds a campaign from scratch, aimed at strangers who fit a description.
**V1 — Guide:** Here's the line to keep them straight: **boosting talks to the people who already follow you; ads go find the ones who don't.**
**V1 — Guide:** Which is why these two belong together. **Social management and social ads are a one-two punch.** Management is the jab: steady posting, a real voice, a page that looks alive. Ads are the right hand that reaches people who've never heard the name. Point ads at a dead page and you've sent strangers to a ghost town. Post and never advertise, and you're only talking to people who already know you.
**V1 — Guide:** Like the rest of our display family, these are bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis.**

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of somebody scrolling on a couch. Then two statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a real feed showing a local business ad next to organic posts, **green box** on the ad. Then text: *they're not just socializing there.*

**V1 — Guide:** So why should an advertiser do this? Three reasons.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: people aren't just socializing there — they're shopping there.** Roughly eight in ten social users research or discover products on these platforms, and well over half of American shoppers have bought something after seeing it on social. That's not where people go for family photos. It's where they decide.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: it's the most precise audience a small business can buy.** A radio or TV schedule reaches everybody tuned in. A billboard reaches everybody who drives past. A market-wide display buy reaches the county. Here: women in their thirties and forties, within fifteen miles, interested in fitness — and that's the whole audience they pay for. *You stop paying to reach people who were never going to buy.*
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: neither posting nor boosting will grow their business — and this is the part reps get wrong.** Posting reaches a slice of the people who already follow them. Boosting reaches more of those same people, and maybe a few friends. Both of those are conversations with an audience they already have.
**V1 — Guide:** So if a client wants *new* customers, somebody who's never heard of them has to see them. Ads are the only door — not a preference, not an upsell.
**V1 — Guide:** And here's the part worth saying out loud, because it changes how the whole program gets sold: ads don't just reach strangers once. **They grow the audience.** Some of those strangers follow the page. Which means next month the posting and the boosting are reaching more people — because the ads went out and got them. Ads grow the audience; management keeps it.
**V1 — Guide:** One honest note: this isn't a magic lead machine. It's excellent at putting the right offer in front of the right stranger. What happens next depends on the offer and where it lands.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a boutique interior or clothing rack, a gym or med spa treatment room, a restaurant patio or event flyer. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses that look good in a photo.** Boutiques, salons, med spas, restaurants, gyms, remodelers. The feed is visual, so work that photographs well starts with an advantage.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses with a describable customer.** If an owner can name the age, the neighborhood, and the interests of their best customer, you can build that audience almost exactly. That conversation is the sale.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses with something time-sensitive.** A sale, a new season, an event, a class starting in January. These can be up in a day and off the same afternoon.
**V1 — Guide:** Honest flip side: if a client can't describe who they're trying to reach, build the description before the campaign. And something genuinely hard to picture belongs in search, not a visual feed.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) Audience settings building as text — location, age, interests — then platform logos with one highlighted → *build the audience*. (2) Four real ad formats in sequence, each labeled → *pick the format*. (3) A real weekly-adjustment view: one ad set paused, budget shifted → *manage it weekly*. Closes on text: *Build the audience · Pick the format · Manage it weekly.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: build the audience.** Starts with a question, not a setting: who's the best customer? Age, how far they'll travel, what they're interested in. That description becomes the audience.
**V1 — Guide:** Then pick the room — you know these from the management lesson. Facebook for the widest and oldest spread, Instagram for visual and younger, TikTok for short video and youngest, Snapchat younger still, LinkedIn when the customer is another business. And know the platforms aren't priced the same: the professional one costs meaningfully more per thousand than the mainstream feeds. Fit first, but fit has a cost attached.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: pick the format.** Four to know, and they each do a different job.
**V1 — Guide:** A **single image ad** is one photo and a message. A **carousel ad** is several images to swipe through — right when there's a range to show. A **video ad** is strongest for explaining or demonstrating, and people consistently prefer video over a still when understanding a product. A **story ad** runs full-screen between stories — native and immediate.
**V1 — Guide:** And a detail that matters for your clients: a radio spot's audio can become a video ad — and an old TV commercial is already most of the way there. A client who thinks he has no video usually already paid for the hardest part.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: manage it weekly.** Campaigns get organized into **ad sets** — groups of ads pointed at one audience — so you can see which audience is actually responding instead of guessing at the campaign as a whole.
**V1 — Guide:** Then every week: pause what isn't working, move budget toward what is, refresh creative that's gone stale. Real-time adjustment is a genuine advantage over anything printed. And it's what separates a managed campaign from a client who spent a few hundred dollars once and decided social ads don't work.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on a Sponsored label, an ad set view, and a call-to-action button.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.
**V1 — Guide:** **Boosted post versus ad campaign** — boosting puts money behind a post that already exists, mostly reaching existing followers. An ad campaign is built from scratch with a chosen audience and format, and it reaches strangers. Different tools, different jobs.
**V1 — Guide:** **Ad set** — a group of ads aimed at one specific audience. Splitting a campaign into ad sets is what lets you tell which audience is actually working.
**V1 — Guide:** **Creative** — the ad itself. The image or video, plus the words. When somebody says "the creative isn't working," they mean the ad, not the targeting.
**V1 — Guide:** **Call to action** — the instruction and the button. Shop Now, Book Now, Learn More, Get Directions. An ad without one is a nice picture.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *Reach strangers who fit · Grow the audience itself · Precise audiences, less waste · Adjusted every week.* Payoff: a real boutique carousel ad beside a real audience-settings view — plus a real photo of a boutique interior. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? Campaigns built for Facebook and Instagram, plus TikTok, Snapchat, or LinkedIn where those fit. Audience building around the client's best customer. Ad set strategy so audiences can be compared rather than blended. A daily schedule. Full campaign management with weekly adjustments. Optional ad design. Radio-spot-to-video production. And reporting.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? They **reach strangers who fit the description** instead of only the people who already follow them. They **grow the audience itself**, so every future post reaches more people. They get **precise audiences with less waste** and formats built for the feed. And it's **adjusted every week** instead of set and forgotten.
**V1 — Guide:** So picture a women's clothing boutique on a small-town main street. Great buyer, beautiful inventory, an owner who posts faithfully — to about four hundred followers, most of whom already shop there.
**V1 — Guide:** After: a carousel ad showing six pieces from the new arrivals, aimed at women within fifteen miles interested in fashion and local shopping. People who'd never heard of the shop started swiping through it on a Tuesday night.
**V2 — Scene:** *"I saw those boots in one of those swipe-y ads and drove over."*
**V1 — Guide:** — and notice she wasn't a follower. She couldn't have been reached by posting, at any volume. That's the entire case for this tactic. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: build the audience · 2: pick the format · 3: manage it weekly. *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: build the audience — start from the best customer, then pick the matching platform. Two: pick the format — single image, carousel, video, or story, each for a different job. Three: manage it weekly — ad sets so you can tell what's working.
**V1 — Guide:** And two things to carry in. **Boosting talks to the people who already follow you; ads go find the ones who don't** — so if a client wants new customers and a bigger audience, ads are the only door. And the pairing: **ads grow the audience, management keeps it.** Sell them together and both work harder.
**V1 — Guide:** That's social media ads. Quick ten-question check. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION D — AE Field Notes *(not narrated)*

**How the advertiser pays.** Bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis**. Impression arithmetic is taught once, in Targeted Display. **Platform choice affects cost** — the professional network runs considerably higher per thousand than the mainstream feeds, and the short-video platforms sit in between. Reps should know the ranking without quoting figures, because it affects what's realistic on a small budget: recommend on fit first, then be honest that fit has a price attached.

**How it's sold — always alongside management.** This is the paid half of the one-two punch. Management keeps the page alive and credible; ads reach strangers. Selling ads onto a dead page is the single most common way this product disappoints, so treat the page as a prerequisite and say so. If a client will only buy one, sell management first and ads second — never the reverse.

**Bundles with:**
- **Social media management** — the intended pairing. Reference it in every pitch.
- **Website / landing pages** — ads need somewhere useful to land, and a specific offer page beats a homepage every time.
- **Reputation management** — a stranger who sees the ad will check reviews before acting. A weak star rating quietly wastes the ad spend.
- **CRM targeting** — same client list, different delivery; the two reinforce each other.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"I tried Facebook ads and got nothing."* → Ask two questions: was it a boost or a built campaign, and did anyone touch it after launch? Nearly every failed attempt is an unmanaged boost with no audience work.
- *"I already post every day / I boost my best posts."* → "Both of those talk to people who already follow you. Ads are how you reach the ones who don't — and how the follower count actually grows. Boosting spends money on the audience you have; ads build you a bigger one."
- *"My customers aren't on social media."* → Usually not true, but check the age. If their customer really is over seventy, Facebook is still the answer, not TikTok.
- *"Just put it everywhere."* → Fit first. Being decent on the right platform beats being invisible on five, and the platforms don't cost the same.
- *"I don't have any video."* → "Do you have a radio spot, or an old TV commercial? Then you've already paid for the hard part."

**Lines for the room:**
- *"Boosting talks to the people who already follow you. Ads go find the ones who don't."*
- *"You stop paying to reach people who were never going to buy."*
- *"If you can describe your best customer, we can build that audience almost exactly."*
- *"Ads grow the audience. Management keeps it."*
- *"Boosting spends money on the audience you already have. Ads build you a bigger one."*

**Retention note.** The failure pattern is the unmanaged campaign — money spent, nothing adjusted, client concludes social ads don't work. Pre-frame the weekly management as the product, not an add-on: *"the setup isn't the work; the weekly adjustments are."* Also watch creative fatigue — the same ad running for months stops performing, and refreshing it is a natural recurring conversation.

**Writer's notes:**
- **Statistics sources:** roughly eight in ten social media users researching or discovering products on social appears consistently across 2026 social commerce compilations (Capital One Shopping; Hostinger survey; SellersCommerce), with individual studies ranging from about 74% to 82% — the script says "roughly eight in ten" to stay inside that spread. More than half of US shoppers having purchased after seeing something on social is also multi-sourced, with figures from about 58% to 76%; the script says "well over half." The preference for video over static images when understanding a product is stated qualitatively rather than numerically, because the figures found were flagged as estimates. **Attribute as "research shows" and re-verify annually.**
- **Deliberately excluded:** every platform CPM, the click-through estimates, the ad-set design fee, the radio-to-video production fee, and both monthly minimums and contract terms. The *relative* cost ranking between platforms is described directionally, with no figures, because it genuinely affects recommendations on a small budget — confirm you're comfortable with that before recording.
