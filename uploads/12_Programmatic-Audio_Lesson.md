# Programmatic Audio Ads — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Targeted Digital Marketing (Room #19 in the Digital House)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1414 counted spoken words ≈ **9:26** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *It's the only advertising that works when nobody's looking at a screen.*

## SECTION A — The Reusable Tactic Lesson Template
*(Identical across all modules. Only the content changes — the beats never do.)*

**Voice convention:** **V1 — Guide** carries the teaching. **V2 — Scene** delivers only in-world lines and the quiz prompts.

**On-screen kit:** animated text · real photos and product screenshots · green callout boxes. **No line illustrations, icon cards, mascots, or drawn metaphors.** Spoken analogies stay spoken.

| # | Segment | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook + What It Is** | Relatable moment → one-sentence definition |
| 2 | **Why It Matters** | Why an advertiser should do this + the evidence |
| 3 | **Who It's Great For** | 3 categories, one reason each + who it's wrong for |

| 5 | **Common Terms** | Four to five terms maximum |

## SECTION B — Programmatic Audio: Full Script

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of somebody driving with a phone mounted showing a streaming audio app. Then a real photo of somebody mowing with earbuds in, and one running. Then real service logos on screen — streaming music, digital radio, podcast apps, smart speakers.

**V2 — Scene:** *"I've had headphones in since six this morning."*
**V1 — Guide:** Think about that person's day. Earbuds on the drive in. A podcast at the desk. Music while they mow the lawn. Hours and hours of attention — and at no point in any of it are they looking at a screen.
**V1 — Guide:** Every other tactic in this house needs eyes. This one doesn't.
**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: **programmatic audio** is buying ads inside online audio — streaming music, digital radio streams, podcasts, audio articles, even smart speakers — bought automatically across a lot of services at once and aimed at a chosen audience.
**V1 — Guide:** And here's some good news: **most of you already know how to sell this.** It's a spot. If you've sold radio or television, the format is familiar. If you've sold outdoor, you already price by the thousand impressions — which is exactly how this works. What's different isn't the ad. It's who hears it and how it's aimed.
**V1 — Guide:** It's bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis**, and on this tactic one impression means exactly what you'd hope: **the spot played, in somebody's ears.** Not a banner that loaded. A spot that was heard.
**V1 — Guide:** The line to carry: **it's the only advertising that works when nobody's looking at a screen.**

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Three statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a real photo of an older person with earbuds in a car, **green box** around them. Then text: *the audio audience didn't leave — it moved.*

**V1 — Guide:** So why should an advertiser do this? Three reasons.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: the audio audience is enormous and it's already there.** Edison Research — the people who have measured American listening for nearly thirty years — put online audio listening at about eight in ten Americans in the past month, and roughly three in four in the past week. That's over two hundred million people.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two — and if you've spent years selling broadcast, hear this one clearly: it's the older audience moving fastest.** Among Americans fifty-five and older, monthly online audio listening went from about half of them in 2024 to about seven in ten in 2026. Two years. That's not a young-person medium anymore, and it's the same audience many of your clients have been buying for a long time.
**V1 — Guide:** And podcasts are now close to six in ten Americans monthly. So this isn't a niche. It's where listening went.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: it reaches people in moments nothing else can.** Commuting. Working out. Mowing. Doing dishes. Running errands. Those are hours of a person's day that no display ad, no social ad, and no streaming TV ad can touch, because there's no screen involved. Audio owns that time by itself.
**V1 — Guide:** One honest note, because you'll get asked. A meaningful share of digital audio listening happens on paid, ad-free tiers — especially music. So the reachable audience is smaller than the total listening audience. That's fine, and being upfront about it makes you credible. There is still an enormous ad-supported pool, and podcasts and digital radio streams are heavily ad-supported.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a coffee drive-thru line in the morning, a home services van, a business with a local event or seasonal push. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses that catch people in transit.** Coffee, drive-thrus, gas and convenience, quick service, car washes. Somebody in a vehicle with audio playing is the single most natural fit in this entire catalog — they're listening, and they're already going somewhere.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses whose customers are working while they listen.** Trades, contractors, farmers, warehouse and shop workers, anybody with earbuds in for eight hours. If a client sells *to* those people — or wants to hire them — audio reaches them when nothing else can.
**V1 — Guide:** **Any client who already has audio.** A radio spot, or the audio track off a TV commercial — either usually works. The creative exists, they've already approved it, and it can go to work somewhere new with no production conversation.
**V1 — Guide:** And the honest flip side: audio can't show anything. A business whose whole pitch is visual — a color, a pattern, a floor plan — is fighting the medium. And it's poor for anything that needs a complicated explanation. Short, simple, memorable, or pick a different room.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) Real service logos filling a grid — music, digital radio, podcasts, smart speakers → *one buy, many services*. (2) Targeting options building as text — location, time of day, interests, listener profile — beside a broadcast station logo with a **green X** through it → *buy the listener, not the station*. (3) A radio cart or audio waveform turning into a digital spot, then a schedule and pixel → *the spot and the moment*. Closes on text: *Many services · The listener, not the station · The spot they already have.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: one buy, a lot of services.** Instead of negotiating with individual audio platforms, the ads are placed automatically across a whole range of them — streaming music services, digital radio streams, podcast networks, audio articles, smart speaker audio.
**V1 — Guide:** That's the "programmatic" part, and it's the same machinery from the display lessons: automated buying, one impression at a time, across many places at once. A small local business gets access to national audio platforms without talking to any of them.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: buy the listener, not the station.** This is the part that matters most for you, because it's the exact opposite of how audio has always been bought.
**V1 — Guide:** A broadcast buy is a station and a daypart. You get everybody listening to that station at that hour, wherever they are and whoever they are. Here, we choose by **location** — right down to the market or the radius. By **time of day** — the daypart you know from radio or TV, except it follows the person instead of the signal. By **interests and listener behavior.** And by device.
**V1 — Guide:** So a coffee shop isn't buying morning drive on one station. They're buying people inside their delivery area, on any audio service, between six and nine in the morning. Same instinct you already have — much finer aim.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: the spot, and the moment.** The creative is a straight audio spot, and an existing radio commercial — or the audio from a TV spot — usually works as-is. If they have neither, production is available.
**V1 — Guide:** Two things make that spot land harder than people expect. It generally can't be skipped past — that's the trade for a free or cheaper listening tier. And the listener's hands and eyes are busy elsewhere, so the ears are genuinely available. That's a rare thing in advertising.
**V1 — Guide:** Then the housekeeping: audience segments identified, geography set, a daily ad schedule, a conversion pixel on the site so we can see what the campaign produced, weekly refinement, and reporting.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on the service grid and the time-of-day targeting options.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.
**V1 — Guide:** **Programmatic audio** — audio ads bought automatically across many services at once, aimed at a chosen audience rather than at a station.
**V1 — Guide:** **Digital audio** — the inventory itself. Streaming music, digital streams of radio stations, podcasts, audio articles, smart speaker audio. All of it.
**V1 — Guide:** **Time-of-day targeting** — the same idea as a broadcast daypart, except aimed at a listener rather than a signal. Morning drive means the person's morning, wherever they are.
**V1 — Guide:** **Host-read versus inserted** — worth knowing because a client will ask. A host-read endorsement is the podcaster personally recommending a business, negotiated with that show. What we run is an inserted spot — the client's own audio, placed into the break. Different thing, and don't let a client assume otherwise.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *Reach them with no screen involved · Buy the listener, not the station · Their radio spot works here · Aimed by place, hour, and interest.* Payoff: a real morning drive-time photo beside a coffee shop counter — plus a real audio app on a car display. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? Ads placed across streaming music, digital radio, podcasts, audio articles, and smart speaker audio. Audience segments identified. Geographic targeting set with the client. A daily ad schedule, including time-of-day. Conversion pixel tracking. Weekly campaign refinement. Regular reporting. And audio production if they need it — though most clients with a radio spot already have what they need.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? They **reach people with no screen involved** — hours of the day nothing else touches. They **buy the listener instead of the station.** Their **existing radio spot goes to work** in a new place. And it's **aimed by place, hour, and interest** rather than by signal.
**V1 — Guide:** So picture a coffee drive-thru on the edge of town. Good coffee, faster than the chain, and almost nobody knows it's there — because the only people who find it are people who already drive past it.
**V1 — Guide:** After: their existing radio spot running across streaming music and podcasts, aimed at people inside a ten-minute drive, between six and nine in the morning. Which is to say: people already in a vehicle, already awake, already deciding where to stop.
**V2 — Scene:** *"I heard you on my podcast on the way in and thought — wait, that's the place by the light."*
**V1 — Guide:** — same spot they'd already paid to produce, in a completely different set of ears, at the exact minute the decision gets made. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: many services, one buy · 2: buy the listener, not the station · 3: the spot they already have. *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: one buy across many audio services — music, digital radio, podcasts, smart speakers. Two: buy the listener, not the station — location, time of day, interests, following the person instead of the signal. Three: the creative is a spot, usually one they already own, and the ears are genuinely available because the eyes are busy.
**V1 — Guide:** And the two lines to carry in. **It's the only advertising that works when nobody's looking at a screen.** And for anybody nervous about digital audio: **the audio audience didn't leave — it moved, and the fifty-five-plus listener is moving fastest.**
**V1 — Guide:** That's programmatic audio. Quick ten-question check. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION D — AE Field Notes *(not narrated)*

**How the advertiser pays.** Bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis**. One impression means the spot played in a listener's ears. Impression arithmetic is taught once, in Targeted Display.

**How it's sold — the easiest digital product for anyone with a traditional-media background.** Radio and TV sellers know the format and the creative usually already exists. Outdoor sellers already think in impressions and cost-per-thousand. Agency planners can slot it straight into an existing audio line. Open with: *"Your spot is already produced. Want it running where your customer is listening now?"*

**The internal reframe worth having.** Reps carrying broadcast inventory — radio or TV — sometimes see digital audio as competition for their own avails. The honest framing is that it follows the same listener into places broadcast can't go: the podcast, the streaming service, the earbuds at work. Sell the combination and reach goes up without the spot changing.

**Bundles with:**
- **Broadcast radio or TV** — the same spot, or the audio track from a TV commercial. Same audience, more of their day. The most natural pairing for anyone carrying broadcast.
- **Social OTT/CTV** — audio for screen-free hours, streaming TV for the living room. Together they cover a day.
- **Geofence and mobile audience targeting** — audio builds familiarity; those tactics catch people near a location.
- **Website / landing pages** — the pixel needs a site, and audio drives searches and direct visits rather than clicks, so a clean site matters.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"Digital audio is for kids."* → "Among Americans fifty-five and up, monthly online audio listening went from about half to about seven in ten in two years. Your customer is already there."
- *"Everybody pays for the ad-free version."* → Be honest: a real share of music listening is ad-free, especially on paid tiers. But podcasts and digital radio streams are heavily ad-supported, and the reachable pool is still enormous. Don't oversell this one.
- *"Can you get the host to say it?"* → No — that's a host-read endorsement negotiated with the show. This is their own spot inserted into the break. Set that expectation before it becomes a complaint.
- *"How do I know anyone heard it?"* → Straight answer: audio has no click, so this is measured on the pixel, on site traffic, and on people mentioning it. Say that up front rather than at renewal.
- *"I already run radio / TV."* → "Perfect. Same spot — or the audio off your TV commercial — no new production, and it follows the same person into their podcast and their earbuds at work."

**Lines for the room:**
- *"It's the only advertising that works when nobody's looking at a screen."*
- *"You're not buying a station and a daypart. You're buying the listener."*
- *"Your spot is already produced. This just puts it where they're listening now."*
- *"Nobody's watching a screen while they mow the lawn. They're still listening."*

**Retention note.** This is the hardest tactic in the catalog to prove, because there's no click. Set the measure at the first meeting — pixel activity, site traffic, and asking staff whether customers mention hearing it. A client who was never given a measure will decide on a feeling, and feelings cancel. Also worth noting: audio creative gets stale in the ear faster than people expect, so spot rotation is a natural recurring conversation.

**Writer's notes:**
- **Statistics sources:** all listening figures are from **Edison Research's The Infinite Dial 2026**, conducted with SSRS — 81% of Americans 12+ listening to online audio in the past month (about 233 million people), 76% in the past week, monthly online audio among Americans 55+ rising from 52% in 2024 to 70% in 2026, and 58% listening to podcasts monthly. **This is the best-sourced module in the 201 block — Infinite Dial is the industry's longest-running listening study, and it's a name radio people already respect. Cite it directly if a rep is challenged.** Re-verify annually, since it publishes every March.
- The honest limitation about ad-free listening comes from Edison's **Share of Ear**, which has reported that a substantial share of digital audio consumption is ad-free, concentrated in paid music tiers. Keeping that caveat in the script is deliberate — a rep who volunteers it earns more credibility than one who gets caught by it.
- **Deliberately excluded:** the CPM figure, the completion-rate estimate printed on the sheet, the monthly minimum, and the contract term. The completion figure was excluded per the standing rule on ad-performance metrics; the script conveys the same point through "generally can't be skipped" and the busy-eyes argument instead.
- **Payoff vertical (coffee drive-thru)** is new. Updated list: diner, roofer, flooring co., hearing clinic, insurance agency, storage co., garden center, medical clinic, auto repair shop, pest control co., RV dealership, used-car lot, physical therapy clinic, wedding/event venue, veterinary clinic, private school, clothing boutique, brewery/taproom, home builder, paint store, **coffee drive-thru**.
