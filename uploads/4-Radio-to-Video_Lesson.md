# Radio to Video Ad — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Creative Services (a production service, not a media tactic — see the note in Section B)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1262 counted spoken words ≈ **8:25** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *Every client who says "I don't have video" already does.*

## SECTION B — Radio to Video: Full Script

> **Note for the writer — this module is different from the others.** Every other module in the catalog teaches a *media tactic*: something a client buys, targeted at an audience, bought on a cost-per-thousand or per-click basis. **This one is a production service.** There's no targeting, no impressions, and no payment model in the media sense — so the segments that normally cover those instead cover the process and the output. The seven-segment structure and quiz blueprint are unchanged.
>
> **And position it accordingly.** This is the answer to the single most common objection across the entire video block. "I don't have any video" appears as a named objection in the social ads, social display, social OTT, programmatic video, YouTube, streaming TV, YouTube TV, Prime Video, and Hulu modules. Reps should think of this less as something to sell on its own and more as the thing that makes nine other conversations possible.

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a radio production console with a waveform on screen. Then that waveform being joined by video footage and a client logo appearing in the corner — **green box** on the logo. Then the finished ad playing on a phone in a social feed.

**V2 — Scene:** *"I'd love to, but we don't have any video."*
**V1 — Guide:** You are going to hear that sentence more than any other sentence in this job. Every video product we sell runs into it — the social ads, the streaming television, all of it. And here's the thing: it's almost never true.
**V1 — Guide:** Because most of those clients have a radio spot. A finished, produced, approved, currently-on-the-air radio spot that somebody wrote, somebody voiced, somebody mixed, and the client already signed off on.
**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: **Radio to Video** takes a client's finished radio spot — a thirty or a sixty they're already running — and our video team builds video around it. Visuals, plus the client's logo. What comes back is a video ad in the social-media style, ready to run.
**V1 — Guide:** And notice what we did *not* do. We didn't write a new script. We didn't book a shoot. We didn't ask the client to approve a new message. The message is the one they already approved and already believe in.
**V1 — Guide:** Here's the line to carry, and I want you to actually use it: **every client who says "I don't have video" already does.** They've just never heard it described that way.

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Two statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a split screen: on one side a production checklist with items crossed out in green — *script · voice talent · approval · mix* — and on the other side an empty checklist labeled *what's left: visuals.*

**V1 — Guide:** So why does this matter? Three reasons.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: video isn't optional anymore, and the client knows it.** Nearly every adult who's online watches video — it's up around ninety-five percent in a given month, and Americans now spend more than sixty percent of their screen time on it. Owners are aware of this. What stops them isn't doubt. It's not having anything to run.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: the expensive part is already done.** Think about what actually goes into a video ad. Somebody has to decide what to say. Somebody has to write it well. Somebody has to perform it. Somebody has to approve it. And somebody has to mix it so it sounds professional.
**V1 — Guide:** All five of those are finished. They exist, in a file, running on the air right now. The only missing piece is what people look at — and that's the piece we add. That's why this is inexpensive compared with producing a video from nothing.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: it removes the reason clients say no.** This is the practical one. Every time a client says they'd like to try streaming TV or social video "but we don't have anything," that conversation used to end. Now it doesn't. You've got an answer, and the answer is that they already own the hard part.
**V1 — Guide:** One honest note. This is a smart, efficient way to get a client on video — it is not the same thing as a purpose-built video shoot. A radio spot was written for the ear, so the visuals are supporting what's being said rather than telling their own story. That's a perfectly good trade for most local businesses, and being straight about it keeps expectations where they should be.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — an HVAC service van, a business with an obviously long-running local presence, a business making a seasonal push. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.
**V1 — Guide:** **Any client already running radio.** That's the whole list, honestly — if there's a spot on the air, there's a video ad waiting inside it. Start every one of these conversations with the clients you already have.
**V1 — Guide:** **Clients who've said no to a video product before.** Go back through them. If somebody passed on social video or streaming television because they had nothing to run, that objection is gone and the conversation is worth reopening.
**V1 — Guide:** **Clients with a seasonal push and no time.** Somebody who needs to be visible in three weeks doesn't have time to plan a shoot. They do have time to hand over a spot that's already finished.
**V1 — Guide:** And the honest flip side: if a client's message is genuinely visual — a transformation, a demonstration, a process somebody needs to *see* — a purpose-built video will serve them better, and you should say so. This works best when the message is a message, not a demonstration.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) A finished spot file being uploaded, **green box** on *:30 or :60* and on the word *finished*. (2) Video and a logo being layered onto the waveform → *the video team builds it*. (3) The completed ad appearing in a social feed → *ready to run*. Closes on text: *Send the spot · We add the visuals · It's ready to run.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts, and it's the simplest process in the whole catalog.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: send us the finished spot.** A thirty or a sixty. And "finished" is the important word — this is the produced, mixed, on-air version, not a script and not an idea. The audio arrives done.
**V1 — Guide:** Which also means the client isn't being asked to make anything or approve a new message. That's most of why this is an easy yes.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: our video team builds the visuals.** They add video to the audio, and they add the client's logo. The audio itself is untouched — the same voice, the same words, the same spot the client already knows and likes.
**V1 — Guide:** Think of it as putting a picture to a song they already chose.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: it comes back ready to run.** What you get is a video ad in the social-media style, built for the way people watch on a phone rather than the way a television commercial was built thirty years ago.
**V1 — Guide:** And that's the moment to have the *next* conversation, because now they have video. Social ads. Social display. Streaming television. Programmatic video. All of those were closed doors five minutes ago.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on a finished waveform and a logo placement.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.
**V1 — Guide:** **Radio to Video** — taking a client's finished radio spot and building video around it, with their logo, to produce a video ad.
**V1 — Guide:** **Fully produced spot** — a finished, mixed, approved commercial that's actually running. Not a script, not a draft, not an idea. This is what we need in order to start.
**V1 — Guide:** **Repurposing** — using creative a client already paid for in a new place. It's one of the most useful ideas in this whole business, because it's the cheapest way to get somewhere new.
**V1 — Guide:** **Social-style video** — video built for the way people watch on a phone, rather than the way a broadcast commercial was cut. Different shape, faster, designed for a feed.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *The message is already approved · No shoot, no script · Inexpensive next to production · Opens every video product.* Payoff: an HVAC service van photo beside the finished video ad playing in a feed. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? A client's finished thirty or sixty-second radio spot converted into a video ad. Video built around the existing audio by our video team. The client's logo included. And an output built in the social-media style, ready to run.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? **A message that's already approved**, so there's nothing new to argue about internally. **No shoot and no script** to write. Something **inexpensive next to producing video from nothing.** And access to **every video product we sell**, which was closed to them before.
**V1 — Guide:** So picture a heating and cooling company. They've run the same radio spot for six years — the owner's voice, a line about being family-owned since the seventies, and the phone number twice. It works. Everybody in town knows it.
**V1 — Guide:** And every time somebody pitched them social video or streaming TV, the answer was the same: we don't have anything for that.
**V1 — Guide:** After: that same spot, that same voice, with video and their logo on it — running in social feeds during the first cold snap in October.
**V2 — Scene:** *"That's the ad I hear in my truck. I didn't know you were on there too."*
**V1 — Guide:** — and that's the quiet advantage nobody plans for. The same voice in two places doesn't feel like two ads. It feels like a company that's everywhere. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: send the spot · 2: we add the visuals · 3: it's ready to run. *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: send us the finished spot — a thirty or a sixty, produced and on the air. Two: our video team adds video and the client's logo, leaving the audio exactly as it is. Three: it comes back as a social-style video ad, ready to run.
**V1 — Guide:** And the one line to carry into every meeting: **every client who says "I don't have video" already does.** Learn that sentence. It will open more doors for you than anything else in this course.
**V1 — Guide:** That's Radio to Video. Quick ten-question check. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION D — AE Field Notes *(not narrated)*

**This is a production service, not a media buy.** There's no impression model, no targeting, and no cost-per-thousand. It produces an asset. Sell it as the thing that makes a media conversation possible.

**Its real job: it removes the most common objection in the catalog.** "I don't have any video" is a named objection in nine modules — social ads, social display, social OTT/CTV, programmatic video, YouTube video, streaming TV, YouTube TV, Amazon Prime Video, and Hulu. **Every one of those becomes sellable the moment this exists.** Reps should treat this as a key, not a product.

**The highest-value activity this unlocks:** go back through every client who declined a video product because they had nothing to run. That's a call list built from past losses, and the objection that killed those deals is gone.

**How it's sold.** Two openers:
- To a current radio advertiser: *"That spot you're running — want it working in a couple more places?"*
- To a client who's said no to video before: *"You told me you didn't have video. You do. It's on the air right now."*

**Bundles with — this is the point of the product:**
- **Social media ads** and **social display** — the most natural home for social-style video.
- **Streaming TV, YouTube, Prime Video, Hulu, programmatic video** — all need video; all were closed without it.
- **Broadcast radio** — the spot keeps running on air, and the same voice showing up in a feed reinforces both. Worth saying to a radio client: this doesn't replace your radio, it extends it.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"My radio spot is for radio, not video."* → Fair, and be honest: the visuals support what's being said rather than telling their own story. For most local messages that's exactly right. If their message truly needs a demonstration, recommend a purpose-built video instead.
- *"Won't it look cheap?"* → Don't oversell. It's a professionally built video ad using their existing audio — it isn't a produced commercial shoot, and it isn't meant to be. Show an example rather than describing it.
- *"I'd rather make a real video."* → Great — that's a bigger conversation and a better product. This is for clients who want to be on video *now* without waiting on production.
- *"Can you change what the spot says?"* → No. The audio is used as-is. If the message needs changing, that's a new spot first.

**Lines for the room:**
- *"Every client who says 'I don't have video' already does."*
- *"That spot you're running — want it working in a couple more places?"*
- *"The expensive part is already done. The only thing missing is what people look at."*
- *"It's putting a picture to a song they already chose."*

**Retention note.** This is a one-time production, so there's nothing to retain directly — but the media it unlocks is recurring. The failure mode is producing the asset and never following through on the media conversation. Book the next meeting when the ad is delivered, not later.

**Writer's notes:**
- **No sales sheet exists for this product.** This script was written from a verbal description and several details are inferred. See the information gaps below and fill them before recording.
- **Statistics:** the two figures used — roughly 95% of online adults watching video in a given month, and US adults spending over 60% of screen time on digital video — are borrowed from the programmatic video module (DataReportal/GWI and Statista respectively). That's deliberate: as a production service this product has no effectiveness data of its own, so the evidence establishes *why video matters* rather than claiming anything about this service. Attribute as "research shows."
- **Nothing excluded for pricing reasons**, because no pricing information was supplied. If there's a production fee, it stays out per standing rules.
- **The honest limitation is stated on camera** — a radio spot was written for the ear, so visuals support rather than lead. That's in the script and in the objections. Keeping it protects reps from a client who expected a commercial shoot.

## APPENDIX — Information Gaps & Prompt Used

**⚠️ Ten things I had to infer or leave vague. Confirm these and the script tightens considerably.**

1. **Output aspect ratios.** Square, vertical, horizontal — or all three? This matters enormously, because vertical is needed for stories and Reels, horizontal for streaming TV and pre-roll. The script says "social-media style" and never names a shape. **This is the most important gap.**
2. **Is this usable for streaming TV and OTT, or social only?** The script implies it opens every video product. If the output isn't suitable for connected TV, that claim needs pulling back immediately.
3. **What the visuals actually are** — licensed stock footage, motion graphics, the client's own photos, or a mix? Reps will be asked and currently have no answer.
4. **Does the client see and approve the visual treatment before delivery**, or is it delivered finished?
5. **Are revisions included?** If a client hates the footage choice, what happens?
6. **Are both :30 and :60 equally usable in digital?** A sixty-second social ad is long. Is the sixty trimmed, or delivered full length?
7. **Can the client supply their own footage or photos** to be used instead of stock?
8. **Does this require the radio spot to be one we're airing**, or will any station's spot do? The description says "our radio broadcasters can upload," which suggests it's tied to spots running with us — worth confirming, because it changes who's eligible.
9. **Is there a captions or on-screen-text option?** Many people watch with sound off, and an audio-led ad is badly exposed without text on screen. **If captions aren't included, that's worth adding — it's the single biggest weakness of an audio-first video ad.**
10. **Is it sold standalone, or only attached to a media buy?**
