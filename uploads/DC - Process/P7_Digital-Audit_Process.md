# Digital Audit — Process Script
**Pillar:** Process · Block 7 of 11
**Audience:** Account executives and agency staff — internal
**Format:** Motion graphics over real screen capture · AI voice read (two voices) · animated text · green callout boxes
**Runtime:** 1920 counted spoken words ≈ **12:48** at 150 wpm
**Spine — the one sentence somebody must repeat cold:** *Prove the deficiency. Then provide the solution.*

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES — READ BEFORE RECORDING

**Two voices.** **V1 — Guide** carries all teaching. **V2** is either **the rep** or **the client**. Distinctly different ElevenLabs voice.

**Written for an AI read.** Short sentences. No semicolons, no parentheticals.

**The humour is recognition, not jokes.** Nothing depends on a pause or a dry delivery.

**⚠️ Write around the method, not the interface. The tool is being rebuilt.** Teach what each category measures and what a low score means commercially. That way a redesign only requires new screen capture, not a new script.

**⚠️ The scores are internal. Do not teach reps to present numbers.** This is deliberate and it matches the prospecting tool, where the grade never reaches the client either. Scores tell a rep where the deficiencies are. **What the client sees is the finding itself — the evidence.** A number on a screen reads as a report card on somebody's livelihood, and a business owner who feels graded stops listening.

**The sales method this block teaches: prove a deficiency, then provide a solution.** In that order, one at a time, without numbers.

**Transitional note.** The website assessment now has its own dedicated tool. This script tells reps to use that for the website portion and the audit for the remaining categories. **Re-record when the two are combined.**

**No illustrations, no icon cards, no mascots.**

**V1 — Guide:** That is not a small advantage. It is most of the reason these meetings go differently.

**V1 — Guide:** And it runs **after** the needs analysis, not before. It needs the profit centers you captured, because those become the search terms it checks them against.

**V1 — Guide:** Now here is the frame for the whole block, and it is how we sell. **You are not delivering a score. You are sharing a discovery.**

**V1 — Guide:** You looked, you found a couple of things holding them back, and you show them what fixes it. That is the method: **prove the deficiency, then provide the solution.**

## SEGMENT 2 — WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

**ON-SCREEN:** Two columns build as text — *your opinion* versus *a generated score* — with a **green box** on the second. Then the six categories animate with a product name appearing beside each.

**V1 — Guide:** Three reasons, and the third one is about the order you sell things in.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. It takes your opinion out of it, and nobody can match it.** Without the audit you are a salesperson telling somebody their website is slow. With it, you both looked at the same thing and it said what it said.

**V1 — Guide:** And because it is proprietary, the competitor calling on that business next week cannot bring one. They will bring a brochure and a price. You brought a diagnosis.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Every deficiency has a solution attached.** Website, social media, reputation, local listings, search visibility — every one is a room in the Digital House. So you never have to work out what to propose. The audit finds the gap and there is something that closes it.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three, and this is how we build accounts. Foundation first.**

**V1 — Guide:** Look at what it examines. Their website. Whether people can find them. Whether their information is right. Whether anybody is saying good things about them. **That is the foundation, and the content that sits on it.**

**V1 — Guide:** None of it is targeted advertising. That comes later, for a reason. Sending paid traffic to a business with a slow website and wrong listings spends their money delivering people into a bad experience.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So fix the foundation, then advertise."*

**V1 — Guide:** Fix the foundation, build the content, then go get traffic. In that order. The audit is how you show a client why that order exists — and it means your second sale to them is easier than your first, because the first one made it work.

## SEGMENT 3 — THE WALKTHROUGH

**ON-SCREEN:** Real screen capture throughout, following the path, **green box** on each element as it is named.

**V1 — Guide:** Four stages. Set it up properly, generate it, read the six categories, then walk the client through it.

### One — set it up properly

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the Google Business Profile, then on the audit input fields as details are pasted in.

**V1 — Guide:** The tool is in **step two** of your resource site. When you open it you get an input form, and how you fill this in matters more than it looks.

**V1 — Guide:** **Do not type the business details from memory.** Open their **Google Business Profile** and copy the business name, the address, and the website exactly as they appear there.

**V2 — Rep:** *"Does that really matter? I know how to spell their name."*

**V1 — Guide:** It matters because consistency is what is being measured. Google compares those details across dozens of directories to decide whether this is a real, credible business. Type a slightly different version and you are auditing a business that does not exist.

**V1 — Guide:** Copy and paste. Every time.

**V1 — Guide:** Then their **four profit centers**, which you already have from the needs analysis. These become the search terms, so specificity is everything. *Metal roofing* is a search term. *General contracting* is a category nobody types.

**V1 — Guide:** Then paste in their **social media links**, which are almost always in the footer of their own website.

**V1 — Guide:** Check it once, then generate.

### Two — the website portion

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the website category, then a cut to the dedicated website research tool.

**V1 — Guide:** One thing about the current setup. **The website assessment now has its own dedicated tool** — the one you used for prospect research, and it goes deeper than the audit's website area. So for now, use that for the website side and this audit for everything else. They will be combined eventually.

### Three — read the six categories

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box moving across each category tab in turn, with the score and a product name appearing beside each.

**V1 — Guide:** Six areas. Read them for yourself first, and read them looking for **deficiencies** — the things you could most easily improve. Here is what each one examines and what it points to.

**V1 — Guide:** **Overview.** Your orientation. Where to look next. Not part of the client conversation.

**V1 — Guide:** **Website.** Whether the site exists, whether it is sound, whether it is secure, and how it behaves on a phone. A deficiency here points to a website conversation, and it is usually the first thing to fix.

**V1 — Guide:** **Social media.** Whether they are active anywhere meaningful, how big the audience is, how often anything gets posted. A useful benchmark is something every two to three days. Deficiency points to social management, social advertising, or both.

**V1 — Guide:** **Reputation.** How many reviews they have and what people are saying. This one matters twice over, because customers read them and Google counts them. Deficiency points to reputation management.

**V1 — Guide:** **Local listings.** Whether their name, address, and phone number match across dozens of directories — all of them, including the ones nobody has heard of, because Google reads them all. Deficiency points to listing optimisation.

**V1 — Guide:** **And search visibility.** Whether they turn up, right now, for their own name and for those profit-center terms you entered. This is usually the most persuasive thing in the whole audit, because it is live and it is about them.

**V1 — Guide:** You will see numbers as you go through this. **Those are for you.** They help you spot the biggest gaps quickly. They are not what you show the client, for the same reason the prospecting grade never leaves your hands — a number on somebody's business reads as a judgement, and people stop listening once they feel judged.

### Four — walk them through it

**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a rep and a client looking at the audit together, then a green box on a single low-scoring category.

**V1 — Guide:** Now the part that decides whether any of this was worth doing. And before the shape of it, one hard rule.

**V1 — Guide:** **You present this live, on a laptop or an iPad, sitting with them. You never print it, you never email it, and you never leave it behind.**

**V2 — Rep:** *"Not even a copy for their file?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Not even that. And this is exactly why. The tool is ours, the method is ours, and it is the reason your meeting is different from everybody else's. Hand somebody a printed copy and there is a decent chance it ends up on a competitor's desk — where it stops being your advantage and becomes their homework.

**V1 — Guide:** So it stays on your screen. Turn the laptop around and scroll through it together. Better meeting anyway — you are looking at it *with* them rather than watching them read.

**V1 — Guide:** And if you are wondering how that squares with the monthly reports we tell you to print and hand over, the difference is simple. **The report is proof of what they bought — give it to them. The audit is how we find things — that one stays with us.**

**V1 — Guide:** Now, the shape of the conversation.

**V1 — Guide:** **Start with something they are doing well.** There is almost always one. It costs you thirty seconds, it is true, and it means the meeting is not a list of failures.

**V1 — Guide:** Then take **one deficiency at a time** and run it through three beats.

**V1 — Guide:** **Prove it.** Show them the actual thing — the search where they do not appear, the directory with the wrong phone number. Not a number. The thing itself.

**V1 — Guide:** **Explain why it costs them.** *"When somebody searches for metal roofing around here, you are not on that page. Whoever is, gets that call."*

**V1 — Guide:** **Then provide the solution.** One thing that fixes it, and what it involves.

**V1 — Guide:** Then stop and let them react before the next one. **One deficiency, one solution, one at a time.** Two in a meeting is plenty. Five is a list of everything wrong with somebody's life's work, and nobody buys from that meeting.

**V2 — Client:** *"Nobody has ever shown me this before."*

**V1 — Guide:** You will hear that a lot. That sentence is the audit doing its job.

## SEGMENT 5 — WHERE THIS GOES WRONG

**ON-SCREEN:** Each mistake appears as text beside the real screen element it relates to.

**V1 — Guide:** Six things.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. Typing the business details instead of copying them.** You have now audited a business that does not quite exist, and the listings result is worthless. Open the Google Business Profile and copy.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Vague profit centers.** If you entered a category rather than something a customer would type, the search results mean nothing. This is where a thin needs analysis shows up, and it shows up in the most visible part of the audit.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Printing it, emailing it, or leaving a copy.** Understandable instinct, and it hands our method to whoever they talk to next. Live on your screen, every time.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four. Showing them numbers.** The scores are yours. Put a number on somebody's business and they hear a grade, and a person who feels graded stops listening. Show them what you found instead — the search, the wrong phone number, the empty page.

**V1 — Guide:** **Five. Piling on.** Nobody buys from a list of everything wrong with their business. One deficiency, one solution, then stop and let them respond. Two per meeting is plenty.

**V1 — Guide:** **And six. Selling advertising before the foundation is fixed.** Tempting, because advertising is the bigger number. But paid traffic to a slow website with wrong listings spends their money badly, and it makes your next conversation harder. Foundation, content, then traffic.

**V1 — Guide:** And the handoff. You now have a conversation, a set of scores, and a clear idea of which rooms this business needs. From here you either pitch it directly, or you send it to our strategy team and let them build the recommendation. That decision is the next block.

## TRAINER NOTES *(not narrated)*

**Where this sits.** Block 7 of 11. **Requires block 6** — the four profit centers become the audit's search terms. Feeds block 8, where the rep decides whether to pitch directly or request a recommendation.

**The sales method this block teaches: prove a deficiency, then provide a solution.** One at a time, without numbers. The audit's scores are an internal navigation aid — they tell a rep where to look. **What reaches the client is the finding itself:** the search they do not appear in, the directory with the wrong phone number, the page that loads slowly on a phone. This matches the prospecting tool, where the grade is also internal.

**Foundation, then content, then targeted advertising.** This is the account-building sequence and the audit is the instrument that justifies it to a client. Everything the audit examines sits in the first two areas of the Digital House. Targeted advertising comes after, because paid traffic delivered into a weak foundation wastes the client's money and makes the next conversation harder.

**The deficiency-to-solution map is the commercial value of this block:**
| Deficiency found in | The room that fixes it |
|---|---|
| Website | Managed websites / the website platform |
| Social media | Social media management · social media ads |
| Reputation | Reputation management |
| Local listings | Local listing optimisation |
| Search marketing | Search engine marketing |

**Worth noting what the audit does *not* cover** — email, display, geofencing, streaming, video, CRM targeting. **The audit sells the foundation products. The needs analysis and the recommendation sell the growth tactics.** Reps who understand that split stop trying to make the audit justify a streaming TV buy.

**⚠️ This tool is a proprietary competitive advantage, and reps should know it.** We built it and only our partners have it. **The competitor calling on that business next week cannot bring one** — they arrive with an opinion and a rate card. That is the single biggest reason these meetings go differently, and it is worth stating in training rather than assuming reps work it out.

It is also the reason for the handling rule below. The tool is only an advantage while it stays ours.

**⚠️ The audit is never printed, emailed, or left with a client.** It is presented **live on a laptop or iPad**, in person. The tool is proprietary and a printed copy can end up being used to sell against us.

**Name the contrast with monthly reports, because it is the opposite instruction.** Block 11 tells reps to print the client report and hand it over. The distinction the script gives: **the report is proof of what they bought, so give it to them. The audit is how we find things, so it stays with us.** Reps who understand the reason will not mix them up.

**The numbers never leave the rep's hands.** Worth checking in the weekly certification meeting — ask a rep how they would present a weak listings result. If the answer contains a number, the block did not land. The correct answer is the wrong phone number on a real directory page.

**The mistakes, with the symptom somebody would notice:**
| Mistake | What it looks like when it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Typing details instead of copying | Listings result is nonsense; client says "that's not even our address" |
| Vague profit centers | Search results show nothing meaningful; a thin CNA becomes visible to the client |
| Printing or leaving the audit | Our method ends up on a competitor's desk, used against us |
| Showing numbers | Client hears a grade on their livelihood and stops listening |
| Piling on deficiencies | Client overwhelmed, buys nothing, "let me think about it" |
| Leading with bad news | Meeting feels like an audit of the owner rather than a plan |
| Selling advertising first | Client's money spent driving traffic into a weak foundation; next sale is harder |

**Practical note for trainers.** Reps need a device they are comfortable presenting from. The same iPad used to capture an insertion order signature works here, and pairing the two in training reinforces both.

**Suggested work product for this block.** One completed audit on a real prospect, plus a written note of **the two deficiencies they would raise, the evidence they would show for each, and the solution attached.** No numbers in the note. That is the test — if a rep cannot describe the deficiency without citing a score, they will cite the score in the meeting.

**Writer's notes:**
- **Written around the method, not the interface**, per direction. The tool is being rebuilt, so the script describes what each category measures rather than naming exact screen elements. A redesign should require new screen capture only.
- **The transitional split is stated openly** — the dedicated website research tool handles the website portion for now, the audit handles the rest. Re-record that beat when they are combined.
- **The CNA dependency is confirmed direction.** Without the profit centers, the search-marketing results are built on guessed keywords.
- **The missing local-listings score is no longer an issue.** Since numbers are internal and never presented, a category without a score does not create a client-facing question. Worth confirming internally, but it no longer affects the script.
- **Scoring is deliberately de-emphasised throughout**, per direction. The tool measures; the rep discovers and reports. If a future version of the tool foregrounds scores more heavily in its interface, this script's guidance still holds — the numbers guide the rep, the findings persuade the client.
- **Cross-module dependencies:** blocks 5, 6, and 7 form the research sequence and should be reviewed together. Block 11's reporting has an "other services" page that pairs with the lowest-scoring category from this audit — that link is worth reinforcing in both.
