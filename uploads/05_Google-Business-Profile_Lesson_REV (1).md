# Google Business Profile — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Online Visibility (Room #5 in the Digital House)
**Audience:** Radio reps learning digital, selling to small mom-and-pop businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime target:** ~6–6.5 minutes + 10-question quiz
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics.

## SECTION A — The Reusable Tactic Lesson Template
*(Use this exact shape for all ~25 tactic modules. Only the content changes — the beats never do.)*

Two questions before you write: **"What's the plain-English definition?"** and **"What are the 3 core mechanics?"** Everything else follows the beats.

**Voice convention (all modules):**
- **V1 — Guide:** the teaching spine. Carries every segment.
- **V2 — Scene:** short in-the-world lines only — a customer's search, a quoted reaction, a review snippet, and the quiz prompts. Its job is to signal a "mode switch" and keep the ear awake.
- *(Optional V3 later if you want a distinct quiz host — but two voices covers everything below.)*

**On-screen rule — the visual kit (all modules):** keep it clean and real. Three ingredients only:
1. **Animated text** — key words and short lines build/fade in as V1 speaks. This is the workhorse.
2. **Real photos** — actual screenshots of the product (e.g., a real Google Business Profile on a phone) and real photography of people/businesses (an HVAC tech, a small business owner, a diner interior). No clip-art, no cartoon figures.
3. **Green highlight box** — a green callout box drawn over a real screenshot to point at a specific section (same style as the sales sheet: box the reviews, box the Call button, etc.), with a text label animating in beside it.

**No cheesy line illustrations, icon/emoji cards, or metaphor graphics** (no glowing houses, cash-register drawings, trust meters, little walking figures). Any analogy the Guide speaks stays *spoken only* — we don't draw it. Quiz cards being tappable is UI, not illustration, so that stays.

**Quiz blueprint (same every tactic):** Q1–3 = the 3 mechanics · Q4–6 = terms · Q7 = who it's good for + why · Q8 = why-it-matters scenario · Q9–10 = features/benefits (one may use the Segment-6 payoff).

**Answer placement rule (all modules):** vary which letter is correct — roughly even across A, B, C, and D, with no repeating pattern. If the correct answer sits at B nine times out of ten, trainees stop reading the options and start pattern-matching, and the quiz measures nothing. Also keep the wrong answers plausible; obviously silly distractors have the same effect.

**Payoff rules (Segment 6):** pick the easiest-to-picture business from Segment 3; keep outcomes plain and clearly *illustrative* — no invented numbers, no real names.

## SECTION B — Google Business Profile: Full Script

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is *(0:00–0:45)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Real phone screenshot of a "near me" search result (the local pack — map + top businesses with stars and a Call button). The search text *"pizza near me"* animates into the search bar. A **green box** draws around the top result, and the text label *Google Business Profile* animates in beside it. *(Photo option: brief real photo of a person on their phone in a kitchen.)*

**V2 — Scene:** *"Pizza near me."*
**V1 — Guide:** Picture someone in your town. It's 6pm, they're standing in the kitchen, and they type that into their phone. In about one second, Google hands them a little map with three restaurants — stars, photos, hours, and a Call button. Whoever's in that box is about to get dinner money. Whoever's not, isn't.
**V1 — Guide:** That box — the name, the map pin, the hours, the reviews, the photos — that's a **Google Business Profile**. In plain terms, it's the free listing that pops up when someone Googles your business by name, or searches for what you sell nearby.
**V1 — Guide:** Think of it as the address sign and the front window of your business, but on the internet. It's how people find the door — and the first peek inside before they walk in.
**V1 — Guide:** And if you've never set one up, or never even heard the term — good, you're normal. Most business owners haven't either. That's exactly why they need you.

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters *(0:45–1:30)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of someone looking at their phone (small-town/main-street feel). Then two real profile screenshots side by side — one complete (photos, correct hours, good reviews), one bare (no photos, one old review). A **green box** flags the differences on each. Stakes text builds: *first impression → before the website → the handshake.*

**V1 — Guide:** Here's what's changed. People in small towns used to drive around, or ask a neighbor, or flip through the phone book. Now they just ask their phone. Who's open? Who's close? Who's any good?
**V1 — Guide:** So the stakes are simple, and kind of brutal. You can be the best barbershop in the county and still lose the customer to the shop down the road — not because that shop is better, but because it showed up on the phone and you didn't. Or you showed up with the wrong hours, no photos, and one sad review from 2019.
**V1 — Guide:** For a small business, this profile is usually the very first impression. Before the website. Before anything. It's the handshake — and you only get one.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For *(1:30–2:15)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos appear one at a time — a restaurant/café (plate of food or interior), an HVAC/plumber tech at work, a salon or auto shop. Each business type's name and its one-line reason type in beside the photo.

**V1 — Guide:** So who do you picture pitching this to? Three easy ones.
**V1 — Guide:** **Restaurants and cafés.** People choose where to eat straight off the profile — the food photos, the star rating, the hours. They decide before they ever click a website.
**V1 — Guide:** **Home and repair services** — plumbers, HVAC folks, electricians. These are "my water heater just died, who's open and close" searches. That tap-to-call button turns a panic into a booked job in about four seconds.
**V1 — Guide:** **Appointment businesses** — salons, dentists, auto shops. It's all about trust before booking, and the reviews and accurate info are where that trust gets built.
**V1 — Guide:** Notice the thread: anytime a customer is nearby, ready to act, and deciding right there on their phone — that's a Google Business Profile business.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics *(2:15–4:00)*
**ON-SCREEN:** One real profile/search screenshot on screen the whole time; a **green box** moves to the relevant section for each mechanic, with its label animating in. (1) Green box around the map / top results → label *local pack*. (2) Green box around the Call / Directions buttons → label *action buttons*. (3) Green box around the reviews + star rating → label *trust signals*. Closes on text: *Show up · Sell on the spot · Earn trust.*

**V1 — Guide:** How does this actually work? Three moving parts. Just three.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: local placement.** When someone searches "near me," Google acts like that friend who always knows the nearest good spot. It picks businesses that are close by *and* have a complete, active profile. That little map with the top three has a name — the **local pack**. Getting into it is the whole game.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: the listing does the selling.** This is the one people miss. The profile isn't just an ad pointing at a website — it's a storefront window that's *also* a cash register. Right there, the customer can see photos, read reviews, tap to call, or tap for directions. Those are the **action buttons**. The sale can happen entirely on the listing.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: trust signals.** Google and customers both ask the same quiet question — "is this a real, cared-for business?" Reviews, verified info, fresh photos, and owner replies all answer *yes*. A busy profile is the lit-up shop waving you in. An empty one is the place with the dusty window and the blinds down.
**V1 — Guide:** That's it. Show up, sell on the spot, earn trust.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms *(4:00–4:45)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and its one-line definition animate in as text, one at a time. For the three terms you can actually see on a profile (local pack, knowledge panel, reviews & rating), a **green box** highlights that element on the real screenshot as it's named. GBP and Verification are text-only.

**V1 — Guide:** Five terms so you sound like you know your stuff — because now you do.
**V1 — Guide:** **Google Business Profile, or GBP** — the free listing itself. Only acronym you need today.
**V1 — Guide:** **Local pack** — the little map with the top few local businesses at the top of a "near me" search.
**V1 — Guide:** **Knowledge panel** — Google a business by name and that info box appears with hours, phone, photos, and reviews. That's the knowledge panel.
**V1 — Guide:** **Reviews and rating** — the star score and the comments. The single biggest trust factor on the whole profile.
**V1 — Guide:** **Verification** — the step where Google confirms you're the real owner, usually with a code, so you're allowed to manage the listing.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff *(4:45–6:00)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature names animate in as a text list (green box ticking to each area on the real screenshot as it's mentioned). Then four benefit lines build in as text: *Get found · Build trust · Turn searches into calls · Learn how customers find you.* For the payoff: two real diner-profile screenshots side by side — **before** (bare) and **after** (photos, correct hours, replies) — with a real photo of a diner/small-business owner. A small text tag reads *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's actually *in* a profile? Your core info — hours, phone, address — plus photos and your logo, the Call / Directions / Website / Message buttons, reviews you can reply to, little posts you can share, and a behind-the-scenes dashboard showing how people found you.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? Four things. They **get found** by people ready to buy right now. They **build trust** through reviews and accurate info. They **turn searches into calls and visits**. And they **learn** how customers are finding them.
**V1 — Guide:** Under the hood it's simple: the profile plugs straight into Google Search and Google Maps — the two places nearly everyone already looks — and Google rewards profiles that are complete, active, and well-reviewed by showing them higher.
**V1 — Guide:** So back to that restaurant. Picture a little family diner. Before: a bare listing, wrong hours, no photos, two ancient reviews — easy to scroll past. After we claimed it, added real photos, fixed the hours, and started replying to reviews —
**V2 — Scene:** *"Hey — I found you on Google!"*
**V1 — Guide:** — they saw a real uptick in folks walking in saying exactly that. Nothing magic. The profile just finally looked as good as the actual place. *(That's the kind of change to picture — an illustration, not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge *(6:00–6:30)*
**ON-SCREEN:** The three mechanics recap as animated text — 1: local placement · 2: the listing does the selling · 3: trust signals — with the real screenshot behind them and the green box tapping each section one last time. A *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts, one more time. One: local placement — showing up in that map for nearby searches. Two: the listing does the selling — call, directions, photos, reviews, right on the profile. Three: trust signals — reviews and activity that say "this is the real deal."
**V1 — Guide:** That's the Google Business Profile — the front window of the Digital House. Quick ten-question check to lock it in. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION C — Quiz (10 Questions)
**ON-SCREEN:** One card per question, four tappable options; correct choice highlights green on select. **V2 — Scene** reads each question stem aloud.

**1.** When someone searches "coffee shop near me," how does Google mostly decide which businesses show at the top?
A) Whoever has the biggest website
B) Whoever spends the most on radio ads
C) Whoever is nearby and has a complete, active profile ✅
D) It's random each time

**2.** A customer finds a restaurant's profile and taps "Directions" without opening the website. This shows that…
A) The profile itself can turn a search straight into a visit ✅
B) The website must be broken
C) The customer made a mistake
D) Directions don't really matter

**3.** Two plumbers show up. One has recent reviews and replies to them; the other has none. Who's more likely to get the call — and why?
A) The one with no reviews, because it looks newer
B) Neither — reviews don't affect calls
C) Whichever is first alphabetically
D) The one with reviews and replies, because it looks active and trustworthy ✅

**4.** What is the "local pack"?
A) A subscription you buy from Google
B) The small map with the top few local businesses at the top of a nearby search ✅
C) A folder of business documents
D) A type of paid ad

**5.** You Google a business by name and a box appears with its hours, phone, photos, and reviews. That box is the…
A) Landing page
B) Local pack
C) Knowledge panel ✅
D) Homepage

**6.** "Verifying" a Google Business Profile means…
A) Paying a monthly fee
B) Automatically getting five-star reviews
C) Linking a social media account
D) Proving to Google you're the real owner so you can manage it ✅

**7.** Which business is an especially strong fit for a Google Business Profile, and why?
A) A national online-only retailer, because it ships everywhere
B) A local diner, because people choose where to eat from the photos, hours, and reviews on the profile ✅
C) A business with no customers
D) A company that only sells to the government

**8.** A small-town hardware store has the wrong hours and no photos on Google. A shopper searching nearby will most likely…
A) Pick the competitor whose profile looks complete and correct ✅
B) Call to double-check the hours
C) Drive over anyway
D) Wait until tomorrow

**9.** Which of these can a business owner actually do with their Google Business Profile?
A) Delete a competitor's listing
B) Change Google's results for everyone
C) Reply to customer reviews ✅
D) See customers' private phone numbers

**10.** In the diner example, what actually changed after the profile was claimed and updated?
A) The food got better
B) They stopped taking walk-ins
C) They deleted all their reviews
D) The profile finally matched the quality of the real place, and more people came in saying they found it on Google ✅

**Answer key:** 1-C · 2-A · 3-D · 4-B · 5-C · 6-D · 7-B · 8-A · 9-C · 10-D
