# MRR Prospect Research — Process Script
**Pillar:** Process · Block 5 of 11
**Audience:** Account executives and agency staff — internal
**Format:** Motion graphics over real screen capture · AI voice read (two voices) · animated text · green callout boxes
**Runtime:** 1676 counted spoken words ≈ **11:10** at 150 wpm
**Spine — the one sentence somebody must repeat cold:** *A high grade does not mean they are doing well. It means call them first.*

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES — READ BEFORE RECORDING

**Two voices.** **V1 — Guide** carries all teaching. **V2 — Rep** asks what the viewer is thinking. Distinctly different ElevenLabs voice.

**Written for an AI read.** Short sentences. No semicolons, no parentheticals.

**The humour is recognition, not jokes.** Nothing depends on a pause or a dry delivery.

**⚠️ The single most important thing in this block is the inversion.** The grade rates the **opportunity**, not the business. A trainee will assume the opposite — that an A means a healthy business — and will then prospect exactly backwards. **Say it in the first thirty seconds and say it again in the recap.**

**Visuals: screen capture throughout.** Every field named should be visible with a **green box** on it. **No illustrations, no icon cards, no mascots.**

**Write around the interface, not through it.** The tool is new and will change. Teach what each finding *means* and what to do about it, so the voice-over survives a redesign and only the screen capture needs re-recording.

## SEGMENT 1 — WHERE THIS FITS

**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a long list of local businesses on a desk. Then the eleven-block pillar with a **green box** on block five. Then a real screen capture of the tool's entry field.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So which of these do I call first?"*

**V1 — Guide:** That question costs more time than any other in this job. A territory full of businesses, no obvious way to rank them, so you start at the top and hope.

**V1 — Guide:** This block fixes that. **MRR prospect research** takes a business name and a website and comes back with two things: everything worth knowing about how that business shows up online, and a **grade** telling you whether they are worth your time.

**V1 — Guide:** And it runs completely cold. No meeting, no call, no needs analysis, and nothing needed from the business itself. Just a name and a web address.

**V1 — Guide:** Now here is the thing to get straight before we go any further. **This is a prospect grade, not a report card on the business.**

**V1 — Guide:** So an **A** does not mean a healthy business. An A means a business that needs you badly. Specifically: they **have no website at all**, or the one they have is **very outdated**, or it is **very slow.**

**V2 — Rep:** *"So an A is the one I want."*

**V1 — Guide:** An A is the one you call this morning. The scale is doing exactly what it says — it grades the prospect, and the best prospect is the one with the most wrong with it.

**V1 — Guide:** So: **a high grade does not mean they are doing well. It means call them first.** Read it as a health score and you will spend your week on the businesses who need you least.

## SEGMENT 2 — WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

**ON-SCREEN:** Two paths build as text — *graded list* versus *alphabetical list* — with a **green box** on the first. Then a real findings panel with several items marked weak.

**V1 — Guide:** Two reasons this exists, and the second one is the whole point of the tool.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. It tells you where to spend your week.** Prospecting blind means giving your best hours to whoever you happened to think of. This ranks them before you make a single call, and tells you what to lead with.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two, and this is why it is called MRR.** That stands for **monthly recurring revenue.** This tool exists to find businesses whose website is quietly failing them — and the answer to that is **Fourge**, our website platform, sold on a monthly recurring basis.

**V1 — Guide:** Look again at what earns an A. No website. A very outdated website. A very slow website. Those are not three random criteria. **That is a description of a Fourge customer.**

**V1 — Guide:** So the tool is not a general-purpose report. It is a machine for finding Fourge prospects. The research finds the problem. Fourge is what fixes it.

**V1 — Guide:** And here is what happens if you skip it. You walk in cold, talk about digital in general terms, and they nod politely. Or you walk in already knowing their site is slow on a phone, they show up for one search term, and a company three towns over is beating them. Completely different conversation — and you needed nobody's permission to prepare for it.

## SEGMENT 3 — THE WALKTHROUGH

**ON-SCREEN:** Real screen capture throughout, following the path, **green box** on each element as it is named.

**V1 — Guide:** Four things. Put it in, read what came back, check the two or three things the tool cannot see, then read the verdict.

### One — put it in

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the business name and website fields.

**V1 — Guide:** The tool lives in **step two** of your resource site, right below the digital audit. Do not let that fool you. It sits below the audit on the page, but it comes **first** in the process, because this one runs cold and the audit needs a needs analysis behind it.

**V1 — Guide:** Enter the business name and their website. That is the whole input.

### Two — read what came back

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box moving across the findings panel — authority, traffic, keywords, paid search, then Google's site health scores, then the money search, then competitors.

**V1 — Guide:** Everything on this screen is pulled automatically, and every finding has a small information icon. **Tap it.** It tells you what that number means in plain language, which is genuinely useful the first dozen times.

**V1 — Guide:** And here is something that is easy to miss, because it looks like a finished report. **You can adjust these criteria yourself.** The tool's assessment is a starting point, not a verdict.

**V2 — Rep:** *"Why would I change what the data says?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Because automated data misses things, and you will sometimes know more than it does. Maybe their traffic looks thin because the site launched six weeks ago. Maybe the search term the tool picked is not what people around here actually call it. You adjust it to be **more accurate**, and the grade recalculates around what you know.

**V1 — Guide:** One discipline with that, and it matters. **Adjust it toward the truth, not toward the answer you want.** Talking a prospect up into an A does not create an opportunity. It just means you spend your morning somewhere that was never going to buy.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is what to actually look at.

**V1 — Guide:** **The headline numbers.** Site authority, monthly traffic, how many search terms they show up for, whether they run paid search. Each flagged **strong, okay, or weak.**

**V1 — Guide:** **Google's own live site test.** Mobile speed, accessibility, search checks, best practices — each out of a hundred. Google's assessment, not ours, which makes it very hard to argue with.

**V1 — Guide:** **The money search.** This is the one search term that matters most for that business — the thing a customer would actually type. The tool tells you the search volume and whether the business showed up at all.

**V1 — Guide:** **And who is winning instead.** The sites beating them for that term.

**V2 — Rep:** *"One of these competitors is a national company with eight hundred thousand visits. That cannot be right."*

**V1 — Guide:** It is right, and it is useless. That is a national platform, not a business down the road. **There is an X beside each one — dismiss it.** What you want is the local competitor just ahead of your prospect, because that is the comparison that lands in a meeting.

**V1 — Guide:** One more thing that trips people up. Sometimes the money search comes back with **zero searches a month.** That does not mean nobody wants what they sell. It means the phrase is wrong. Try how a customer would actually say it.

### Three — check what the tool cannot see

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the website override section — site age and design, mobile-friendly toggles, pre-filled values.

**V1 — Guide:** Below the automatic findings is a short section **pre-filled from their homepage** — how old the site looks, whether it works on a phone.

**V1 — Guide:** Pre-filled means guessed. So **open their website on your phone** and look at it for ninety seconds, then correct anything the tool got wrong.

**V1 — Guide:** Three settings on each — **strong, okay, or weak.** And remember what weak means here. **Weak is not a criticism. Weak is an opportunity to pitch.**

### Four — read the verdict

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the grade panel — the letter, the priority label, the opportunity line. Then on Services to Pitch. Then on the client report button.

**V1 — Guide:** Now the grade. You get a letter, a priority label, and a one-line summary of where the opportunity is.

**V1 — Guide:** An **A** is the top of the list — no website, very outdated, or very slow. Further down you get something like **B, secondary priority, opportunity is weak search visibility, solid prospect worth pursuing.** Still worth a call, just not the first one you make today.

**V1 — Guide:** That is your call-order decision, and it is for you. Not for them.

**V1 — Guide:** Underneath it you get **services to pitch** — the specific things this business needs. And here is the part worth noticing. A typical list: search optimisation, accessibility, targeted landing pages.

**V1 — Guide:** **Fourge includes all three of those.** So most of the time, that list of separate problems has one answer, and it is one monthly line item rather than four conversations.

**V1 — Guide:** And there is a **client report** you can print or save as a PDF — the evidence, the Google scores, the competitor comparison. That is what you take into a meeting.

## SEGMENT 5 — WHERE THIS GOES WRONG

**ON-SCREEN:** Each mistake appears as text beside the real screen element it relates to.

**V1 — Guide:** Five things, and the first one is the big one.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. Reading it as a health score.** A high grade means big opportunity, which means call them. Get that wrong and you skip your best prospects. Worth a sticky note for the first month.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Taking the competitor list at face value.** Dismiss the national platforms. Comparing a small contractor to a company with eight hundred thousand monthly visits does not make your point — it makes you look like you did not read your own research.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Panicking at zero search volume.** That is a wording problem, not a business problem. Rephrase the search the way a customer would say it out loud.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four. Treating the findings as final.** The automatic assessments at the top are adjustable and the website checks at the bottom are pre-filled guesses. Spend ninety seconds on their actual site on your actual phone, then correct anything the tool got wrong. That is where you will find the thing worth leading with.

**V1 — Guide:** **And the flip side of that — do not adjust it to get a better grade.** The grade only helps you if it is honest. A manufactured A costs you a morning.

**V1 — Guide:** **Five. Showing the client the grade.** That is your prioritisation. The **findings** are what the client sees, and they are plenty — Google's own scores and a competitor beating them beat any letter.

**V1 — Guide:** And the handoff. Once the grade says this business is worth pursuing and you know what to lead with, you go have the conversation. That is the needs analysis, the next block — and it is what makes the digital audit possible later.

## SEGMENT 6 — RECAP AND WHERE THIS LEADS

**ON-SCREEN:** The four steps recap as text, then the pillar animates forward with a **green box** moving from block five to block six.

**V1 — Guide:** Four steps. Put in a name and a website — nothing else needed. Read the findings, tapping the information icons, dismissing any competitor who is not a real competitor, and adjusting anything the tool got wrong. Look at their site yourself and correct the pre-filled checks. Then read the verdict and the services to pitch.

**V1 — Guide:** And the line to keep. **A high grade does not mean they are doing well. It means call them first.** An A is a business with no website, a very outdated one, or a very slow one — which is to say, an A is a business waiting for Fourge.

**V1 — Guide:** Remember what the tool is for. It finds businesses whose website is failing them, and Fourge is the answer to that. The research finds the problem. Fourge fixes it, monthly.

**V1 — Guide:** Next up is the conversation itself — the client needs analysis. Take the quiz and we will see you there.

## TRAINER NOTES *(not narrated)*

**Where this sits.** Block 5 of 11. First tool used on a prospect. Runs **cold** — no CNA required, which is why it precedes the needs analysis and the digital audit.

**⚠️ The grade is a prospect grade. That is the whole block.** **A = the best prospect**, defined as a business with **no website, a very outdated website, or a very slow website.** The scale is internally consistent — the only trap is a trainee assuming it is a health score. **Check this in the weekly certification meeting** — ask a rep what an A means. If they say "they are doing well," the block did not land and they will prospect backwards for months.

**Note the A criteria are website-failure criteria.** That is deliberate: this tool exists to find Fourge prospects, so the top grade describes a business that needs a website. Reps who understand that will connect the grade to the product without being told.

**Contrast it explicitly with the Digital Audit (block 7)**, where scores run the other way: on the audit, a high score means the business is performing well. Two tools, two scales, opposite directions. Reps will meet both in the same week.

**The mistakes, with the symptom somebody would notice:**
| Mistake | What it looks like when it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Reading the grade as health | Best prospects skipped; worst ones chased |
| Keeping national competitors | Rep compares a local contractor to an enterprise platform in a meeting |
| Panicking at zero volume | Prospect written off over a badly worded search term |
| Treating findings as final | The most persuasive finding — a bad mobile experience — never gets noticed |
| Adjusting criteria to inflate a grade | A manufactured A wastes a morning and erodes trust in the tool |
| Showing the client the grade | Business owner reacts to a letter instead of the evidence |

**Fourge is the product this tool sells.** MRR is monthly recurring revenue. The tool finds failing websites; Fourge is the recurring-revenue answer. **And the services-to-pitch list is usually a Fourge list** — search optimisation, accessibility, and targeted landing pages are all included in the platform, so several separate findings collapse into one line item. Make sure reps see that.

**Suggested work product for this block.** Each AE runs the tool on **three real prospects in their own territory** before the block is signed off, and brings the graded results to their weekly account manager meeting. Nothing teaches the inversion faster than seeing it on businesses they actually know.

**Writer's notes:**
- **Written around the method, not the interface**, per direction — the tool is new and will change. Field names are described by what they mean rather than by exact label, so a redesign should only require new screen capture.
- **The A criteria are confirmed and stated in the script:** no website, very outdated website, or very slow website. **The lower bands are not documented.** The script uses the observed B example (secondary priority, weak search visibility) and describes the rest as descending opportunity rather than inventing labels. **Supplying the full scale would tighten Segment 3 further** but the block is teachable without it, because A is the one that drives behaviour.
- **The adjustable criteria at the top of the audit are now taught**, along with the discipline that goes with them: adjust toward accuracy, never toward a better grade. This was missing from the first draft.
- **⚠️ Unconfirmed: whether the letter grade appears on the client-facing report.** The script tells reps the grade is internal and the findings are client-facing, which is the safe reading. **If the grade does print on the client report, that guidance needs changing** — and arguably the report needs changing.
- **Cross-module dependency:** block 7, the Digital Audit, has an opposite scale and requires CNA input. Blocks 5, 6, and 7 should be reviewed together if any one changes.
- Product eligibility badges appear on some findings, indicating whether a given product will work on that business's current setup. The script does not go into detail, since the logic may change alongside Fourge. **Confirm how this should be taught.**
