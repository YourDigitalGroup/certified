# YouTube Video Ads — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Targeted Digital Marketing (Room #22 in the Digital House)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1725 counted spoken words ≈ **11:30** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *You get five seconds. Make them count.*

## SECTION A — The Reusable Tactic Lesson Template
*(Identical across all modules. Only the content changes — the beats never do.)*

**Voice convention:** **V1 — Guide** carries the teaching. **V2 — Scene** delivers only in-world lines and the quiz prompts.

**On-screen kit:** animated text · real photos and product screenshots · green callout boxes. **No line illustrations, icon cards, mascots, or drawn metaphors.** Spoken analogies stay spoken.

| # | Segment | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook + What It Is** | Relatable moment → one-sentence definition |
| 2 | **Why It Matters** | Why an advertiser should do this + the evidence |
| 3 | **Who It's Great For** | 3 categories, one reason each + who it's wrong for |
| 4 | **How It Works — 3 Core Mechanics** | Exactly 3, roughly 4 beats each |
| 5 | **Common Terms** | Four to five terms maximum |
| 6 | **Features, Benefits & The Payoff** | Included → benefits → one payoff story |
| 7 | **Recap + Quiz Bridge** | Restate the 3 mechanics, warm handoff |

**Quiz blueprint:** Q1–3 the three mechanics · Q4–6 terms · Q7 fit · Q8 scenario · Q9–10 features/benefits. Vary the correct letter across A–D.

## SECTION B — YouTube Video Ads: Full Script

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real screenshot of a YouTube video titled something like *how to change a hydraulic filter*, with a local business's ad playing before it — **green box** on the *Skip Ad* countdown. Then the same ad shown on a phone, a laptop, and a living-room TV, with a green bracket linking all three.

**V2 — Scene:** *"How to change the hydraulic filter on a — okay, this one looks right."*
**V1 — Guide:** That man is standing in a shop with a wrench in his hand and a problem to solve. He came to YouTube to learn something, and he'll sit through five seconds of somebody's ad to get it.
**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: **YouTube video ads** are video ads that run on YouTube — and on Google's partner sites and apps — shown only against content related to the business.
**V1 — Guide:** One thing to be precise about, because we sell two different YouTube products. **This one is YouTube the video platform** — creators, how-to videos, reviews, the content people search for. **YouTube TV is a separate product**: that's the live television service with local channels, national channels, and live sports. Different inventory, different buy, different sheet. Don't blend them.
**V1 — Guide:** What makes *this* one distinctive is that you can choose **the actual videos and channels your ad plays before.** Not just an audience — the content itself. Nothing else we sell lets you do that.
**V1 — Guide:** It's bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis.** And the line to carry, because it governs everything: **you get five seconds. Make them count.**

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Three statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a real living-room TV showing YouTube, **green box** on it. Then a YouTube search bar with a how-to query typed in. Then text: *television and a search engine at the same time.*

**V1 — Guide:** So why should an advertiser do this? Three reasons.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: nearly everybody's there.** Pew Research puts YouTube at eighty-four percent of American adults — the highest reach of any platform, and the only one where a majority of every adult age group shows up. There's no "my customers aren't on it" conversation to have.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two — and this is the one reps miss: YouTube is a search engine.** It's the second-largest one there is, handling billions of searches a month. People don't just wander in. They arrive with a question — how to fix it, how to install it, what to look for before buying one.
**V1 — Guide:** That's a different viewer than somebody watching a show — that's somebody with a problem, actively solving it. The closest thing to search intent that video advertising has.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: you can pick the content itself.** Every other video product we sell buys an audience and hopes the surroundings work out. Here you name the channels and the subject matter. For a business with a narrow customer, that's worth more than any demographic.
**V1 — Guide:** One honest note: most people will skip. That's built in, and fine. But it means the creative has to earn the stay — a real conversation to have before a client hands you a slow-building commercial.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a farm equipment lot or service bay, a music store or hobby shop, a business demonstrating a service. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses whose customers watch how-to videos.** Farm and equipment dealers, auto parts, hardware, pools, music stores, hobby shops. Their customer is already on YouTube learning to do the thing, and the ad can be aimed at exactly those videos.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses with something to demonstrate.** Equipment, services, transformations, facilities. Video does the showing, and YouTube is where people go expecting to watch something.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses with a narrow, specific customer.** A niche is usually a weakness in advertising — too small to buy efficiently. Here it's an advantage, because you can aim at the exact videos that niche watches instead of paying to reach a whole market to find them.
**V1 — Guide:** Honest flip side: this needs video. No video and no budget to make any is a conversation to have first — production is available, but don't let it surprise them. And a business with nothing visual and nothing to explain does better in search.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) Four format cards build as text with their clocks — *skippable · non-skippable · bumper · outstream* → *four formats, four clocks*. (2) A real channel-selection view with specific channels checked, **green box** on them → *choose the content*. (3) A timeline with the first five seconds boxed in green → *the five-second rule*. Closes on text: *Four formats · Choose the content · Five seconds.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: four formats, and each one has a clock.** Know these, because the first thing a client will ask is how long their video should be.
**V1 — Guide:** **Skippable ads** — skip allowed after five seconds; the video runs at least twelve seconds and up to three minutes. The workhorse, fits almost any objective. **Non-skippable** — up to fifteen seconds, watched all the way through. Best for straight awareness. **Bumper ads** — six seconds, unskippable, and that's the whole ad. Great for one simple idea repeated often. **Outstream** — runs on Google's partner sites and apps rather than YouTube itself, mobile and tablet only.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: choose the content, not just the audience.** This is the mechanic that makes YouTube different from everything else we sell, and it's the one to lead with in a meeting.
**V1 — Guide:** We can target by **channel** — naming the actual channels the ad should run on. By **topic and placement** — the subject matter of the videos. And by **audience and demographics** on top of that, the way we do everywhere else.
**V1 — Guide:** So an equipment dealer doesn't buy "men thirty-five to sixty-five." They buy tractor maintenance videos. Somebody watching a hydraulic repair walkthrough is a better prospect than any demographic description of him.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: the five-second rule.** Everything about the creative follows from one fact: on the workhorse format, the viewer can leave after five seconds.
**V1 — Guide:** So the name goes in early. The point goes in early. Any offer goes up front. A commercial that builds to a reveal is one almost nobody sees the end of — and saying that kindly, before production, saves everybody a month.
**V1 — Guide:** Then the housekeeping: identifying topics and placements, channel targeting set up, audience and demographic layers, full campaign management, weekly refinement, reporting, and video production if they need it.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on a Skip Ad countdown, a six-second bumper, and a channel list.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.
**V1 — Guide:** **Skippable and non-skippable** — skippable lets the viewer out after five seconds and can run up to three minutes. Non-skippable holds them for up to fifteen. Different tools: one earns attention, one buys it.
**V1 — Guide:** **Bumper ad** — six seconds, can't be skipped. Too short to explain anything, perfect for one idea people see repeatedly.
**V1 — Guide:** **Channel targeting** — choosing the specific channels an ad runs on. The single most distinctive thing about advertising on YouTube.
**V1 — Guide:** **Outstream** — YouTube ads that run on Google's partner sites and apps instead of on YouTube itself. Mobile and tablet only, so don't promise a client television placement from an outstream buy.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *Pick the actual content · Reach people solving a problem · The widest reach of any platform · Formats for any length of message.* Payoff: a real farm-equipment service bay photo beside a YouTube how-to video with an ad playing. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? Campaigns across YouTube and Google's partner network. All four formats. Topics and placements identified. Channel targeting. Audience and demographic targeting. Full campaign management with weekly refinement. Reporting. And video production if they need it.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? The ability to **pick the actual content** their ad plays against — which nothing else offers. Access to **people actively solving a problem** rather than passively watching. **The widest reach of any platform**, across every age group. And **formats for any length of message**, from six seconds to three minutes.
**V1 — Guide:** So picture a farm equipment dealer. Two locations, good service department, and their advertising has always been the same quarter-page ad with a photo of a tractor and a phone number.
**V1 — Guide:** After: a fifteen-second spot showing their service bay and a technician, running before equipment repair and maintenance videos — the specific channels their customers already watch when something breaks. Name and location on screen in the first three seconds, because most of them would skip at five.
**V2 — Scene:** *"I was watching a video on how to fix it myself, and your ad came on, and I thought — or I could just call these guys."*
**V1 — Guide:** — and that's the whole thing. Not a demographic. A man with a broken machine, watching a repair video. You can't buy that moment anywhere else. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: four formats · 2: choose the content · 3: five seconds.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: four formats with four clocks — skippable up to three minutes, non-skippable up to fifteen seconds, bumper at six, and outstream on partner sites, mobile and tablet only. Two: choose the content — channels, topics, and placements, on top of audience. Three: the five-second rule, which decides how the creative gets written.
**V1 — Guide:** And two things to carry in. **You get five seconds — make them count.** And the differentiator: **nowhere else can you choose the actual videos your ad plays before.** Also keep the two YouTube products separate — this is the video platform, YouTube TV is the live television service.

### SEGMENT 8 — Into the Room
**ON-SCREEN:** Objections as animated text. Then three real channel thumbnails with a **green box** on each — a how-to channel, a local sports channel, a review channel. Then three sentences. Then *Quiz →*.

**V1 — Guide:** Before the quiz — two questions you'll get, the argument I'd lead with, and one thing to settle before you leave.

**V1 — Guide:** First, and it comes up with almost every older client.

**V2 — Scene:** *"My customers are older. They're not on YouTube."*

**V1 — Guide:** They are, and the numbers aren't close. Around eighty-four percent of American adults use it — it's one of the few platforms with genuinely no age ceiling.

**V1 — Guide:** But don't argue with the number. Ask instead: *"When your furnace went out, did you watch a video about it first?"* Almost everybody says yes, and then they've made your point for you.

**V1 — Guide:** Second, the one that will trip you up if you haven't sorted it in your own head.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Isn't this the same as the YouTube TV thing you pitched me?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Different products. **"This is the YouTube platform — the videos people search for and watch on purpose. YouTube TV is the live channel lineup, the cable replacement."**

**V1 — Guide:** Two names, two products, two different buys. Get that clear before you walk in, because a confused rep creates a confused client.

**V1 — Guide:** Now the argument I'd lead with, because nothing else in the catalogue can do it.

**V1 — Guide:** **You can choose the actual videos your ad plays in front of.**

**V1 — Guide:** Think about how you've bought television your whole career. You buy a programme, or a daypart, or a rating. You get near the content you want.

**V1 — Guide:** Here you can name it. The local high school sports channel. The channel reviewing the exact equipment your client sells. The how-to videos people watch right before they give up and call a professional. **That's not adjacency — that's picking the moment.**

**V1 — Guide:** And it's the reason a small business can compete here. You don't need a big budget to own a small, precise piece of attention.

**V1 — Guide:** Three sentences to know cold. To open: **"Nowhere else can you choose the actual videos your ad plays before."** On the platform confusion: **"This is the YouTube platform. YouTube TV is the live channel lineup — different product."** And on creative: **"You get five seconds. Say who you are and why they should care before the skip button lights up."**

**V1 — Guide:** And the one thing to settle before you leave — the creative. A commercial that saves the name for the end will not work here. Agree that the first five seconds carry the message, and if they only have a radio spot, that's a conversation about turning it into video.

**V1 — Guide:** That's YouTube video ads. Quick ten-question check.

## SECTION C — Quiz (10 Questions)


**1.** What is true of the four ad formats?
A) They all allow the viewer to skip after five seconds have elapsed
B) They are all priced identically on a cost-per-view basis
C) Each one has a different length limit and a different clock ✅
D) Only two of them are available to local advertisers

**2.** "Choose the content, not just the audience" means you can…
A) Name the channels and topics your ad plays in front of ✅
B) Select which demographic groups are shown the advertisement
C) Pick the time of day
D) Decide how many times each viewer will see the advertisement

**3.** What is the five-second rule?
A) Ads must run five seconds
B) Viewers who watch five seconds are billed as a completed view
C) Creative must be refreshed every five weeks to avoid fatigue
D) Everything essential happens before the skip button appears ✅

**4.** A "bumper ad" is…
A) A fifteen-second non-skippable spot
B) Six seconds, non-skippable ✅
C) An end-of-video ad
D) A short ad that appears as an overlay on the video player

**5.** "Channel targeting" lets an advertiser…
A) Choose which streaming service the ad appears on
B) Choose a network
C) Limit the campaign to channels above a set subscriber count
D) Pick the specific channels their ad runs before ✅

**6.** "Outstream" refers to ads that run…
A) Outside the client's chosen geography
B) After the main content has finished playing entirely
C) On partner sites and apps, not on YouTube itself ✅
D) Without sound unless the viewer chooses to unmute them

**7.** Which is the strongest fit?
A) A client whose only piece of video saves the business name right until the very end
B) A paint store, because people watch how-to videos before starting ✅
C) A business whose customers never watch online video of any kind
D) A client who wants to buy the local evening news

**8.** What is the core benefit?
A) Choosing the exact videos your ad appears before ✅
B) A lower cost per view than television advertising
C) Guaranteed completion of every advertisement served
D) The live channel audience

**9.** A client says their customers are too old for YouTube. What do you ask?
A) Whether they have taken a proper look at their own website analytics recently
B) Whether they would consider a smaller test budget to find out
C) What age range they believe their customers actually fall into
D) When their furnace went out, did they watch a video about it first ✅

**10.** What should be agreed about the creative?
A) That a new commercial will be produced before launch
B) The first five seconds carry the message ✅
C) That the video will run no longer than fifteen seconds
D) That captions are added

**Answer key:** 1-C · 2-A · 3-D · 4-B · 5-D · 6-C · 7-B · 8-A · 9-D · 10-B

## SECTION D — Trainer & Writer Reference *(not narrated)*

> **Note on structure.** The objections, lines, and first-meeting warning that reps most need are **narrated in Segment 8**, immediately before the quiz. Everything below is reference for trainers and for the rep card.

**How the advertiser pays.** Bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis**. Impression arithmetic is taught once, in Targeted Display.

**⚠️ This is not YouTube TV, and it is not streaming TV. Three separate products.**
- **YouTube Video Ads (this):** the YouTube video platform — creators, how-to content, reviews, the videos people search for. Plus Google's partner network for outstream. **The distinctive capability is channel and topic targeting.**
- **YouTube TV Ads (Room 24):** the live television subscription service — local channels, national channels, live sports, breaking news. A television buy.
- **Streaming TV (Room 23):** ads inside streaming services like Hulu, Tubi, and Pluto. Also a television buy, different inventory.

Never pitch this one as "television." If a client wants live TV, that's the YouTube TV product and it's a different conversation, different inventory, and a different budget. Blending them is the fastest way to sell something the client didn't get.

**How it's sold.** The strongest opener is about content, not audience: *"What does your customer type into YouTube when something goes wrong?"* Every good answer is a channel target. Owners find this concrete in a way that demographic targeting never is.

**Then handle length before production.** Ask what they have and how long it is. A thirty-second commercial usually needs its front end rebuilt, not just trimming. A client who understands the five-second rule *before* they write the script saves a month of rework.

**Bundles with:**
- **YouTube TV** — the natural pair for a client who wants both the searched-for content and live television, but sell them as two distinct line items so nobody's confused about what they bought.
- **Programmatic video** — the same creative running around video on other sites and apps.
- **SEM** — the pairing to point out: search ads catch the person typing into Google, this catches them typing into YouTube. Same instinct, two search engines.
- **Website / landing pages** — the click needs a destination worth landing on.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"My customers are older."* → "Pew has YouTube at eighty-four percent of American adults, and it's the only platform where a majority of every single age group shows up."
- *"Everybody skips those ads."* → Be straight: most do. That's why the name and the point go in the first five seconds, and why we can also use non-skippable and bumper formats where it matters.
- *"Isn't this the same as YouTube TV?"* → No, and be clear: YouTube TV is the live television service with local channels and sports. This is the YouTube video platform — how-to videos, reviews, creators. Two products, two sheets.
- *"I don't have a video."* → Production is available. Also check for an old TV commercial; it may need its opening rebuilt rather than being remade.
- *"Can you put me on a specific channel?"* → Often yes, and that's the best question a client can ask. Lean into it.

**Lines for the room:**
- *"You get five seconds. Make them count."*
- *"Nowhere else can you choose the actual videos your ad plays before."*
- *"What does your customer type into YouTube when something goes wrong?"*
- *"You're not buying men thirty-five to sixty-five. You're buying tractor maintenance videos."*
- *"A niche audience is usually too small to buy efficiently. Here it's an advantage — you aim at the exact videos they watch."*

**Retention note.** Two risks. **Creative decay** — a single spot running for months against a narrow set of channels gets seen repeatedly by the same people and starts getting skipped on sight; rotation is a natural recurring conversation. And **the skip anxiety problem**: a client who watches his own ad get skipped will lose confidence fast, so pre-frame at the first meeting that skipping is normal and expected, and agree on what you'll actually watch together.

**Writer's notes:**
- **Statistics sources:** the 84% of US adults figure is **Pew Research Center**, which also finds YouTube the only platform where a majority of every adult age group is present. YouTube as the second-largest search engine handling billions of monthly searches is widely reported and consistent across sources. Attribute as "research shows" and re-verify annually.
- **Deliberately not used here:** Nielsen's TV watch-time share for YouTube. That data is real and strong, but it belongs to the **YouTube TV** module — using it here would position this product as a television buy, which it isn't, and would step on a product sold separately.
- **Deliberately excluded:** the CPM figure, the completion-rate estimate, the monthly minimum, the contract term, and the production quote. The format length specs *were* kept — they're creative specifications rather than pricing, and a rep genuinely cannot brief a client without them.
- **Confirm before recording:** whether skippable ads on this program are billed on impressions throughout or whether a view-based model applies to any portion. The script says cost-per-thousand per the sheet and deliberately does not get into billing nuance beyond that, but reps should not be surprised by a client's question here.
- **Payoff vertical (farm equipment dealer)** is new. Updated list: diner, roofer, flooring co., hearing clinic, insurance agency, storage co., garden center, medical clinic, auto repair shop, pest control co., RV dealership, used-car lot, physical therapy clinic, wedding/event venue, veterinary clinic, private school, clothing boutique, brewery/taproom, home builder, paint store, coffee drive-thru, technical college, senior living community, **farm equipment dealer**.
