# Insertion Orders — Process Script
**Pillar:** Process · Block 9 of 11
**Audience:** Account executives and agency staff — internal
**Format:** Motion graphics over real screen capture · AI voice read (two voices) · animated text · green callout boxes
**Runtime:** 1612 counted spoken words ≈ **10:45** at 150 wpm
**Spine — the one sentence somebody must repeat cold:** *Nothing happens until the IO exists.*

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES — READ BEFORE RECORDING

**Two voices.** **V1 — Guide** carries the teaching. **V2 — Rep** is the voice of the person learning, asking what the viewer is already thinking. Use a distinctly different ElevenLabs voice for V2. It should sound like a colleague, not a narrator.

**Written for an AI read.** Sentences are deliberately short. No semicolons, no parentheticals, no nested clauses. Numbers that should be spoken as words are written as words. If a line reads awkwardly in synthesis, break it shorter rather than rewriting it longer.

**The humor is in the words, not the delivery.** Nothing here depends on a pause, a dry tone, or comic timing, because synthesis will not deliver those. It depends on recognition — the viewer thinking *yes, that has happened to me.* If a producer is tempted to add a pause for effect, the line needs rewriting instead.

**Visuals: real screen capture, motion graphics on top.** Every step should show the actual form. Motion graphics handle the animated text, the green highlight boxes, the transitions, and the emphasis. **No illustrations, no icon cards, no mascots** — same rule as the tactic modules.

**Avoid spoken number collisions.** The form has three *steps* and segment five has five *mistakes*. Never say "step three" inside the mistakes list, because in an audio read the listener cannot tell which "three" is meant. The mistakes refer to screens by name — "the review screen" — not by number.

**Terms and fees are deliberately absent.** The agreement section is covered in separate compliance training. This script names that it exists and moves on.

## SEGMENT 1 — WHERE THIS FITS

**ON-SCREEN:** The pipeline animates left to right as text — *CNA · Audit · Recommendation · Proposal · **Insertion Order** · Fulfillment* — with a **green box** landing on Insertion Order. Then a real screen capture of the IO form loading.

**V2 — Rep:** *"Alright, they said yes. Now what?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Now the fun part. Everything up to here has been talking. This is where it becomes real.

**V1 — Guide:** The **insertion order** is the agreement between your group and your client. Who they are, what they bought, what it costs, when it starts. It is the thing that turns a conversation into a campaign.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is the sentence to remember. **Nothing happens until the IO exists.** No setup, no build, no ads. Our team is not being difficult about that. Until the IO exists there is no record of what was sold.

**V1 — Guide:** So a signed IO sitting in your truck is not a sold campaign. It is a piece of paper in a truck.

## SEGMENT 2 — WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

**ON-SCREEN:** A real submission confirmation screen, then a **green box** on the message about the Trello card being built and the account manager being notified. Text builds: *one form · three things happen.*

**V1 — Guide:** Here is what most people miss about this form. It is not paperwork. It is a trigger.

**V1 — Guide:** The moment you submit it, things start happening on their own. A client record gets created. A Trello card gets built on your group's board. The account manager gets notified.

**V1 — Guide:** And copies go out to everybody who needs one. Your client gets it. Your group's accounting department gets it. Our accounting department gets it. Nobody has to be chased for a copy.

**V1 — Guide:** You do not have to email anybody, or follow up, or wonder whether it went through.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So I do not need to text somebody to check?"*

**V1 — Guide:** You do not. But if you would feel better seeing it with your own eyes, go look at your Trello board. The card will be sitting there with the insertion order and all the details on it. That is your receipt.

**V1 — Guide:** And that is the difference between this and the old paper version, which needed a human being on the other end to notice it had arrived.

**V1 — Guide:** Which is also why it is worth taking a little care as you go. Your account manager still reviews every order, so you are not out there without a net. But two accounting departments are going to bill from what you entered, so getting it right the first time saves everybody a round of corrections.

## SEGMENT 3 — THE THREE STEPS, WALKED THROUGH

**ON-SCREEN:** Three step tabs animate in — *Client Info · Services · Review and Submit*. Then the real form, walked through, with a **green box** moving to each field group as it is named.

**V1 — Guide:** The form has three steps. Client info, services, then review and submit. Let us go through them.

### Step one — client info

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the *I am* dropdown, then the Trello handle field auto-filling. Then the returning client dropdown.

**V1 — Guide:** Start by telling it who you are. There is an **I am** dropdown. Pick your name and it fills in your Trello handle. Not in the list yet? Just type your information in instead.

**V1 — Guide:** Then the important one. **Returning client, or new client.**

**V1 — Guide:** If this business is already in the system, pick them from the dropdown. Their information fills in, and everything attaches to the client they already have.

**V1 — Guide:** If they are genuinely new, leave it on new client and fill in the fields. Business name, contact, phone, email, website, service area, business type.

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on campaign dates. The end date auto-calculates as the length is selected.

**V1 — Guide:** Then campaign dates. IO date, start date, and length. Pick the length and the end date calculates itself, so you are not counting months on your fingers.

**V1 — Guide:** And there is a **save draft** button. Use it. Half the information in a parking lot? Save it and finish later. Nobody is timing you.

### Step two — services

**ON-SCREEN:** Services being selected, with running totals updating. Green box on *one-time total*, *monthly recurring*, *services selected*.

**V1 — Guide:** Step two is what they bought. As you select services, three numbers update at the bottom. One-time total, monthly recurring, and how many services are selected. Watch that monthly recurring number — it is the one that matters most to your business and to theirs.

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the *Workflows Triggered* panel populating as services are added.

**V1 — Guide:** And selecting a service does more than add a line item. Certain services trigger workflows automatically, and you will see them appear as you go. The form is not just recording the sale. It is starting the work.

**ON-SCREEN:** The kick-off call panel appears. Green box on the locked date and time fields, then on the *Open the AM's Calendar* button.

**V1 — Guide:** Some services need a **kick-off call**, and when one is required the form will tell you. There are two steps to it, in order.

**V1 — Guide:** First, open the account manager's calendar and actually book the call. Second, enter the date and time you booked.

**V2 — Rep:** *"What if I just put a date in and sort it out later?"*

**V1 — Guide:** You cannot. Those fields stay locked until you open the calendar. That is on purpose, and it is doing you a favor. A kick-off call that exists on the form but not on anybody's calendar is a call that does not happen.

**ON-SCREEN:** The intake form panel opens. Green box on *Need AM Help*, then on *Save Intake Info*.

**V1 — Guide:** Step two is also where the **intake forms** live now. They used to be separate documents. Now they are built in, attached to the services you selected.

**V1 — Guide:** Most of the time you will fill these in yourself, and you are well placed to. You just had the needs analysis conversation. You already know their goals, their audience, what they are trying to sell. A lot of these answers came up last week.

**V1 — Guide:** Where you are not sure, you have options. Leave it blank and keep going. Or if a section really needs the client in the room, the **need help** button brings your account manager in to work through it with them.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So it is fine to use that?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Completely. It is there for exactly that. The only thing worth avoiding is guessing, because a guess reads like a fact to whoever builds the campaign.

**V1 — Guide:** One exception, and it is a nice one — one less thing on your plate. **The website intake form is always handled by the account manager.** Websites have a lot of moving parts, so if a website is on the order, your account manager takes that conversation. You do not need to touch it.

### Step three — review and submit

**ON-SCREEN:** The review screen with the services summary and client recap. Green box on the agreement checkboxes, then the signature panel showing draw and type options.

**V1 — Guide:** The last step shows you everything back. Services with their fees, and the client information you entered. Read it. This is the last moment a typo is free.

**V1 — Guide:** Below that is the **agreement section**, with the terms your client is accepting. There are separate confirmations rather than one, because they cover different things.

**V1 — Guide:** We cover those terms properly in their own training, so I will not walk them here. One thing though. Do not click through them silently. Your client is agreeing to real commitments, and hearing them from you beats discovering them later.

**V1 — Guide:** Then the signature. And here is the part worth planning for, because it can change how your meetings go.

**V1 — Guide:** This whole form works on an iPad. So you can sit across from your client, fill it in together, turn the tablet around, and have them **sign with their finger** right there. Signed, submitted, done, before you leave the parking lot.

**V2 — Rep:** *"That is better than emailing it and waiting."*

**V1 — Guide:** Considerably. A signature you get in the room is a signature. A form you email is a maybe.

**V1 — Guide:** A drawn signature on a tablet is always our preference. On a laptop with no touchscreen, use the **type** option instead — the client types their full legal name and that counts as their legal signature.

**V1 — Guide:** Either way, do not leave it thin. Full legal name, title, date. That is how everybody downstream knows who agreed to this.

**V1 — Guide:** Then submit. And that is a campaign.

## SEGMENT 5 — COMMON MISTAKES AND THE HANDOFF

**ON-SCREEN:** Each mistake appears as text with the real screen element that causes it, boxed in green. Then the handoff animates — *submit · Trello card · account manager · fulfillment*.

**V1 — Guide:** Five things go wrong. Four of them are easy to avoid.

**V1 — Guide:** **One, and this is the big one. Renaming a returning client.** Pick an existing client, then change their business name in that field, and you do not update them. You create a second version of them, with a second Trello list.

**V1 — Guide:** Now there are two of the same client and nobody knows which is real. If a name genuinely needs correcting, that happens in client profiles, not here. The form will even warn you. Believe it.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Guessing on an intake answer.** A guess reads exactly like a fact to the person building the campaign. Blank is better than wrong, and the help button is better than both.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Filling out the website intake form yourself.** Easy one to forget, because you will be filling in all the others. That one goes to your account manager.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four. Starting over instead of saving a draft.** People abandon a half-finished form and begin again from scratch two days later. The draft button is right there.

**V1 — Guide:** **Five. Skimming the review screen.** That last page is showing you exactly what is about to become a contract and a live campaign. Ten seconds of reading beats a correction later.

**V1 — Guide:** Now the handoff. When you submit, the client record is created, the Trello card is built on your group's board, and the account manager is notified. Copies go to your client and to both accounting departments. From there it goes to fulfillment.

**V1 — Guide:** And the Trello card is where the record lives. Months from now, if anybody asks what was sold and when, that card is the answer.

**V1 — Guide:** Your job after submitting is simple. Watch that card. If our team needs anything, they will ask on it.

## TRAINER NOTES *(not narrated)*

**Where this sits.** Block 9 of 11. Comes after the recommendation or proposal is accepted. Feeds into Intake Form Training (block 10) and Client Reporting (block 11), and routes all communication through Trello (block 4).

**The mistakes, with the symptom somebody would notice:**
| Mistake | What it looks like when it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Renaming a returning client | Two Trello lists for the same business. Nobody knows which is live. Updates land on the wrong one. |
| Guessing on intake | Campaign builds to the wrong specification. Discovered after launch. |
| AE filling the website intake | Kick-off call has to happen anyway, and now the form contradicts what the account manager captures. |
| Not saving a draft | Form abandoned and restarted from scratch. Deal slows for no reason. |
| Skimming the review screen | A typo becomes a contract and a live campaign. |

**Client-facing risk in this step.** The signature and agreement section is a contractual moment. An AE who clicks through the confirmations without saying anything to the client creates a problem that surfaces at the first invoice or the first cancellation request. **Terms are taught in separate compliance training** — this script points at that deliberately rather than half-teaching it.

**Who receives the IO.** On submission it goes to the client, your group's accounting department, and our accounting department — plus the Trello card, which is where the record lives long-term. Worth reinforcing with AEs, because it is the reason accuracy matters beyond fulfillment: two accounting departments bill from this document.

**Signature guidance.** A drawn signature captured in person on a tablet is the preference. The typed option is a legitimate fallback for laptops without touchscreens, and a typed full legal name is a legal signature. What is not acceptable is leaving the authorization fields thin — full legal name, title, and date should always be complete.

**Escalation.** If the form does not fit the situation, ask on the Trello card rather than working around the form. Workarounds create the duplicate-record problems above.

**Writer's notes:**
- Written from the live form structure. **Verify against the current build before recording** — field names and step labels will drift.
- **Terms, fees, and minimum commitments are deliberately excluded** per direction. If that changes, the agreement beat in segment three needs expanding rather than replacing.
- The old script's Step 2 and Step 3 resource-site navigation is gone entirely. The IO now lives at its own group link. **The Resources Section script needs updating to match**, since it currently sends people to a folder for a fillable PDF.
- Two things I could not confirm from the form skeleton and left out: whether an AE can reopen a submitted IO, and what happens if a service is added after submission. Both are questions an AE will ask.
- **Cancellations are processed through the same form and are deliberately not covered here** — tabled for separate treatment. When that is written, decide whether it belongs as a section in this block or as its own block, since the mechanics are the same but the conversation is completely different.
- **Distribution is taught but not quizzed.** Ten questions on the fixed weighting could not cover it without dropping something more behavioural. If distribution turns out to be a common misunderstanding, it is the natural swap for question ten.
