# Client Reporting — Process Script
**Pillar:** Process · Block 11 of 11
**Audience:** Account executives and agency staff — internal
**Format:** Motion graphics over real screen capture and real photography · AI voice read (two voices) · animated text · green callout boxes
**Runtime:** 1947 counted spoken words ≈ **12:59** at 150 wpm
**Spine — the one sentence somebody must repeat cold:** *The report is not the deliverable. The meeting is.*

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES — READ BEFORE RECORDING

**Two voices.** **V1 — Guide** carries the teaching. **V2 — Client or Rep** delivers the in-world line — something a client actually says, or the question a rep is thinking. Use a distinctly different ElevenLabs voice.

**Written for an AI read.** Short sentences. No semicolons, no parentheticals. Break lines shorter rather than rewriting longer if synthesis stumbles.

**The humor is recognition, not jokes.** Nothing depends on a pause or a dry delivery.

**Visuals.** Real report pages on screen with green callout boxes on the section being discussed. Real photography for the delivery beats — a rep across a desk, a printed report in somebody's hands. **No illustrations, no icon cards, no mascots.**

**No benchmark figures.** The script names what appears in a report and what to lead with. It never quotes a performance number, because those belong in a live report, not in training.

## SEGMENT 2 — WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

**ON-SCREEN:** Two paths build as text — *reported monthly, in person* and *emailed and never opened* — with a **green box** on the first. Then a real cancellation-style conversation photo.

**V1 — Guide:** So why does this get its own block? Because reporting is where the relationship gets built.

**V1 — Guide:** In our experience it is the single biggest factor in whether a client stays. Not the results. **The reporting.**

**V2 — Rep:** *"Not the results? That seems backwards."*

**V1 — Guide:** It sounds that way at first. But a client who understands what they are getting will stay through a quieter month, because you already talked about it. A client who has not heard from anybody in four months has nothing to judge by except a feeling.

**V1 — Guide:** So this is not a chore. It is a standing appointment with a client who wants to talk to you.

**V1 — Guide:** One thing worth saying now, because it saves you later. **Be careful not to oversell digital at the pitch.**

**V1 — Guide:** Digital is excellent. It is not magic, and it is not the only thing that moves a business. Every medium has strengths and weaknesses. Promise the moon in the sales meeting and every report afterward feels like a letdown, no matter how well the campaign performed.

**V1 — Guide:** Sell it honestly and your reports will look good for years.

## SEGMENT 3 — THE STEPS, WALKED THROUGH

**ON-SCREEN:** Four numbered steps build as text, then each gets real screen capture or photography.

**V1 — Guide:** Four steps. Get the report. Read it before they do. Deliver it in person. Then have the conversation.

### One — get the report

**ON-SCREEN:** Real screen capture of the resource section, into Reports, then market, then client. **Green box** following the path.

**V1 — Guide:** Reports live in your resource section under Reports, organized by market. One market means one folder. Several means pick yours, then pick your client.

**V1 — Guide:** Every month is there and available to download. And the timing is dependable. **Reports for the previous month are posted by the seventh.**

**V1 — Guide:** Now here is the approach we recommend, and it makes your life easier. **Do not wait for the report to arrive and then try to book a meeting.**

**V1 — Guide:** Set up a **standing appointment in the middle of every month** to review the month before. Same client, same slot, already on both calendars. You are not chasing anybody and they are expecting you.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So the meeting exists before the report does."*

**V1 — Guide:** Exactly. Report ready by the seventh, meeting mid-month, a week to prepare. Nobody is scrambling and nothing gets skipped because the calendar filled up.

**V1 — Guide:** And one thing worth knowing before we move on, because it comes up with bigger accounts. **Some clients want a live dashboard.**

**V1 — Guide:** Usually a larger advertiser who is used to logging in and looking at numbers whenever they want. And the answer is yes, we can do that. We have the technology, and there is **no additional fee**.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So I can offer that to anybody?"*

**V1 — Guide:** No, and this is the part to get right. It is **case by case, and only where it is warranted.** And it only works for the **programmatic products** — display, video, streaming, audio. A client buying search optimization or reputation management is not getting one, because that is not how those services produce data.

**V1 — Guide:** So do not promise one. **Ask your account manager.** If the budget and the appetite are there we will look at it and very often say yes. But that is a conversation to have with us first, not a commitment to make in the room.

### Two — read it before they do

**ON-SCREEN:** Real report pages turning, **green box** moving across each section as it is named.

**V1 — Guide:** Never walk into a report meeting without having read the report. Ten minutes is enough, and it is the difference between presenting and reacting.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is what you will find inside.

**V1 — Guide:** A **title page** with their name, logo, and dates. Small thing, but it looks like it was made for them, because it was.

**V1 — Guide:** An **overview and definitions page** with the snapshot and the terms explained. It exists because the words in this business are strange to normal people. There is no shame in reading a definition out loud.

**V1 — Guide:** Then **service pages**, one per tactic. That is where the detail lives — who was reached, what they did, what devices they used.

**V1 — Guide:** And finally, the **other services page.** We will come back to that one, because it is quietly the most valuable page in the document.

**V1 — Guide:** And use this. **If anything on that report raises a question, call your account manager.**

**V1 — Guide:** Not sure what a number means, how a tactic works, or how to explain something a client will ask. That is what your account manager is for, and they would much rather hear from you the day before than the day after.

**V2 — Rep:** *"What if it is my first one and I am just nervous about it?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Then tell us and we will get on the call with you. For your first few reports we are happy to join — either to prepare beforehand, or to be there while you deliver it.

**V1 — Guide:** To be clear, that is training support rather than a permanent arrangement. These are your relationships and you should own them. But nobody should figure out their first report meeting alone.

### Three — deliver it in person

**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a printed report being handed across a desk. Green box on the printed document.

**V1 — Guide:** Now the part that matters most, and the part most people skip.

**V1 — Guide:** **Print it. Take it to them. Sit down.**

**V2 — Rep:** *"Would it not be faster to just email it?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Much faster. And here is why we would ask you not to.

**V1 — Guide:** When a business moves their digital work over to us from somebody else, we ask why. And the answer is almost always the same two things. **The other provider emailed the report. Or they never sent one at all.**

**V1 — Guide:** That is it. Not price, not results. Nobody comes to us saying the campaigns were bad. They come because nobody ever sat down with them.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So that is how we win most of that business."*

**V1 — Guide:** It is. Which also means it is how somebody could win yours.

**V1 — Guide:** An emailed report gets opened by almost nobody. And when it is opened, the client sees numbers with nobody there to say which ones matter. So they scan it, feel unsure, and close it.

**V1 — Guide:** A printed report in your hand is a reason to be in the room — and that room is where you hear what is happening in their business, and where the next sale comes from.

**V1 — Guide:** Nobody ever stayed because of a well-designed PDF. They stay because of the person who brought it.

**V1 — Guide:** Same goes for a live dashboard. If a client has one, terrific — it does not replace the visit. A dashboard nobody explains is an emailed report in a fancier package. You still show up.

### Four — have the conversation

**ON-SCREEN:** Three questions build as large animated text — *How is business? How is traffic? How are sales?* Then the report appears beside them with a **green box** on the delivery figures.

**V1 — Guide:** Now the conversation. This is where people overcomplicate things, so let us keep it simple.

**V1 — Guide:** You already know how to do this. Think about talking to a client about a radio schedule in a market with no ratings, or a television buy when viewership is down. You do not open a spreadsheet. You ask how things are going.

**V1 — Guide:** Same three questions here. **How is business? How is traffic? How are sales?**

**V1 — Guide:** That is the meeting. Those questions, and then listening.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So what do I do with all the numbers?"*

**V1 — Guide:** You use them for one job. **To show that the campaign delivered what you promised it would deliver.**

**V1 — Guide:** That is the honest scope of a report. The campaign ran, it reached who it was supposed to reach, and it did what was sold. That is what you are accountable for.

**V1 — Guide:** What you are not accountable for is every factor in that business's month. Digital does not carry more responsibility than any other medium you sell — and nobody should treat it that way, including you.

**V1 — Guide:** So stay out of the weeds. If a client wants detail it is all right there. Most will not. Most want to know their money is doing something and somebody is paying attention.

**V1 — Guide:** And translate anything you do explain. If it is a word they would not use at their own dinner table, say it plainly.

**V1 — Guide:** **The Reports folder** in your resource section, organized by market and then by client. That is where every monthly report lives.

**V1 — Guide:** **A printer.** Genuinely. That is not a joke, it is the tool.

## SEGMENT 5 — WHERE THIS GOES WRONG

**ON-SCREEN:** Each mistake appears as text alongside the real report element it relates to.

**V1 — Guide:** Five mistakes. The first one is the most common thing in this entire business.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. Emailing it.** Still the most common one. Four seconds, and it buys you nothing.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Leading with impressions.** A business owner does not care how many times an ad appeared. Open where they care.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Using our vocabulary instead of theirs.** Every unexplained term is a small moment where a client feels lost, and people do not renew things that make them feel lost.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four. Going quiet on a slower month.**

**V1 — Guide:** It happens to every business. Sales go up, sales go down, some months are quieter. That is normal, not a crisis. What matters is that you still show up. Keep the standing meeting and have the same conversation.

**V1 — Guide:** One thing worth understanding. Occasionally a campaign comes in under its numbers, and there is usually a simple reason. When an audience is targeted very tightly, some months there is just less buyer intent out there to find. Not common, and not a mystery.

**V1 — Guide:** And it is not yours to handle alone. Our strategists review and refine campaigns daily, specifically so this does not land in your lap in front of a client. If a number looks short, raise it on the Trello card before your meeting and we will tell you what is going on.

**V1 — Guide:** A client who sees you show up in a quiet month will trust you for a long time. That is worth more than a good month.

**V1 — Guide:** **Five. Ignoring the other services page.** Which brings us to the handoff.

**ON-SCREEN:** Real report other-services page with a **green box** on it, then the Digital House graphic, then the pipeline looping back to the CNA block.

**V1 — Guide:** That last page lists what your agency does that this client is not buying yet. Every month, in person, in their hands.

**V1 — Guide:** So use it on purpose. And go back and look at their original digital audit — whatever category scored lowest is almost certainly on that page right now.

**V1 — Guide:** That is not a coincidence. It is a list of things you already proved they needed.

**V1 — Guide:** And the handoff loops all the way around. Reporting leads to renewal conversations, renewals lead to a fresh needs analysis because a year has passed and their business changed, and that starts the whole process again.

**V1 — Guide:** Not the end of the line. The top of the next lap.

## SEGMENT 6 — RECAP AND WHERE THIS LEADS

**ON-SCREEN:** Four steps recap as text. Then the full pipeline animates as a loop rather than a line, with a **green box** travelling around it.

**V1 — Guide:** Four steps. Set a standing meeting mid-month. Read the report before they do, and call your account manager if anything is unclear. Print it and take it to them. Then ask how business is, how traffic is, and how sales are — and use the numbers to show the campaign delivered what you promised.

**V1 — Guide:** And the line to keep. **The report is not the deliverable. The meeting is.**

**V1 — Guide:** That is the whole process, start to finish — and why it is a loop, not a line. Every report meeting begins the next conversation, the next needs analysis, and the next recommendation.

**V1 — Guide:** Take the quiz. And then go pull your reports, because it is probably past the seventh.

## TRAINER NOTES *(not narrated)*

**Where this sits.** Block 11 of 11, and the loop point. Reporting feeds renewal, which feeds a fresh CNA (block 6), which restarts the pipeline from there. Worth reinforcing that the process is a cycle rather than a finish line.

**The mistakes, with the symptom somebody would notice:**
| Mistake | What it looks like when it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Emailing the report | Client says they are "not sure it is working" despite good results |
| Leading with impressions | Client's eyes glaze, then asks what any of it means |
| Unexplained jargon | Client stops asking questions, which reads as agreement but is not |
| Going quiet on a bad month | Client notices the gap, and remembers it at renewal |
| Ignoring the other services page | Account never grows beyond what was originally sold |

**The other services page is a structured upsell mechanism.** A page listing tactics the client is not buying, delivered in person, every month. **Pair it with the original digital audit** — the lowest-scoring category from that audit is usually on that page, which means it is a documented need rather than a pitch. This is the strongest growth mechanic in the whole pillar and it is easy to overlook.

**On the bad-month coaching.** This is the highest-value content in the block. The instruction is: bring it up first, have a reason, have a change. **Never deliver a bad number without an adjustment attached.** An AE who does this well will hold accounts that others lose.

**Consistency with the tactic curriculum.** Every impression-based tactic module tells reps to agree the measure at the first meeting rather than defaulting to clicks — display, streaming TV, audio, geofencing all carry that pre-frame. **This is where that promise gets kept.** If either side is revised, check both.

**Live dashboards.** Available case by case, at no additional fee, for **programmatic products only** — display, video, streaming, audio. Not available for services like SEO or reputation management, where the data does not work that way. **Reps should never promise one; they should ask.** Two failure modes to watch: promising a dashboard that cannot be delivered, and not knowing the option exists at all and losing a large advertiser to somebody who offers it. Also worth reinforcing that a dashboard does not remove the monthly meeting — an unexplained dashboard is the emailed-report problem in a more expensive form.

**Suggested work product for this block.** A one-page report meeting agenda: the two or three outcomes to lead with, one thing being adjusted, one question about their business, and one item from the other services page. If an AE walks in with that filled out, the meeting works.
