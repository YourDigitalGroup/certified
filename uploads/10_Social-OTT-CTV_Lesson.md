# Social OTT/CTV Ads — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Targeted Digital Marketing (Room #17 in the Digital House)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1460 counted spoken words ≈ **9:44** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *Television didn't go away. It moved — and now it can be targeted.*

## SECTION A — The Reusable Tactic Lesson Template
*(Identical across all modules. Only the content changes — the beats never do.)*

**Voice convention:** **V1 — Guide** carries the teaching. **V2 — Scene** delivers only in-world lines and the quiz prompts.

**On-screen kit:** animated text · real photos and product screenshots · green callout boxes. **No line illustrations, icon cards, mascots, or drawn metaphors.** Spoken analogies stay spoken.

| # | Segment | Job |

## SECTION B — Social OTT/CTV: Full Script

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a family watching a smart TV in a living room. Then a real streaming-service ad break on a TV screen showing a local business's social-style video, with **green boxes** on the logo, the business name, and a QR code in the corner. Then a phone shown scanning that code and landing on the website.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Hang on — that's the builder from out on the highway. On Hulu?"*
**V1 — Guide:** Yes. And that reaction is worth a lot, because for a local business, being on television has always meant *you made it.*
**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: **Social OTT and CTV ads** are video ads that play on people's televisions during streaming shows and movies — built from the business's own social video, with their logo, name, and a QR code on screen.
**V1 — Guide:** Two acronyms, close enough to treat together. **OTT** is over-the-top — video delivered over the internet instead of a cable box. **CTV** is connected TV, the television it plays on. When a client says "streaming ads," this is it.
**V1 — Guide:** Now two things to get straight, because both trip reps up.
**V1 — Guide:** First, the name. We call it *Social* OTT because the **creative comes from their social video** — it keeps that authentic, social look instead of being a polished commercial. It does **not** run on social platforms. It runs on the television. If a client hears "social" and pictures Facebook, correct that immediately.
**V1 — Guide:** Second, and just as important: **this is a creative variation on our standard streaming TV product, not a different place to advertise.** Same streaming services, same televisions, same buying basis. The only real difference is what the ad looks like — a polished commercial in one case, their own social video in the other. Sell this one when the client has good social video and no commercial.
**V1 — Guide:** Like the rest of the family, it's bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis.** And here's what one impression means on this tactic specifically: **the ad played, all the way through, on one screen, during somebody's show.** Not a banner that loaded. A video that ran on a television.
**V1 — Guide:** And the big idea to carry: **television didn't go away. It moved — and now it can be targeted.**

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Three statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a real photo of a living room with several people watching one screen, **green box** around the group. Then text: *ad-supported is where the viewing is.*

**V1 — Guide:** So why should an advertiser do this? Three reasons.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: the television audience is already there.** Streaming is now close to half of all TV viewing — it passed broadcast and cable combined. About nine in ten American households have a connected TV device. This isn't a coming trend to sell somebody on. It's where the audience went, and most local businesses haven't followed.
**V1 — Guide:** And here's the number that answers the objection you'll get every time — "everybody pays extra to skip the ads." Roughly three-quarters of American homes have at least one ad-supported streaming service, and ad-supported viewing is still around three-quarters of all TV watching. The ads moved too.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: it makes a small business look like a big one.** A local builder running between innings of a streaming ballgame sits in the same break as national brands. There's no cheap seat on a television screen, and for a business trying to be taken seriously on a big purchase, that borrowed credibility is the product.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: unlike broadcast, this can be aimed.** A cable buy gets a program and everybody watching. This gets the households you chose — by who they are, what they've been doing online, even people who've visited the client's site. Same living room screen, digital targeting behind it.
**V1 — Guide:** One honest note on budget, and it protects you: television inventory costs more per thousand than a banner on a website. Considerably. That's not a markup, it's what a TV screen is worth. So set the expectation early that this is a presence tactic bought at a real budget, not a cheap add-on.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a new home under construction, a bank or medical office exterior, a business with existing video content on a phone screen. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.
**V1 — Guide:** **Big-ticket businesses that need to look established.** Home builders, remodelers, auto, medical practices, banks, attorneys. When somebody's about to spend serious money, they want the business to look permanent. Television does that better than anything else we sell.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses that already have video.** Social video, a walkthrough, an old TV commercial — they're most of the way there. And a radio spot's audio can become video, same as we covered on social ads.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses whose customers are families.** Anything decided in a living room — schools, vacations, vehicles, home improvement, dining. One television reaches everybody in the room, which almost nothing else we sell can do.
**V1 — Guide:** Honest flip side: no video and no budget for any means not ready. Somebody who needs the phone ringing this week should buy search ads. And a very tight service area may be too small to run efficiently — check before promising.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) A real vertical social video reshaped into a TV-format ad, with logo, name, and description appearing → *build it from what they have*. (2) A TV screen with a **green box** on the QR code, then a phone scanning it and landing on the site → *the QR code bridge*. (3) Targeting options building as text beside a map and schedule → *aim it like digital*. Closes on text: *Build it from their video · Bridge it with a QR code · Aim it like digital.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: build it from what they already have.** We take an existing social video and rebuild it as a television ad — their logo, their business name, a description, formatted for the screen.
**V1 — Guide:** And notice what we're *not* doing — producing a polished thirty-second commercial. It keeps the social look on purpose, because that's what people are used to watching and it reads as real rather than corporate. If a client has nothing, video can be produced. But most of them already have something usable and don't know it.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: the QR code — the mechanic that matters most.** The oldest problem in television advertising is that you cannot click a TV. For seventy years that was the tradeoff: big impact, no way to know what happened next.
**V1 — Guide:** The QR code fixes it. It sits on screen through the ad, somebody points their phone at it, and lands on the client's website — from the couch, in about four seconds. That's the difference between "we ran some TV" and "here's what the TV did." It's the strongest thing in this product.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: aim it like digital, not like TV.** A traditional television buy is a program and a time slot. Everybody who watches gets the ad, whether they'd ever buy or not.
**V1 — Guide:** This uses the same targeting as the rest of the display family — audience, behavioral, keyword and contextual, and retargeting — plus geography and a daily schedule. So a local builder isn't buying "everybody watching the game." They're buying homeowners in their service area who are watching the game.
**V1 — Guide:** Then the management: audience segments, ad set strategy, weekly refinement, reporting, and an option to measure on-site visits so a client can see whether viewers turned up in person.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on a TV screen ad, a streaming service's ad-supported tier, and a living room with several viewers.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.
**V1 — Guide:** **OTT and CTV** — over-the-top means video delivered over the internet instead of a cable box. Connected TV is the television it plays on. In practice, both mean streaming ads.
**V1 — Guide:** **Ad-supported streaming** — the tiers of streaming services that include commercials, usually cheaper or free. This is where these ads run, and it's most of the streaming audience.
**V1 — Guide:** **Non-skippable** — these ads generally can't be skipped past. That's the trade a viewer makes for a cheaper subscription, and it's why the ad actually gets watched.
**V1 — Guide:** **Co-viewing** — more than one person watching the same screen. It's normal on a television and almost unheard of on a phone, and it means one impression on a TV can reach a whole family.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *Look like an established brand · Reach the whole room · A measurable path off the TV · Built from video they already have.* Payoff: a real home-builder social video beside the same content as a TV ad with a QR code — plus a real photo of a new home. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? Existing social video converted into a television ad with logo, name, and description. A QR code linking to their site. Optional video production. Audience segments identified. The full targeting toolbox. A daily schedule. Ad set strategy and management with weekly refinement. Optional on-site visit measurement. And reporting.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? They **look like an established brand**, because there's no cheap seat on a television. They **reach the whole room** instead of one person on one phone. They get **a measurable path off the screen** through the QR code. And it's **built from video they already have.**
**V1 — Guide:** So picture a small home builder. Twelve houses a year, excellent work, and a social page full of walkthrough videos of finished kitchens that maybe three hundred people have ever seen.
**V1 — Guide:** After: that same kitchen walkthrough playing on streaming television, in front of homeowners in their three counties — with the company name, and a QR code that put the floor plans in somebody's hand while they were sitting on the couch.
**V2 — Scene:** *"My wife scanned the code during a show and we spent the rest of the night looking at your plans."*
**V1 — Guide:** — and notice all of it. Both of them in the room. Television doing the impressing. And a QR code turning a commercial into a conversation. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: build it from their video · 2: bridge it with a QR code · 3: aim it like digital. *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: build it from video they already have, keeping the social look on purpose. Two: the QR code — the first real answer to television's oldest problem, that you can't click a TV. Three: aim it like digital, with the same targeting as the rest of the display family.
**V1 — Guide:** And the two lines to carry in. **Television didn't go away — it moved, and now it can be targeted.** And when somebody tells you everybody skips the ads: about three-quarters of American homes have an ad-supported streaming service, and ad-supported viewing is still around three-quarters of all TV watching.
**V1 — Guide:** That's Social OTT and CTV. Quick ten-question check. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION D — AE Field Notes *(not narrated)*

**How the advertiser pays.** Bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis**. One impression means the video ad played on one screen during a viewer's show. On-site visit measurement is an optional add-on. Impression arithmetic is taught once, in Targeted Display.

**Budget expectations — set these early.** Streaming television inventory costs **considerably more per thousand** than web display. That's the nature of a TV screen, not a markup, but it means this is not a small add-on line — it needs a real budget to deliver meaningful presence. A rep who sells this at a display-sized budget will deliver a campaign nobody notices. Quote the model, never the rate, and be candid that TV costs more than banners.

**How it's sold.** Two openers work. For a client already doing social: *"You've got video that three hundred people have seen. Want it on television?"* For a client who has always wanted to be on TV but couldn't justify it: *"You can be on television now without a television budget — and unlike cable, you can pick who sees it."*

**Bundles with:**
- **Social media management and social ads** — the source of the video, and the reason there's anything to convert.
- **Social display** — same repurposed creative, two very different screens.
- **Targeted display and geofencing** — identical targeting toolbox and buying basis; often one program.
- **Website / landing pages** — the QR code has to land somewhere good. A campaign-specific page makes this dramatically stronger, and it's the natural attached sale.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"Everybody skips the ads now."* → "About three in four American homes have an ad-supported streaming service, and ad-supported viewing is still roughly three-quarters of all TV watching. And on those tiers the ads can't be skipped."
- *"I can't afford television."* → "You can't afford a cable schedule. This is different — but be straight with them that it costs more per thousand than banner advertising, so don't promise TV on a display budget."
- *"I don't have a commercial."* → "You've got video on your social page. That's the raw material. And if there's truly nothing, production is available."
- *"How would I ever know if it worked?"* → This is the best answer in the catalog. "The QR code. That's the first time television has been able to tell you."
- *"Isn't this just YouTube?"* → Different thing. This is the television in the living room during a show, not pre-roll on a phone.

**Lines for the room:**
- *"Television didn't go away. It moved — and now it can be targeted."*
- *"There's no cheap seat on a television screen."*
- *"For seventy years the problem with TV was that you couldn't click it. The QR code fixed that."*
- *"You're not buying everybody watching the game. You're buying homeowners in your service area who are watching the game."*
- *"One television reaches everybody in the room."*

**Retention note.** Two risks. First, the usual measurement problem — but here the QR code is the antidote, so make scans and site traffic the agreed measure from day one rather than clicks. Second, **budget mismatch**: an underfunded streaming campaign produces nothing visible, the client sees no result, and cancels. That failure is preventable at the first meeting by being honest about what this costs relative to display.

**Writer's notes:**
- **Statistics sources:** streaming approaching half of all US TV viewing and surpassing broadcast plus cable combined is from **Nielsen's The Gauge** (47.5% in December 2025); the ad-supported share of roughly three-quarters of TV viewing is from **Nielsen's Q1 2026 Ad Supported Gauge** (near 73%). Connected TV device penetration around nine in ten US households is reported across 2026 CTV compilations. The figure that about three-quarters of US homes have at least one ad-supported streaming service comes from 2024–2026 industry reporting. **Nielsen figures are the strongest sourcing in the 201 block — cite them by name if a rep is challenged.** Note that Nielsen is transitioning to a new measurement methodology (DASH) which may adjust reported streaming share; re-verify before recording.
- **Deliberately excluded:** the CPM figure, the on-site-visit add-on rate, the creative conversion fee, the optional video production fee, the monthly minimum, and the contract term. Completion-rate figures for CTV were also excluded as ad-performance metrics, though they're strong — the script conveys the same point through "non-skippable" instead.
- **The sales sheet lists compatibility with "all major platforms — Facebook, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, Pinterest, Snapchat, TikTok."** As with Social Display, this is read as referring to the *look* of the social creative being converted, not to where the ads run — these run on streaming television. Same confirmation request as Room #16.
- **Payoff vertical (home builder)** is new. Updated list: diner, roofer, flooring co., hearing clinic, insurance agency, storage co., garden center, medical clinic, auto repair shop, pest control co., RV dealership, used-car lot, physical therapy clinic, wedding/event venue, veterinary clinic, private school, clothing boutique, brewery/taproom, **home builder**.
