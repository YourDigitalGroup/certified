# SEO & AEO — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Online Visibility (Room #6 in the Digital House)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime target:** ~6.75 minutes + 10-question quiz *(15 sec over the standard 6:30 — the technical-cleanup beat in Segment 4 earns it; see note in Section B)*
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics.

## SECTION A — The Reusable Tactic Lesson Template
*(Use this exact shape for all ~25 tactic modules. Only the content changes — the beats never do.)*

Two questions before you write: **"What's the plain-English definition?"** and **"What are the 3 core mechanics?"** Everything else follows the beats.

**Voice convention (all modules):**
- **V1 — Guide:** the teaching spine. Carries every segment.
- **V2 — Scene:** short in-the-world lines only — a customer's search, a quoted reaction, a review snippet, and the quiz prompts. Its job is to signal a "mode switch" and keep the ear awake.
- *(Optional V3 later if you want a distinct quiz host — but two voices covers everything below.)*

**On-screen rule — the visual kit (all modules):** keep it clean and real. Three ingredients only:
1. **Animated text** — key words and short lines build/fade in as V1 speaks. This is the workhorse.
2. **Real photos** — actual screenshots of the product (e.g., a real search results page, a real AI answer on a phone) and real photography of people/businesses (a roofer on a ladder, a dental office, a shop owner at a laptop). No clip-art, no cartoon figures.
3. **Green highlight box** — a green callout box drawn over a real screenshot to point at a specific section (same style as the sales sheet: box the organic listings, box the AI answer, box the page title), with a text label animating in beside it.

**No cheesy line illustrations, icon/emoji cards, or metaphor graphics** (no glowing houses, ladder-climbing figures, robot mascots, trust meters). Any analogy the Guide speaks stays *spoken only* — we don't draw it. Quiz cards being tappable is UI, not illustration, so that stays.

## SECTION B — SEO & AEO: Full Script

> **Runtime note:** the beat table in Section A stays the standard for all modules. This tactic runs about 15 seconds long because Segment 4's technical checklist is read rapid-fire (five short lines, one on-screen line each). Segments 5–7 slide 15 seconds later to match. If a tighter cut is needed, the two easiest trims are the speed line's "fifteen years of add-ons" aside and the padlock line's second sentence.

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is *(0:00–0:45)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Real phone screenshot of a search results page for a typed-out question. The question *"do I need a new roof or just a repair"* animates into the search bar. A **green box** draws around the AI-written summary paragraph at the very top, label *AI answer*. The box then slides down to the list of unpaid website listings below it, label *organic results*. *(Photo option: brief real photo of a homeowner on a laptop at a kitchen table.)*

**V2 — Scene:** *"Do I need a new roof, or just a repair?"*
**V1 — Guide:** Somebody in your town types that into their phone at nine o'clock at night. Notice they didn't type two words — they typed a whole question, like they're talking to a person. And notice what came back. Up top, a paragraph that just… answers them. Under that, a list of websites. Nobody paid to be in either one of those spots. They earned it.
**V1 — Guide:** Earning those spots is **SEO** — Search Engine Optimization. In plain terms, it's the work of getting a business's website to show up in the free search results, the ones nobody paid for.
**V1 — Guide:** And that paragraph up top? That's the newer half of the job. **AEO** — Answer Engine Optimization — is making sure the business is the one the AI reads and repeats when it answers somebody's question.
**V1 — Guide:** Here's the way to hold it in your head. Think of the search engine as a librarian. SEO is making sure your book is on the shelf, labeled clearly, where she can find it. AEO is for the new librarian who doesn't hand you a stack of books anymore — she just tells you the answer out loud. You want to be the book she read.
**V1 — Guide:** Two acronyms, one job: be the business that comes up when somebody asks. And if this all sounds a little abstract right now — that's fine. It gets concrete fast.

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters *(0:45–1:30)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of someone on a phone or laptop at home (small-town/main-street feel). Then two real screenshots side by side — one results page where a local business appears in the top listings *and* gets named in the AI answer, one where it appears nowhere. A **green box** flags the presence on one and the absence on the other. Stakes text builds: *asked, not browsed → answered before clicked → rented vs. owned.*

**V1 — Guide:** Here's what's changed, and it changed fast. People don't browse anymore. They ask. And more and more, they get an answer without clicking anything at all.
**V1 — Guide:** So think about what that means for the shop on Main Street. If the answer at the top of the page names three roofers and yours isn't one of them, you didn't lose a bidding war. You never got invited. The customer was helped, decided, and moved on — and your website never even got a visit.
**V1 — Guide:** And here's the part that lands with owners. Ads are rent. The second you stop paying, you're gone. This is the other kind — the kind you own. It takes patient work, and it doesn't happen overnight, but the pages you build keep answering questions long after you built them.
**V1 — Guide:** So the stakes are pretty plain. Somebody in your town is asking a question your client could answer better than anybody. Right now, somebody else is answering it.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For *(1:30–2:15)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos appear one at a time — a roofer or HVAC tech at a home, a professional office setting (attorney, dentist, financial office), a clinic or dealership with a visible list of services. Each business type's name and its one-line reason type in beside the photo.

**V1 — Guide:** So who do you picture pitching this to? Three easy ones.
**V1 — Guide:** **Big-ticket service businesses** — roofers, HVAC, remodelers, med spas. Nobody spends eight thousand dollars off a whim. They research for days first, and every one of those days is a search. Land a few of those and it pays for a lot.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses people have questions about before they'll call** — attorneys, dentists, insurance, financial folks. "How much does this cost?" "How long does it take?" "Is it covered?" Every one of those questions can be its own page on the website. And every page is another door into the building.
**V1 — Guide:** **Multi-service or multi-location businesses** — clinics, dealerships, contractors with six different service lines and three towns. Each service and each town can have its own page, which means a whole lot of different ways to get found instead of just one.
**V1 — Guide:** Notice the thread. Anytime the customer starts with a question instead of a purchase, and takes more than five minutes to decide — that's an SEO and AEO business.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics *(2:15–4:15)*
**ON-SCREEN:** One real screenshot set on screen the whole time; a **green box** moves to the relevant piece for each mechanic, with its label animating in. (1) Green box around a page-speed/mobile view of the site → label *foundation*; then a five-line text checklist builds in beside it, one line at a time as V1 names each — *loads fast · works on a phone · nothing broken · every page findable · locked and secure*. (2) Green box around a page title and an FAQ block that matches the typed question → label *answers*. (3) Green box around another site's article linking to the business, then around the AI answer naming it → label *authority*. Closes on text: *Be readable · Be the answer · Be vouched for.*

**V1 — Guide:** How does this actually work? Three moving parts. Just three.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: the foundation — can the machines read you?** Before anybody ranks anywhere, a search engine has to be able to get into the website, read it, and read it fast. This is the unglamorous part. It's the library copy of your book with the cover glued shut — doesn't matter how good the writing is if nobody can open it.
**V1 — Guide:** And you don't need to know how any of it gets fixed. You just need to know the five things we look at, because these are the five things quietly holding a lot of small-business websites back.
**V1 — Guide:** **Does it load fast?** People bail on a slow site, and search engines know it. Usually the culprit is boring — giant photos straight off somebody's phone, or a website carrying fifteen years of add-ons it doesn't use anymore.
**V1 — Guide:** **Does it work on a phone?** Not "does it technically appear" — does it actually work. Buttons you can hit with a thumb, text you don't have to pinch. Most of these customers are on a phone, and a site built for a desktop in 2014 shows it.
**V1 — Guide:** **Is anything broken?** Dead links, pages that error out, photos that don't load, a contact form quietly going nowhere. Every one of those is a door that sticks.
**V1 — Guide:** **Can every page be found?** A site needs a clean index of its own pages — a little table of contents handed to the search engine, so nothing's stranded off in a corner where nobody links to it.
**V1 — Guide:** **Is it locked and secure?** The little padlock in the address bar. No padlock and some browsers throw up a warning before anybody even sees the homepage — which is a rough first handshake.
**V1 — Guide:** That's the technical cleanup. Nothing here wins an award. It's plumbing. But you cannot out-write a website nobody can open, and this is the part that gets skipped, because it's the part nobody can see.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: the answers — do your pages say what people are asking?** This is where the real work lives. You find the actual words and questions people type, then you make sure the website plainly answers them — in the page's headline, in the writing, in a straight-up Q&A section. One page, one question, answered like a human would answer it. Do that well and you get the bonus round: when an AI is looking for a clean answer to quote, a clean answer is exactly what it finds.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: the authority — does anybody else back you up?** Anybody can put "best plumber in town" on their own website. Search engines don't care what you say about yourself; they care who else says it. So links from other real websites, the business listed correctly in the places that list businesses, the same name and address and phone number everywhere, real reviews. That's the pile of evidence — and it's the same pile an AI digs through when it decides which business to name.
**V1 — Guide:** That's it. Be readable, be the answer, be vouched for.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms *(4:15–5:00)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and its one-line definition animate in as text, one at a time. For the three terms you can actually see on a results page (organic results, keyword, AI Overview), a **green box** highlights that element on the real screenshot as it's named. SEO/AEO and backlink are text-only, with the backlink shown as a green box on a linking sentence in another site's article.

**V1 — Guide:** Five terms so you sound like you know your stuff — because now you do.
**V1 — Guide:** **SEO and AEO** — Search Engine Optimization and Answer Engine Optimization. Earning the free spots in search, and earning your way into the AI's answer. Same work, two places it pays off.
**V1 — Guide:** **Organic results** — the unpaid listings. If it says "Sponsored," that's an ad. Everything else is organic, and that's the part we're earning.
**V1 — Guide:** **Keyword** — just the actual words somebody typed. Not a magic word you buy. A keyword can be one word or a whole sentence.
**V1 — Guide:** **Backlink** — a link from somebody else's website pointing to yours. Treat it as a vote of confidence. A few good ones beat a hundred junky ones.
**V1 — Guide:** **AI Overview** — the AI-written summary that shows up above the regular results. When people say "AI is eating search," that box is what they mean.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff *(5:00–6:15)*
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature names animate in as a text list (green box ticking to each area on the real screenshots as it's mentioned). Then four benefit lines build in as text: *Get found without paying per click · Get chosen as the expert · Get quoted by the AI · Keeps working after it's built.* For the payoff: two real roofing-company screenshots side by side — **before** (one thin page, no answers) and **after** (a page per service, a plain-English FAQ, the business named in the AI answer) — with a real photo of a contractor at a job site. A small text tag reads *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** So what's actually *in* this? Research first — the real questions and phrases people in that town are typing. Then the technical cleanup, and by now you know the list: trimming what's making the site slow, getting it right on a phone, hunting down the broken links and dead pages, keeping the site's own index of pages clean and current, and making sure the padlock's there. Ongoing, too — sites break on their own. Somebody adds a plugin, deletes a page, uploads a photo the size of a billboard, and something quietly stops working. Then the on-page work: page headlines and descriptions, the headings, the links between pages, even labeling the photos. Then content — a real page for each service, plus articles and Q&A pages that answer the questions people actually ask. Then the credibility side: getting the business listed and linked in the right places, with the name, address, and phone matching everywhere. And running through all of it, the AI layer — plain, quotable answers, plus little labels in the code called **schema markup**, which is just a way of telling search engines and AI "this is our address, these are our hours, this is the question and this is the answer" in a format they can't misread.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? Four things. They **get found** without paying for every single click. They **get chosen**, because the business that answers the question looks like the one that knows what it's doing. They **get quoted** — named inside the AI answer instead of skipped over. And the work **keeps paying** after it's built.
**V1 — Guide:** Under the hood, here's the nice simplicity of it: search engines and AI tools are both eating the same meal. Pages they can read, answers they can trust, other people vouching for you. Feed that, and you show up in both places.
**V1 — Guide:** So back to that roofer. Picture a small local roofing company. Before: one page on the website. A logo, a phone number, "Quality Roofing — Call Today." Nothing that answers a single thing a worried homeowner is actually wondering about. After: a page for each thing they do, a plain-English FAQ answering "how do I tell if hail actually damaged my roof," the site quick on a phone, listed and linked in the right places —
**V2 — Scene:** *"Your website answered my question, so I figured I'd just call you."*
**V1 — Guide:** — and that became a pretty common opening line on the phone. Nothing magic. They just stopped shouting and started answering. *(That's the kind of change to picture — an illustration, not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge *(6:15–6:45)*
**ON-SCREEN:** The three mechanics recap as animated text — 1: foundation · 2: answers · 3: authority — with the real screenshot behind them and the green box tapping each area one last time. A *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts, one more time. One: the foundation — fast, works on a phone, nothing broken, every page findable, padlock on. Two: the answers — pages that plainly answer what people are typing, which is exactly what an AI wants to quote. Three: the authority — other websites, listings, and reviews backing up the claim.
**V1 — Guide:** That's SEO and AEO — the room in the Digital House where you stop renting attention and start earning it. Quick ten-question check to lock it in. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION C — Quiz (10 Questions)
**ON-SCREEN:** One card per question, four tappable options; correct choice highlights green on select. **V2 — Scene** reads each question stem aloud.

**1.** Which of these is part of the technical cleanup on a website?
A) Writing more social media posts
B) Speeding up the site, fixing what's broken, making it work on a phone, and making sure every page can be found ✅
C) Raising the client's prices
D) Buying a higher spot in the search results



**2.** What's the best way to handle the questions customers ask before they buy?
A) Keep them off the website so people have to call
B) Answer them plainly on the website, in the page headline and a Q&A section ✅
C) Put them all on one page in tiny print
D) Only answer them in a monthly email

**3.** Two competing contractors have equally nice websites. One is linked to and listed by other credible sites; the other only talks about itself. Who's more likely to be named in search and in AI answers — and why?
A) The one that only talks about itself, because it controls the message
B) The one other sites back up, because outside evidence is what search engines and AI trust ✅
C) Neither — links and listings don't affect anything
D) Whichever one is older

**4.** What are "organic results"?
A) The listings marked "Sponsored"
B) The unpaid listings a business earns rather than buys ✅
C) A type of monthly subscription
D) Results that only show on phones

**5.** A "backlink" is…
A) A link from another website pointing to yours ✅
B) A link back to your own homepage
C) A broken link that needs fixing
D) A paid placement on a search page

**6.** The AI-written summary paragraph that appears above the regular search listings is called…
A) A sponsored result
B) An AI Overview ✅
C) A backlink
D) A landing page

**7.** Which business is an especially strong fit for SEO and AEO, and why?
A) A roadside stand that only takes cash from passing traffic
B) A local roofing company, because people research a big repair for days and type real questions the whole time ✅
C) A business with no website and no plans for one
D) A company that only sells to one existing customer

**8.** A homeowner asks their phone whether a cracked tooth needs a crown. The AI answers and names two local dentists. Your client's practice isn't one of them. What most likely happened?
A) The AI is broken
B) The patient got helped, picked from the two that were named, and never visited your client's site ✅
C) The patient will search again tomorrow
D) Nothing — the AI answer doesn't affect who gets called

**9.** Which of these is part of the work on an SEO and AEO campaign?
A) Cleaning up technical issues, optimizing pages, creating content that answers real questions, and building outside credibility ✅
B) Buying guaranteed placement at the top of Google
C) Deleting competitors' websites
D) Writing the AI's answers directly

**10.** In the roofing example, what actually changed?
A) They lowered their prices
B) They went from one page that said nothing to pages that plainly answered what homeowners were already asking ✅
C) They stopped answering the phone
D) They removed their website

**Answer key:** 1-B · 2-B · 3-B · 4-B · 5-A · 6-B · 7-B · 8-B · 9-A · 10-B
