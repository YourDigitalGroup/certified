# SEM — AE Field Notes

**How it's sold.** Rarely a first sale — it's the accelerator you attach once something exists to send traffic to. The natural sequence is website or landing pages first, then SEM. Selling ads into a weak site is the fastest way to create an unhappy client.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"I tried Google Ads and it didn't work."* → Ask two questions: did it point at your homepage, and were there negative keywords? Nearly every failed campaign fails on one of those. They didn't buy a bad tactic, they ran an unmanaged one.
- *"Why pay when I show up for free?"* → You don't show up for free at the top of the page, and free takes months. This is the fast lane while the earned work builds. Different jobs.
- *"It stops the day I stop paying."* → True, and say so plainly. This is rented attention, which is exactly why it belongs beside the things they own — the website and the earned rankings. Never argue this one; agreeing builds more credibility than dodging.
- *"I'll run it myself."* → The setup isn't the work. The weekly negatives, the schedule, the budget shifting — that's the work, and it's what nobody does after month two.

**Lines for the room:**
- *"Every other ad you buy interrupts somebody. This one shows up because they asked."*
- *"You pay nothing to be seen. You pay when someone chooses you."*
- *"If your ad lands on your homepage, you're paying for the click and then hiding the answer."*
- *"Relevance beats budget — you don't have to outspend the big guy, you have to be more specific than him."*

**Retention trap — pre-frame at the first meeting.** Clients judge SEM on clicks, then feel cheated when clicks don't feel like customers. Say it before they can: *"Clicks are the cost, not the result. What we'll look at together is calls and forms — that's what conversion tracking is for."* Setting the measure up front prevents the month-three argument.

**How the advertiser pays — say this early.** Search is bought **per click**. Being shown costs nothing; the advertiser owes only when somebody clicks. State the model, never the rate — and always contrast it with display, which is bought per thousand impressions and charges for the ad being seen. Reps who blur the two create unhappy clients by month two.

**The caution that goes with it:** the advertiser is paying for the *visit*, not the customer. What happens after the click decides whether it works, which is why landing pages aren't optional on this product.

**No budget arithmetic on this tactic.** Unlike impression products, search needs no math to be understood — everybody already knows what a click is. Walking through cost-per-click figures and assumed call rates adds risk (it implies a promised result) without adding clarity. Keep the model, skip the math.

**Evidence worth memorizing (two, not twenty):**
- **Close to half of all searches** are people looking for a local business or service.
- **A large majority of people who search for something nearby on a phone visit a business the same day,** and a meaningful share buy.

Both prove one idea, and it's the line: *the difference isn't the ad, it's the moment.* Answer "does this work?" with behavior, never with ad metrics.
