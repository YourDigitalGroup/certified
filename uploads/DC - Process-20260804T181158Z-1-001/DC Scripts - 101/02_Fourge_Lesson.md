# Fourge — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Online Visibility (Room #02 in the Digital House — the website platform)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1553 counted spoken words ≈ **10:21** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no tiers, no contract terms.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *Everything included, nothing to manage, and nobody else can sell it.*

## SECTION A — The Reusable Tactic Lesson Template
*(Identical across all modules. Only the content changes — the beats never do.)*

**Voice convention:** **V1 — Guide** carries the teaching. **V2 — Scene** delivers only in-world lines and the quiz prompts.

**On-screen kit:** animated text · real photos and product screenshots · green callout boxes. **No line illustrations, icon cards, mascots, or drawn metaphors.** Spoken analogies stay spoken.

| # | Segment | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook + What It Is** | Relatable moment → one-sentence definition |
| 2 | **Why It Matters** | Why an advertiser should do this + the evidence |
| 3 | **Who It's Great For** | 3 categories, one reason each + who it's wrong for |
| 4 | **How It Works — 3 Core Mechanics** | Exactly 3, roughly 4 beats each |
| 5 | **Common Terms** | Four to five terms maximum |
| 6 | **Features, Benefits & The Payoff** | Included → benefits → one payoff story |
| 7 | **Recap + Quiz Bridge** | Restate the 3 mechanics, warm handoff |

**Quiz blueprint:** Q1–3 the three mechanics · Q4–6 terms · Q7 fit · Q8 scenario · Q9–10 features/benefits. Vary the correct letter across A–D.

## SECTION B — Fourge: Full Script

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real phone screenshot of a slow site loading — spinner, blank space. Then a real Fourge site loading instantly beside it. **Green boxes** on both load states. Then real screenshots of several Fourge sites in sequence.

**V2 — Scene:** *"There's a stain on the carpet and I need somebody today."*

**V1 — Guide:** She has her phone out. She has found a local carpet cleaner. And she is now watching a blank white screen with a little spinning circle on it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three seconds from now she is gone, and that business will never know she existed.

**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: **Fourge** is our website platform. We built it, we own it, and it is designed to do one job — turn somebody who lands on a local business's website into somebody who contacts them.

**V1 — Guide:** And it is **fully managed**, which means the business owner never touches it. They never log into a dashboard, never update anything, never wonder whether the site is secure. They send us a change and we make it.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is the analogy, and it works for anyone who has sold anything with production attached. Most websites are like a brochure somebody printed once and left in a rack. Fourge is like having a salesperson at the front door who never goes home.

**V1 — Guide:** It is sold on a **monthly recurring basis** — one price, with the hosting, the security, and the support inside it.

**V1 — Guide:** And the line to carry: **everything included, nothing to manage, and nobody else can sell it.**

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Three statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a **green box** framing the gap between three seconds and 8.6 seconds. Then text: *the average website is failing by more than double.*

**V1 — Guide:** So why should an advertiser care? Four reasons, and the first one is the one that changes the conversation.

**V1 — Guide:** **One: the average website is losing people before it loads.** Around fifty-three percent of mobile visitors leave if a page takes more than three seconds. Three seconds is the line.

**V1 — Guide:** And the average website takes about **eight and a half seconds** on a phone. Not a bad website. The average one.

**V1 — Guide:** So most local businesses are more than twice as slow as the point where people give up. And they have no idea, because the owner looks at their own site on their own laptop on their own office wifi, where it is fine.

**V1 — Guide:** Three quarters of people say they have hit a site they considered too slow — it is the single most common complaint people have about the web, ahead of error messages and things being broken.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two: everything else you sell lands here.** Every search ad, every display campaign, every social ad, every listing you fix — all of it sends a human being to a website. If that website is slow or confusing, you are paying to deliver people into a bad experience.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three: the owner does not want to manage a website.** They never did. Somebody told them they needed one, they got one, and now it sits there aging while they run a business.

**V1 — Guide:** **And four — this one is for you rather than the client. Nobody else can sell this.** Fourge is ours. It is not a platform anybody can license or resell. So when you put this in front of a business, you are not one of four people in town offering the same thing at a different price. There is no comparison shopping, because there is nothing to compare it to.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Can I get this somewhere else?"*

**V1 — Guide:** No. And that is a genuinely comfortable position to sell from.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a service van, a professional office, a business with an obviously dated site on a phone. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses somebody needs right now.** Carpet cleaning, plumbing, HVAC, locksmiths, towing, urgent medical. When somebody needs help today, they are on a phone and they are impatient. Speed is not a nice-to-have for these businesses. It is the whole thing.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses selling trust.** Legal, dental, healthcare, financial, insurance, funeral homes. A dated site quietly says the business is not doing well, whether or not that is true. This is the fastest way to look as good as they actually are.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses whose site has not been touched in years.** The single easiest qualifying question you can ask: *"when was the last time anything changed on your website?"* If the answer is a shrug, you have a conversation.

**V1 — Guide:** And the honest flip side: a business with a genuinely good, fast, well-maintained site does not need this, and saying so is how you keep the account. Also, if somebody wants to build and control their own site as a hobby, this is not for them — the whole point is that they never touch it.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) A real Fourge site loading instantly, with structure highlighted → *built for one job*. (2) A list of included capabilities animating in with **green boxes** → *everything is in it*. (3) A real photo of a person at a desk making a change, then a phone showing a request being sent → *we do the work*. Closes on text: *One job · Everything in it · We manage it.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts.

**V1 — Guide:** **One: it is built for one job.** This is not a general-purpose website platform with a local business template dropped on top. It was designed from the beginning to turn visitors into contacts.

**V1 — Guide:** Which shows up in ways an owner will notice. It loads fast because speed was engineered in at the platform level, not bolted on afterwards. It is structured so somebody can find what they need and get in touch without hunting. And security is enterprise-grade and built in, not something anybody has to maintain.

**V1 — Guide:** And every site is **hand-crafted** by real people, built around that specific business and its market. Not a template with a logo swapped in, and not generated.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two: everything is in it.** This is the mechanic that makes the sale, so learn the list.

**V1 — Guide:** **Search and answer engine optimisation**, built in from launch. An **AI chatbot** that answers visitors and captures leads around the clock, including at midnight. **Accessibility** built to standard by default, which reduces legal exposure. **Targeted landing pages**, included, built to convert. Plus hosting, security, and analytics set up properly from day one.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Wait — all of that's in the website?"*

**V1 — Guide:** All of it. And that reaction is the sale. Because we sell most of those things separately, and a client who priced them individually would be looking at four conversations instead of one line.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three: we manage it, and that is not a small thing.** The client never logs into a dashboard. They send us a change and we make it. We monitor the site, we maintain it, and if something breaks they call us and we fix it — they are never left troubleshooting.

**V1 — Guide:** And the team doing that is entirely US-based. Design, build, and support.

**V1 — Guide:** One more thing worth knowing: **we do the heavy lifting on content too.** We gather what we need from them — their services, their story, what makes them different — and build the rest. The most common reason a business never replaces its website is that nobody wants to write it. That excuse is gone.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on the relevant part of a real Fourge site.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.

**V1 — Guide:** **Proprietary platform** — a platform a company built and owns, rather than one it licensed from somebody else. That is what Fourge is, and it is why nobody else in your market can offer it.

**V1 — Guide:** **Fully managed** — every update, change, and fix is handled by us. The client's job is to tell us what they want. That is the whole job.

**V1 — Guide:** **AEO** — answer engine optimisation. Traditional optimisation gets a business found in search results. This is about getting them **cited by AI assistants** when somebody asks one a question. It is newer, it is growing fast, and most local businesses have never heard of it.

**V1 — Guide:** **Brochure site** — a website that exists, looks respectable, and does nothing. It describes the business and provides no obvious next step. That is what most local businesses have, and it is the thing you are replacing.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *Fast enough to keep the visitor · Everything included in one price · Nothing for them to manage · Nobody else can sell it.* Payoff: a real carpet cleaning van photo beside a fast-loading site on a phone with a call button. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? A hand-built site designed around that business. Engineered speed and enterprise-grade security. Search and answer engine optimisation from launch. An AI chatbot capturing leads around the clock. Accessibility built to standard. Targeted landing pages. Hosting, SSL, and analytics set up properly. Content built with them rather than demanded from them. Full ongoing management by a US-based team. And e-commerce where a business needs it.

**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? A site that is **fast enough to keep the visitor** they already paid to attract. **Everything in one price**, with nothing appearing on a bill later. **Nothing to manage**, ever. And something **nobody else in their market can offer them**, which cuts both ways nicely.

**V1 — Guide:** So picture a carpet cleaning company. Two vans, excellent work, been going eleven years. Their website was built by somebody's cousin in about 2016 and it takes the best part of ten seconds to open on a phone.

**V1 — Guide:** And here is the thing the owner never saw. Their customers are people with a stain on the carpet, standing in a living room, on a phone, right now. Every one of those people was leaving before the site opened.

**V1 — Guide:** After: a site that opens instantly, a call button at the top of every page, a page for each service and each town they cover, and a chatbot answering at nine at night when somebody spills something.

**V2 — Scene:** *"You were the first one that actually loaded."*

**V1 — Guide:** — and that is the whole product in one sentence. They did not win that job on price, or on reputation, or on being the best in town. They won it by opening. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap + Quiz Bridge
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: built for one job · 2: everything in it · 3: we manage it. *Quiz →* button slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: built for one job — engineered speed, real structure, hand-crafted per business. Two: everything is in it — search and answer optimisation, the chatbot, accessibility, landing pages, hosting, security. Three: we manage all of it, so the client never touches a dashboard.

**V1 — Guide:** And the line to carry in: **everything included, nothing to manage, and nobody else can sell it.**

**V1 — Guide:** Plus the qualifying question that opens every one of these conversations: *"when was the last time anything changed on your website?"*

**V1 — Guide:** That's Fourge. Quick ten-question check. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION D — AE Field Notes *(not narrated)*

**How the advertiser pays.** **Monthly recurring**, with hosting, security, and support inside the price. Tiers are based on how much site the business needs. **Rates, tier definitions, and any minimum terms stay on the rate card and out of the script** — quote them from current collateral, never from memory.

**⚠️ The exclusivity argument is the one reps under-use.** Fourge is proprietary. **No competitor in your market can sell it**, and a client cannot price-shop it. That does two things: it removes the comparison conversation entirely, and it means you are never the fourth person that month offering the same website at a different number. Say it plainly — *"you can't get this anywhere else"* — and let it sit.

**The qualifying question that opens everything:** *"When was the last time anything changed on your website?"* A shrug is a qualified prospect. Nothing else you ask will sort prospects faster.

**The demo is the pitch.** Pull up their site on your phone alongside a real Fourge site. Do not describe the speed difference — show it. Then hand them the phone.

**Bundles with — and here is where care is needed:**
- **Search ads, display, social, and every traffic tactic** — all of them land on a website. This is the argument for fixing the site first, and it is genuine: paying to send people to a slow site is spending a client's money badly.
- **Google Business Profile and local listings** — the foundation pair. Fix where they are found and where they land.
- **⚠️ Accessibility, SEO/AEO, targeted landing pages, and the AI chatbot are all *included* in Fourge.** Before selling any of those as a standalone, check whether the client is on Fourge. **Selling somebody something they already have is the fastest way to lose their trust.**

**Objections and responses:**
- *"My website's fine, I looked at it this morning."* → "On a laptop, on your office wifi. Let's look at it on my phone, on mobile data, the way your customer does." Then show them.
- *"I'd rather buy a site once than pay monthly."* → "You can. Then you own the hosting, the security, the updates, and finding somebody when it breaks at eight on a Sunday. This is one number and none of that."
- *"Can I make my own changes?"* → "You can ask for any change you want and we make it, usually faster than you'd manage it yourself at eleven at night. But no, you won't have a dashboard — that's the point."
- *"Is it a template?"* → "No. Designed and built from the ground up for your business, by people. Not a template, not generated."
- *"Can I get this somewhere else?"* → "No. We built it. That's the honest answer and it's a good one."
- *"I don't have time to write all the content."* → "You don't have to. We gather what we need from you and build the rest."

**Lines for the room:**
- *"Everything included, nothing to manage, and nobody else can sell it."*
- *"When was the last time anything changed on your website?"*
- *"Three seconds is where people give up. The average site takes over eight."*
- *"You're looking at it on a laptop. Your customer is standing in a living room on a phone."*
- *"You already paid to get those people to your website. This is about what happens when they arrive."*

**Retention note.** This is a recurring product, which makes retention the whole business. The strongest protection is that **the client experiences the management** — every change they request and we make is a reason to stay. If a client goes six months without asking for anything, that is a quiet risk, not a success. **Prompt them: a seasonal update, a new service page, fresh photos.** A site that visibly changes is a site nobody cancels.

**Writer's notes:**
- **Statistics sources:** 53% of mobile visitors abandoning after three seconds originates with **Google Consumer Insights** and is very widely reproduced. The average mobile load time of roughly 8.6 seconds (against 2.5 on desktop) comes from 2026 load-time analyses of leading sites. The finding that around three quarters of mobile users have encountered a site they considered too slow — the most common web frustration — appears across 2026 speed compilations. **Attribute as "research shows" and re-verify annually.** All three prove one idea: *the average website is more than twice as slow as the point where people leave.*
- **Deliberately excluded:** all pricing, the tier page counts, minimum terms, and every conversion-rate figure from the speed research. Conversion-rate data is abundant here and persuasive, but it reads as a promised result. The behavioural figures make the same point without the exposure.
- **⚠️ Never name a competing platform.** The comparison is available and strong, but it is made by category — "a general-purpose builder," "a DIY platform" — not by brand.

  - **Room 03 Accessibility · Room 06 SEO/AEO · Room 07 Targeted Landing Pages · the AI Chatbot module** all teach products that Fourge includes. **Each needs a line clarifying standalone versus inclusion.**
