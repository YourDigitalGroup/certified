**Pillar:** Targeted Digital Display (Room #26a in the Digital House — the roof)

## SECTION A — The Reusable Tactic Lesson Template
*(Identical across all modules. Only the content changes — the beats never do.)*

**Voice convention:** **V1 — Guide** carries the teaching. **V2 — Scene** delivers only in-world lines and the quiz prompts.

**On-screen kit:** animated text · real photos and product screenshots · green callout boxes. **No line illustrations, icon cards, mascots, or drawn metaphors.** Spoken analogies stay spoken.

| # | Segment | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook + What It Is** | Relatable moment → one-sentence definition |
| 2 | **Why It Matters** | Why an advertiser should do this + the evidence |
| 3 | **Who It's Great For** | 3 categories, one reason each + who it's wrong for |

| 5 | **Common Terms** | Four to five terms maximum |

## SECTION B — Netflix Video Ads: Full Script

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a family on a sofa, television on. Then a real screenshot of a full-screen video ad playing on a TV during a program. **Green box** on the screen. Then real Netflix interface screenshots — series, films, a live event.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Did you watch it? Everybody at work was talking about it."*

**V1 — Guide:** That is the thing about Netflix. It is not just something people watch. It is the thing people talk about on Monday morning.

**V1 — Guide:** And for a long time, a local business could not get anywhere near it. There were no ads to buy.

**V1 — Guide:** That changed. So, plainly: **Netflix Video Ads** put your client's commercial inside Netflix programming — the series, the films, and now the live events — playing on the television, and on phones, tablets, and computers too.

**V1 — Guide:** Here is the analogy, and it will land with anyone who has sold television. This is a prime-time spot in the most-watched living room in the country. Except instead of buying a daypart and hoping, you are choosing who sees it.

**V1 — Guide:** And here is the part that makes this different from every other kind of advertising you sell. **These viewers chose to see ads.**

**V1 — Guide:** Netflix offers a cheaper plan, and the trade is that it carries advertising. So nobody on that plan was ambushed. They made a deal, and they know what the deal was.

**V1 — Guide:** Which is the line to carry: **they picked the plan with ads. You're not interrupting anybody.**

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Three statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a comparison bar — *four to five minutes an hour* against *twelve to sixteen minutes an hour.* **Green box** on the first.

**V1 — Guide:** So why should an advertiser care about this specifically? Three reasons.

**V1 — Guide:** **One: the audience is enormous, and it got that way fast.** Netflix's ad-supported tier reaches more than **two hundred and fifty million monthly viewers worldwide** — up from around seventy million in 2024. That is one of the fastest audience builds in the history of advertising.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two: they are not casual about it.** More than **eight in ten** of those members watch every single week. Not occasionally. Weekly.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three, and this is the one that surprises people: most new members are choosing this plan.** In the countries where it is offered, the ad-supported plan accounts for **more than sixty percent of new sign-ups.**

**V1 — Guide:** Put those together and you get the idea worth carrying: **this is not a leftover audience. It is the plan most people are picking, and they are watching it constantly.**

**V1 — Guide:** And one more thing, which every one of you will appreciate because you have sold television or radio. **The ad load is light.** Somewhere around four to five minutes of advertising an hour, against the twelve to sixteen minutes that is normal on traditional television.

**V2 — Scene:** *"So there's less to hide behind."*

**V1 — Guide:** Exactly. Fewer spots in the break means your client's commercial is not the ninth thing somebody has sat through. That is worth saying out loud in a meeting.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a med spa reception, a home improvement showroom, a family watching TV with no cable box visible. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses where image does a lot of the selling.** Med spas, dental practices, home improvement, financial advisors, jewellers, higher-end services. Premium content rubs off. A business that looks good next to a well-made show looks good, full stop.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses that already have a commercial.** If they run television, or you have turned their radio spot into video, the creative is done. This is the easiest incremental buy in the whole catalogue for those clients.

**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses trying to reach the households cable gave up on.** These viewers largely do not have a cable box. If your client has been buying broadcast for twenty years and wondering where the younger households went, they went here.

**V1 — Guide:** And the honest flip side: **this is a premium buy, so a very small budget spreads too thin.** Better to own a smaller audience properly than to appear once in front of everybody. Also, if a client wants to count leads by Friday, set expectations — this builds demand rather than harvesting it. **Pair it with something that captures.**

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) Real screenshots — a series, a film, a live event — with a **green box** on each → *where it plays*. (2) Targeting options animating in → *who sees it*. (3) A full-screen ad playing with no skip control visible → *why it lands*. Closes on text: *Premium content · Chosen audience · Nowhere to go.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts.

**V1 — Guide:** **One: where the ad plays.** Inside Netflix programming — the series, the films, and increasingly the **live events**, which is newer and worth knowing about because live is where the biggest single audiences show up.

**V1 — Guide:** It runs primarily on the **television**, which is where most Netflix viewing happens. But it also reaches phones, tablets, and computers, so a client is not confined to one screen.

**V1 — Guide:** And every one of those environments is **brand-safe.** This is professionally produced, award-winning programming. There is no user-generated content, no comments section, and no risk of your client's commercial turning up beside something they would be embarrassed by.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two: who sees it.** You are not buying a time slot. You are buying an audience.

**V1 — Guide:** There is **demographic targeting** — the basics, like age and household characteristics. And there is **behavioural targeting**, which uses aggregated groups built from viewing patterns and interests rather than anything about a named person.

**V1 — Guide:** Say it that way if a client asks. *"Netflix builds audience groups based on the kinds of things people watch. Your ad goes to the group that matches your customer."* That is accurate, and it is enough.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three: why it lands differently.** The ad is **full-screen and non-skippable.** There is no five-second countdown, no skip button, and nothing to scroll past. The commercial plays start to finish.

**V1 — Guide:** Hold onto that. It is the biggest selling feature in this module and we come back to it. Almost nothing else in digital video can promise it.

**V1 — Guide:** Combine it with a light ad load and an audience that opted in, and you have something unusual: an ad that plays all the way through, to somebody who is not annoyed about it.

**V1 — Guide:** And how the client pays for it: **on a cost-per-thousand impression basis**, like the other video products you sell. One impression here means one video ad played during a program.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on the relevant real screenshot.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.

**V1 — Guide:** **Ad-supported tier** — the cheaper subscription plan that carries advertising. It is the plan your client's ads run inside, and it is the one most new members now choose.

**V1 — Guide:** **Non-skippable** — the viewer cannot skip past it. The whole commercial plays. Straightforward, and a genuine advantage over anything with a skip button.

**V1 — Guide:** **Behavioural targeting** — reaching aggregated audience groups built from what people watch and what they are interested in. **Groups, not individuals** — that distinction matters and you should keep it in your language.

**V1 — Guide:** **Brand safety** — the assurance that a client's ad appears beside professionally produced content and nothing unpredictable. Some clients will not know the term. All of them care about the idea.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *Premium content · A chosen audience · The whole spot plays · Every screen.* Payoff: real med spa interior photo beside a TV playing a commercial. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? Full-screen, non-skippable video inside premium Netflix programming, including live events. Demographic and behavioural audience targeting. Brand-safe, award-winning content. Reach across television, mobile, desktop, and tablet. And a light ad load, so there is far less competing for attention than in a traditional break.

**V1 — Guide:** What does the advertiser get? **Premium company** for their brand. **A chosen audience** rather than a hopeful one. **The whole spot playing**, every time. And **every screen** the household uses.

**V1 — Guide:** So picture a med spa. Good practitioners, lovely space, and a real problem: everybody they want to reach has heard of them exactly never, and their whole category runs on trust and looking established.

**V1 — Guide:** Before: social ads that reached people scrolling past in three seconds, and a radio schedule that described a visual business with no pictures.

**V1 — Guide:** After: their commercial, full screen, in the living rooms of households matched to their customer profile, sitting inside programming those households actually chose to watch. Same commercial they already had.

**V2 — Scene:** *"A woman came in and said she'd seen us on Netflix. She said it like we were a real company."*

**V1 — Guide:** — and that is the thing you cannot buy anywhere else. Not just the reach. **The company you keep.** *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: premium content, every screen · 2: demographic and behavioural targeting · 3: full-screen, non-skippable.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: it plays inside premium series, films, and live events, mostly on the television but across every screen, and it is brand-safe throughout. Two: you aim it with demographic and behavioural targeting — audience groups, not individuals. Three: it is full-screen and non-skippable, with a light ad load, sold on a cost-per-thousand impression basis.

**V1 — Guide:** And the line to carry in: **they picked the plan with ads. You're not interrupting anybody.**

### SEGMENT 8 — Into the Room
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a rep walking into a business. Each objection appears as animated text as V2 speaks it, response beneath. Then a three-way comparison builds — *skip in five seconds · scrolled past in two · plays all the way through* — with a **green box** on the third. Then the three sentences in green boxes, held long enough to photograph. Then *Quiz →* slides up.

**V1 — Guide:** Before the quiz, let's get you ready for the meeting itself. Two questions you are going to be asked, the argument I would lead with, and one thing to settle before you leave the room.

**V1 — Guide:** First, the objection you will hear most often.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Nobody watches the ads on Netflix."*

**V1 — Guide:** Two hundred and fifty million people are on the plan that carries them, and more than eight in ten watch every single week. And nobody was put on that plan against their will — most new members choose it, because it costs less.

**V1 — Guide:** So the honest answer is short. They watch, and they knew what they were agreeing to.

**V1 — Guide:** Second, the one that will catch you out if you have not thought about it in advance.

**V2 — Scene:** *"How will I know if it worked?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Do not oversell here. This builds demand rather than harvesting it, and a client expecting the phone to ring on Tuesday will be disappointed by the first report.

**V1 — Guide:** So agree what success looks like before it runs, and put something alongside it that captures the demand — a search campaign, a landing page, retargeting. Then something in that report moves.

**V1 — Guide:** Now the argument I would actually lead with, because it is the one your clients will understand fastest.

**V1 — Guide:** **Nobody can skip it.**

**V1 — Guide:** Think about what has happened to this business. You used to sell a thirty-second spot and it played — all thirty seconds, every time, whether the viewer was thrilled about it or not.

**V1 — Guide:** Then digital arrived and everything became escapable. Five seconds on a video platform and they are gone. Two seconds in a feed and they have scrolled past. You started selling exposure instead of a spot.

**V1 — Guide:** This is different. **Full screen, on the television, and there is no skip button.** The whole commercial plays, start to finish.

**V1 — Guide:** So for any client who has bought television or radio, this is the closest thing in digital to the product they already trust. That is not a footnote. **Open with it.**

**V1 — Guide:** Three sentences worth knowing word for word. To frame the buy: **"You're not buying a time slot, you're buying an audience."** For the client worried about interrupting people: **"Four or five minutes an hour. On regular television it's twelve to sixteen."** And to close: **"Nobody can skip it, nobody can scroll past it, and it fills the screen."**

**V1 — Guide:** And the one thing to settle before you leave. This is premium inventory, so a modest budget spread across everybody accomplishes nothing. Say plainly that you would rather own a smaller audience properly than appear once in front of the whole county. Have that conversation at the first meeting and the second meeting goes well.

**V1 — Guide:** That's Netflix Video Ads. Quick ten-question check. No pressure — you already know this.

## SECTION D — Trainer & Writer Reference *(not narrated)*

> **Note on structure.** The material reps most need — the two live objections, the two lines for the room, and the budget pre-frame — is now **narrated in Segment 8**, immediately before the quiz. This section holds everything that does not belong on camera: the full objection set, product-family reference, sources, and open confirmations.

**How the advertiser pays.** Impression-based, on a **cost-per-thousand basis.** One impression is one video ad played during a program. **The full impression arithmetic is taught once, in the Targeted Display module** — do not repeat it here. **Never quote a rate.**

**⚠️ Where this sits in the video family — reps confuse these constantly:**
| Product | What the client is buying |
|---|---|
| **The broad streaming buy** | Streaming television across a range of services |
| **Netflix** *(this module)* | Netflix specifically — premium series, films, and live events |
| **Hulu** | Hulu specifically |
| **Prime Video** | Prime Video specifically, with purchase-behaviour targeting |
| **YouTube TV** | The live pay-TV service — local channels and live sport |
| **YouTube video ads** | The YouTube platform, not television |

**Netflix's distinctive argument versus its siblings.** Prime Video's edge is purchase behaviour. Hulu's edge is the specific show. **Netflix's edge is the opt-in audience and the light ad load** — most new members are choosing this plan, and there are roughly four to five minutes of advertising an hour against the twelve to sixteen normal on traditional TV. That combination does not exist anywhere else you sell.

**How it's sold.** Best as part of a video or streaming conversation rather than alone. Natural pairings: any client already running television, anyone whose radio spot has been converted to video, and any foundation-first client who has reached the roof of the Digital House.

**Budget warning — say this at the first meeting.** This is premium inventory. **An underfunded buy spread across a wide audience achieves nothing** — the same warning that applies to YouTube TV and Prime Video. Better to own a tighter audience properly.

**Objections — the full set.** The two marked **★ are narrated in Segment 8.** The rest are here as a reference sheet for trainers and for the rep card.
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| ★ *"Nobody watches the ads on Netflix."* | 250 million are on that plan and 80%-plus watch weekly. Nobody was forced onto it |
| ★ *"How will I know if it worked?"* | It builds demand rather than harvesting it. Agree the measure up front and pair it with a capture tactic |
| *"I don't want to interrupt people's evening."* | They chose the cheaper plan knowing ads came with it — and it's four or five minutes an hour, not fifteen |

**⚠️ Non-skippable is the strongest argument in this module and it is easy to under-sell.** Set up as a mechanic in Segment 4, then sold properly in Segment 8 with the comparison that matters to this audience: a thirty-second television spot always played in full; then digital made everything escapable — five seconds on a video platform, two seconds in a feed. **This is the closest thing in digital video to the product a traditional seller already trusts.** Reps should open with it, not save it.

**Retention note.** A brand and demand product, so month two feels quiet if it was oversold as direct response. **The pre-frame is narrated in Segment 8** — agree what success looks like before the first report, and pair it with a capture tactic so something in the report moves.
