# Trello Basics — Process Script
**Pillar:** Process · Block 4 of 11
**Audience:** Account executives and agency staff — internal
**Format:** Motion graphics over real screen capture · AI voice read (two voices) · animated text · green callout boxes
**Runtime:** 1232 counted spoken words ≈ **8:13** at 150 wpm
**Spine — the one sentence somebody must repeat cold:** *If it is not on the card, it did not happen.*

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES — READ BEFORE RECORDING

**Two voices.** **V1 — Guide** carries all teaching. **V2 — Rep** asks what the viewer is thinking. Distinctly different ElevenLabs voice.

**Written for an AI read.** Short sentences. No semicolons, no parentheticals. Break lines shorter rather than rewriting longer.

**The humour is recognition, not jokes.** Nothing depends on a pause or a dry delivery.

**Visuals: this block lives on screen capture.** Every section named should be visible with a **green box** on it. A viewer should be able to follow along in a second window. **No illustrations, no icon cards, no mascots.**

**The biggest change from the old script.** The original ground rules were a list of things not to do. **Every one of them is now a consequence**, because a rule without a reason gets broken the first time somebody is busy.

## SEGMENT 2 — WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

**ON-SCREEN:** Two paths build as text — *on the card* versus *in somebody's inbox* — with a **green box** on the first. Then a real card with a comment thread visible.

**V1 — Guide:** So why does it matter where a conversation happens?

**V1 — Guide:** Because of what happens when it happens somewhere else. You email a question to the person you spoke to last time. They are out for two days. Nobody else can see it. Your campaign sits still, and neither of you knows the other is waiting.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So it is not that email is banned. It is that nobody else can see it."*

**V1 — Guide:** That is exactly it. A comment on the card is visible to whoever picks it up. An email is visible to one person who may be on holiday.

**V1 — Guide:** And there is a second reason, which you will appreciate about eight months from now. **The board is the record.** When somebody asks what was approved, when it went live, or what the client said in March, the answer is on the card. Nobody has to go digging through a mailbox.

## SEGMENT 3 — THE WALKTHROUGH

**ON-SCREEN:** Real screen capture throughout, following the path being described, **green box** on each element as it is named.

**V1 — Guide:** Three things. Get in, change one setting, then learn four sections.

### Getting in

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on an invitation email, then the accept screen, then the login setup.

**V1 — Guide:** When your group comes on board, you get an invitation to join your Trello board. It arrives by email, and it will come from either **Trello** or **Atlassian**, which owns Trello. Both are legitimate. Neither is spam.

**V1 — Guide:** Click the link, accept the invitation, and set up your login. One thing to get right: **use the same email address the invitation was sent to.** Sign up with a different one and you will end up with an account that cannot see your board.

**V1 — Guide:** After that, there are three ways back in. The **Trello link** on your resource site, typing **trello dot com** straight into a browser, or the **mobile app.** Get the app. Most of the time you will want this while you are between meetings, not at a desk.

### Change one setting

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box on the notification settings, switching from periodic to instant.

**V1 — Guide:** Now do this before you do anything else. Go into your notification settings and change email notifications from periodic to **instantly.**

**V2 — Rep:** *"Why does that matter?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Because by default Trello batches them up and sends them along later. Which means our team asks you something on a Tuesday and you find out about it Thursday, and a launch slides by two days for no reason at all.

**V1 — Guide:** And here is the part almost nobody knows, which is the most useful thing in this entire block. **When you get one of those email notifications, you can just reply to the email.** Your reply lands on the right card automatically.

**V1 — Guide:** So you do not have to log in, find the board, find the client, find the card. You reply from your phone like it is any other email, and it goes exactly where it needs to go.

### The four sections

**ON-SCREEN:** Green box moving to each of the four sections in turn.

**V1 — Guide:** Four sections on your board. You will use one of them constantly and three of them occasionally.

**V1 — Guide:** **Quick resources.** Training links, who to contact for what, blog articles, service timelines, ad specifications. This one changes often, so it is worth a look every few weeks. If you have ever wondered how long something takes or what size an ad needs to be, it is in here.

**V1 — Guide:** **General questions.** For anything not tied to a specific client. How does a tactic work, what does this term mean, is this possible. Post it here and **use the handle** so the right people are notified.

**V1 — Guide:** **Recommendations.** When you submit a request for a recommendation, the card shows up here and stays until the deal is sold or put on hold. That is where you will see questions from our strategy team, and where you can add anything you forgot.

**V1 — Guide:** **And your active clients**, which is where you will spend nearly all your time. Every client has their own list, with cards for their insertion order, their active services, and any questions about them specifically. **Labels tell you the status** — live, on hold, complete — so you can see where everything stands at a glance.

**V1 — Guide:** One habit that matters more than it sounds. **When you comment, use the at symbol and tag somebody.** A comment with no tag is a note nobody gets told about. It just sits there looking like it was handled.

## SEGMENT 5 — WHERE THIS GOES WRONG

**ON-SCREEN:** Each mistake appears as text beside the real board element it relates to, with a **green box** on the element.

**V1 — Guide:** Five things. None of them will get you in trouble, and all of them cost time.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. Moving, creating, or deleting a card.** Please do not. Those cards are in a queue our team works from, and the position is doing a job. **Move a card and it drops out of that queue — which means your campaign quietly stops progressing and nobody finds out for a week.**

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Changing due dates or labels.** Same reason. Those reflect where fulfillment actually is. Change a label to *live* because you assume it is live and now our team is working from information you invented. If something looks wrong, comment on it instead.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Commenting without tagging anybody.** Your question is on the card, so you have done your part — except nobody was told. Then you wait, we wait, and everybody is annoyed about a missing at symbol.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four. Taking it to email or text.** Not because email is forbidden, but because it disappears. The person you told might be out, and in eight months nobody will be able to find what was agreed.

**V1 — Guide:** **Five. Drowning in notifications and blaming Trello.** If you are getting alerts about things that have nothing to do with you, you are probably **watching** a card you do not need to watch. Turn that off on the card itself. And you do not need to request access to our workspace — you already have everything you need.

**V1 — Guide:** And the handoff. From here, everything you do lands on this board. The recommendation you request, the order you submit, the campaign that goes live, the question you ask at four on a Friday. This is the room where all of it happens.

## TRAINER NOTES *(not narrated)*

**Where this sits.** Block 4 of 11. Follows the Resource Section, feeds into MRR Prospect Research. Every subsequent block routes communication through this board, so it has to land.

**The mistakes, with the symptom somebody would notice:**
| Mistake | What it looks like when it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Moving, creating, or deleting a card | Campaign stalls silently. Nobody notices for a week |
| Changing due dates or labels | Fulfillment works from a status the AE invented |
| Commenting without tagging | Question sits unread; both sides think they are waiting on the other |
| Going to email or text | No record, and the recipient may be out |
| Watching cards unnecessarily | Notification fatigue, then the board gets ignored entirely |

**Complete board reference**
- **Quick Resources** — training links, contact routing, blog articles, service timelines, ad specs. Updated frequently
- **General Questions** — non-client-specific questions. Use the group handle
- **Recommendations** — RFR cards land here and stay until the deal is sold or put on hold
- **Active Clients** — one list per client, with cards for the insertion order, active services, and client-specific questions. Labels: live · on hold · complete

**Access notes**
- Invitations arrive from **Trello or Atlassian**. Both legitimate — worth saying, because they look like phishing to a cautious user
- **Sign-up email must match the invitation email**
- Partners should **not** request access to our workspace; their board access is already provisioned
- Three entry points: resource site link · trello.com · mobile app

**The reply-by-email capability is the single most useful thing in this block** and it was buried as a "bonus tip" in the original script. It removes the main friction — an AE between meetings does not have to log in and navigate to a card. Promote it in training and mention it again at the weekly certification meeting.

**⚠️ One thing this script cannot state, and should.** There is **no stated response expectation** for a question posted on the board. This is the most common source of frustration in any shared-board workflow — an AE posts at four on a Friday and has no idea whether to expect an answer that day, Monday, or at all. **Agree an expectation and add it to Segment 2.** Even "our team works the board every business day" is enough. Without it, reps fall back to texting somebody they know, which is the behaviour this block exists to prevent.

**Suggested work product for this block.** Three confirmations before the block is marked complete: the AE has **accepted the invitation**, **switched notifications to instantly**, and **installed the mobile app.** All three are checkable in about a minute.

**Writer's notes:**
- Content is drawn from the original Trello Basics podcast script, re-sequenced into get in, change one setting, learn four sections.
- **Every ground rule was converted from a prohibition into a consequence.** The original said "don't create, move, or delete cards." This version explains that a moved card leaves the working queue and the campaign stalls. Same instruction, and it will actually be followed.
- **Cross-module dependencies:** the Insertion Orders block (9) tells reps the Trello card is built automatically on submission and is their receipt. The RFR block (8) explains what puts a card in Recommendations. The Reporting block (11) tells reps to raise short numbers on the card before a client meeting. If any of those change, check this block.
- The original script's line about not requesting workspace access is kept, since it is a real support ticket generator.
