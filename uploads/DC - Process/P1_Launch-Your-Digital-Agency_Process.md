# Launch Your Digital Agency — Process Script
**Pillar:** Process · Block 1 of 11
**Audience:** Owners, managers, and account executives at partner groups — internal
**Format:** Motion graphics over real screen capture and real photography · AI voice read (two voices) · animated text · green callout boxes
**Runtime:** 1210 counted spoken words ≈ **8:04** at 150 wpm
**Spine — the one sentence somebody must repeat cold:** *You are not adding a service. You are becoming a different kind of company.*

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES — READ BEFORE RECORDING

**Two voices.** **V1 — Guide** carries all teaching. **V2 — Rep** is somebody in the field saying the thing out loud. Distinctly different ElevenLabs voice.

**Written for an AI read.** Short sentences. No semicolons, no parentheticals. Break lines shorter rather than rewriting longer if synthesis stumbles.

**The humour is recognition, not jokes.** Nothing depends on a pause or a dry delivery.

**This block and the next one used to be a single script.** It was split because five topics in one video read as a document. **This block is the decisions about who you are.** The next block, *Getting Ready to Sell*, is everything you do before you talk to a client. Keep them in that order — block two depends on the partnership decision made here.

**This block is different from the rest of the pillar.** It makes an argument rather than teaching a procedure. Warmer, slightly more personal, and the only place where being persuasive is the point.

**Visuals.** Fewer screens than other blocks, so it leans on real photography of partner businesses, real websites and listings, and animated text. **No illustrations, no icon cards, no mascots.**

## SEGMENT 2 — WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

**ON-SCREEN:** Four reasons build as animated text, each with real supporting imagery — a rep presenting, a proposal on a table, a budget shifting. **Green box** on each as it is named.

**V1 — Guide:** Four reasons to do this, and none of them are about being trendy.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. You have to walk the walk.** You are about to sell websites, listings, reviews, and social media. If your own website is eight years old and your listing has the wrong hours, that will come up — not because a client checks, but because you will know, and it shows in how you sell.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Digital has to be in every pitch.** Not a page at the back, not an add-on if there is budget left. The moment digital becomes optional in your presentation, it becomes optional in their budget.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. You need to actually know this stuff.** Not enough to nod along — enough to answer the question in the room without saying you will check.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four, and this is the one that matters most for your business.** Traditional budgets are moving. You watch it happen every year.

**V1 — Guide:** But that money is not disappearing. It is going somewhere. The only question is whether it goes through you, or around you.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So we are protecting the account, not just adding a product."*

**V1 — Guide:** Exactly that. Controlling the digital budget is how you keep the whole relationship, not just part of it.

## SEGMENT 3 — TWO DECISIONS

**ON-SCREEN:** Two numbered decisions animate in as text, then each gets its own real-world visual.

**V1 — Guide:** So let us make the two decisions.

### Decision one — what do you call it?

**ON-SCREEN:** Real examples of partner digital agency brands — logos, a website header, a business card.

**V1 — Guide:** You need a name, a web address, and a look. And here is the first thing to know, because people worry about this one. **You are not doing it by yourself.**

**V1 — Guide:** We have been doing marketing for twenty-five years. So sit down with us. We will help you land on a name, find a web address that is actually available, and then we build the website for you.

**V2 — Rep:** *"So we do not have to go hire somebody for this."*

**V1 — Guide:** You do not. Bring us a few ideas and we will help you get to the right one.

**V1 — Guide:** One piece of advice from experience. Do not overthink the tagline. Do think about the name, because you will be saying it out loud several times a day for years. If it is hard to say on the phone, pick a different one.

### Decision two — how do you describe us?

**ON-SCREEN:** Two paths build as text side by side — *name us as your digital partner* and *complete white label* — with a **green box** on the first.

**V1 — Guide:** This is the most consequential decision you will make in this whole program, so slow down here. You have two choices for how 44i Digital shows up, and we have a recommendation.

**V1 — Guide:** **Option one, and the one we would point you toward. Name us as your digital partner.** You introduce us openly as the specialists behind your digital work. A strategic alliance, out in the open.

**V1 — Guide:** **Option two. Complete white label.** We do everything under your brand and your client never hears our name. That option absolutely exists and some groups use it.

**V1 — Guide:** But get comfortable with the first one. It is cleaner, and it makes everything easier from day one.

**V2 — Rep:** *"Easier how? I thought clients would rather think we did it all."*

**V1 — Guide:** A few reasons. Nobody has to remember what they are allowed to say. You can put us on a call or bring us into a meeting without any awkwardness. And you get to borrow twenty-five years of experience instead of implying you have it.

**V1 — Guide:** Clients find it reassuring, too. A dedicated team behind the work is not a weakness — it is the same reason nobody minds that their accountant uses a specialist for tax law.

**V1 — Guide:** Either way, what does not work is being vague.

**V2 — Rep:** *"What happens if I am vague?"*

**V1 — Guide:** You get asked directly. Usually by the sharpest client you have, usually in front of other people. *"So who actually does this work?"* And if you have not decided, you will hear yourself improvising an answer, and they will hear it too.

**V1 — Guide:** So pick one. Write down how you would answer that question. Make sure everyone on your team answers it the same way. Ten minutes, and it is done.

## SEGMENT 5 — WHERE THIS GOES WRONG

**ON-SCREEN:** Each mistake appears as text with a real-world visual — a business card with no digital mention, two people giving different answers, a half-built brand.

**V1 — Guide:** Four things go wrong at this stage. All of them are avoidable in an afternoon.

**V1 — Guide:** **One. Being vague about who does the work.** Covered already, and worth repeating, because it is the one that costs credibility rather than time.

**V1 — Guide:** **Two. Different people giving different answers.** Worse than being vague. A client hears one version from you and another from somebody in the office, and now they are wondering what else is unclear. Write it down and circulate it.

**V1 — Guide:** **Three. Building the brand from scratch.** Groups commission a logo, pay for graphics, and put together a website — all of which we would have helped with, and some of which already exists. Ask first.

**V1 — Guide:** **Four. Picking a name nobody can say.** It sounds trivial until you have said it four hundred times on the phone. Say your shortlist out loud before you commit.

**V1 — Guide:** And the handoff from here is straightforward. Once you know what you are called and how you describe us, the next question is whether you are actually ready to sell it. That means getting your own house in order and getting your team trained — which is exactly the next block.

## TRAINER NOTES *(not narrated)*

**Where this sits.** Block 1 of 11. Nothing precedes it. Feeds directly into *Getting Ready to Sell*, which depends on the partnership decision made here.

**This block behaves differently from the rest of the pillar.** It makes an argument rather than teaching a procedure. No forms, no click paths, no fulfillment handoff. Treat the tonal difference as deliberate.

**The decision that actually matters.** The white label versus named partner choice has lasting consequences. **Groups should make it, write down the answer, and make sure every client-facing person gives the same answer.** The failure is not picking wrong. The failure is not picking, then improvising in front of a client.

**The mistakes, with the symptom somebody would notice:**
| Mistake | What it looks like when it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Vague about who does the work | Asked directly in front of others, and the answer sounds improvised |
| Two people, two answers | Client hears both versions and starts questioning everything else |
| Building the brand from scratch | Money and weeks spent on assets that already existed |
| A name nobody can say | Four hundred awkward phone calls, and no way back |

**Suggested work product for this block.** A one-page launch sheet with three things written down: **the agency name, the chosen partnership model, and the exact answer to "who does the work?"** If a group finishes this block without those three, the block did not land.
