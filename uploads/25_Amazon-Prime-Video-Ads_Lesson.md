# Amazon Prime Video Ads — Lesson Script
**Pillar:** Targeted Digital Marketing (Room #25 in the Digital House)
**Audience:** Media AEs and agency personnel learning digital, selling to small local businesses in towns under 100k
**Format:** Browser module — animated text + real photos/screenshots + green-box callouts + voice-over (multi-voice). No line illustrations or metaphor graphics.
**Runtime:** 1741 counted spoken words ≈ **11:36** at 150 wpm
**Voice:** Plain-spoken, analogy-first, define every term, "you've got this" energy. No pricing, no internal specifics.
**Spine — the one sentence a trainee must repeat cold:** *Everybody else guesses what people want. Amazon knows what they bought.*

## SECTION A — The Reusable Tactic Lesson Template
*(Identical across all modules. Only the content changes — the beats never do.)*

**Voice convention:** **V1 — Guide** carries the teaching. **V2 — Scene** delivers only in-world lines and the quiz prompts.

**On-screen kit:** animated text · real photos and product screenshots · green callout boxes. **No line illustrations, icon cards, mascots, or drawn metaphors.** Spoken analogies stay spoken.

| # | Segment | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook + What It Is** | Relatable moment → one-sentence definition |
| 2 | **Why It Matters** | Why an advertiser should do this + the evidence |
| 3 | **Who It's Great For** | 3 categories, one reason each + who it's wrong for |
| 4 | **How It Works — 3 Core Mechanics** | Exactly 3, roughly 4 beats each |
| 5 | **Common Terms** | Four to five terms maximum |
| 6 | **Features, Benefits & The Payoff** | Included → benefits → one payoff story |
| 7 | **Recap + Quiz Bridge** | Restate the 3 mechanics, warm handoff |

**Quiz blueprint:** Q1–3 the three mechanics · Q4–6 terms · Q7 fit · Q8 scenario · Q9–10 features/benefits. Vary the correct letter across A–D.

## SECTION B — Amazon Prime Video Ads: Full Script

### SEGMENT 1 — Hook + What It Is
**ON-SCREEN:** Real photo of a family watching Prime Video on a smart TV. Then a real full-screen streaming ad break, **green box** on the whole frame with label *full screen, no skip.* Then two text columns building side by side: *what they watched · what they bought*, with a green box on the second.

**V2 — Scene:** *"He's had a Prime account since college. Everything comes in a box with a smile on it."*
**V1 — Guide:** Which means Amazon knows something about that man that nobody else in advertising does. Not what he browsed. Not what he watched. **What he actually bought** — for years.
**V1 — Guide:** So, plainly: **Amazon Prime Video ads** are video ads running inside Prime Video — the originals, the movies, the series, the live football — targeted using Amazon's own information about what people purchase.
**V1 — Guide:** Now, we sell several streaming television products, so let's place this one. **Streaming TV** runs across services like Hulu and Tubi. **YouTube TV** is the live channel lineup — local news and sports. **This one** is Prime Video specifically, and what makes it different isn't where it plays. It's what it knows.
**V1 — Guide:** Every other targeting method in this entire catalog is an inference. What somebody searched, what page they read, what show they watched, what the Census says about their neighborhood. All useful, all guesses. **Purchase history isn't a guess.**
**V1 — Guide:** It's bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis**, and one impression means the ad played full-screen on one screen during somebody's show. And the line to carry: **everybody else guesses what people want — Amazon knows what they bought.**

### SEGMENT 2 — Why It Matters
**ON-SCREEN:** Two statistics build as large animated numbers. Then a real full-screen ad frame with a **green box** on it. Then a text pair: *inferred interest* / *actual purchase*, second one boxed green.

**V1 — Guide:** So why should an advertiser do this? Three reasons.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: the audience is enormous and it's already paying for it.** Around two-thirds of American households use Prime Video, largely because it comes bundled with the Prime membership most of them already have. And ads are the default experience — the large majority of subscribers watch with commercials rather than paying extra to remove them.
**V1 — Guide:** So this isn't a small corner of streaming. Prime Video reaches well over a hundred million ad-supported viewers in the United States and takes a real share of all American television viewing.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: targeting on purchases is a genuinely different thing.** This is the whole reason to sell this product, so don't rush it.
**V1 — Guide:** Think about how we've targeted everything else. Somebody read an article about boats, so maybe they want a boat. Somebody watched a fishing show, so maybe they fish. Those are reasonable guesses and they work. But **somebody who bought a tackle box, a rod, and two hundred dollars of tackle last spring is not a guess.** That's a fisherman.
**V1 — Guide:** For a local business that sells in a category people also buy online, that distinction is worth a great deal.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: it's full-screen, unskippable, and safe.** The ad takes the whole screen and the viewer can't click past it. And it runs beside professionally produced content — big-budget originals and live sports — not beside whatever somebody uploaded. That's what people mean by **brand-safe**, and for a business protective of its name in a small town, it matters.
**V1 — Guide:** One honest note on budget, and it's the same one from the other television products. This is among the most expensive things per thousand that we sell. A client who can't fund it properly reaches almost nobody, and you're better off recommending a different video product than selling a campaign that disappears.

### SEGMENT 3 — Who It's Great For
**ON-SCREEN:** Three real photos, one at a time — a sporting goods or outdoor store, a specialty retailer, a business with a household product line. Each type's name and reason type in beside it.

**V1 — Guide:** Three easy targets.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses in a category people also shop online.** Sporting goods, outdoor gear, auto parts, pet supplies, hobby, tools, home goods, baby. Amazon can find the people who buy in that category — and those are the same people walking past the client's store.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses whose customer is hard to describe but easy to recognize by behavior.** "People who fish" isn't a demographic you can buy anywhere else. "People who bought fishing gear" is.
**V1 — Guide:** **Businesses that need to look substantial next to national brands.** A full-screen unskippable ad in the middle of a premium original does something for a local business that no banner ever will.
**V1 — Guide:** And the honest flip side: if a client's category has no real online-purchase footprint, the purchase-data advantage mostly disappears and a standard streaming TV buy will serve them just as well for less. Say that. And as with all television, a thin budget here buys nothing anybody notices.

### SEGMENT 4 — How It Works: 3 Core Mechanics
**ON-SCREEN:** (1) Real Prime Video content stills, then a full-screen ad frame, **green box** on it → *premium, full screen, no skip*. (2) A shopper-audience panel building as text — category buyers, in-market segments — **green box** on it → *targeted on purchases*. (3) Geography, demographics, topics and channels building as text beside a report → *layer it and manage it*. Closes on text: *Premium and unskippable · Purchase-based · Layered.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three moving parts.
**V1 — Guide:** **One: premium content, full screen, no skip.** The ads run inside Prime Video's own programming — originals, licensed movies and series, and live sports. It's a full-screen ad on the biggest screen in the house, and on ad-supported Prime Video the viewer watches it.
**V1 — Guide:** And the environment is controlled. Professionally produced content only. A client never has to worry about what their ad ended up next to, which is a real anxiety for businesses in small towns where everybody knows everybody.
**V1 — Guide:** **Two: targeted on what people bought.** This is the mechanic that makes the product, so learn how to say it.
**V1 — Guide:** Amazon builds **shopper audiences** — groups of people defined by real purchasing behavior in a category, plus interest-based audiences on top. So instead of asking a client to describe their customer's age and income, you can ask a much better question: *what do your customers buy?*
**V1 — Guide:** And be precise about the privacy side, because a client will ask. Nobody is handing over an individual's shopping list. These are **aggregated audience groups** — thousands of households that share a purchasing pattern. No names, no order histories, nothing about one specific person. Say it exactly that way.
**V1 — Guide:** **Three: layer the rest and manage it.** On top of the shopper audiences, there's geographic targeting to keep the money where the client can actually serve, demographic targeting if it's worth narrowing further, and identification of topics, placements, and channels so the ads run against fitting content.
**V1 — Guide:** Then full campaign management, weekly refinement, reporting, and video production if they need it.

### SEGMENT 5 — Common Terms
**ON-SCREEN:** Each term and definition animate in as text, with green boxes on a full-screen ad frame and a shopper-audience panel.

**V1 — Guide:** Four terms and you're fluent.
**V1 — Guide:** **Shopper targeting** — reaching people based on what they've actually purchased, rather than what we assume they're interested in. The single distinctive thing about this product.
**V1 — Guide:** **Brand safety** — the assurance that an ad appears beside professionally produced content, not beside something that could embarrass the business. Clients care about this more than reps expect.
**V1 — Guide:** **Non-skippable** — the ad runs; the viewer can't click past it. That's the trade for the ad-supported tier, and it's why the spot gets seen.
**V1 — Guide:** **Consideration** — the stage between somebody knowing a business exists and actually buying. Most advertising either builds awareness or asks for the sale; this works the middle, which is where most decisions are actually made.

### SEGMENT 6 — Features, Benefits & The Payoff
**ON-SCREEN:** Feature list animates in. Four benefit lines: *Reach people who actually buy the category · Full screen, can't be skipped · Premium and brand-safe · Local geography on a national platform.* Payoff: a real outdoor-store photo beside a full-screen Prime Video ad. Small tag: *illustrative example.*

**V1 — Guide:** What's in it? Full-screen non-skippable video ads inside Prime Video programming. Shopper and interest-based targeting built on real purchasing behavior. Geographic targeting. Demographic targeting. Topics, placements, and channel targeting identified. Full campaign management with weekly refinement. Regular reporting. And video production if they need it.
**V1 — Guide:** What does the owner get? They **reach people who actually buy in their category**, not people we hope are interested. An ad that's **full screen and can't be skipped.** A **premium, brand-safe environment** beside real programming. And **local geography on a national platform**, so they aren't paying to reach the whole country.
**V1 — Guide:** So picture an independent outdoor and sporting goods store. Great staff, deep knowledge, and they lose customers constantly to online ordering — people who buy their gear in a box and never walk in.
**V1 — Guide:** After: a full-screen spot during Prime Video programming, aimed at households in their region who buy in exactly those categories — hunting, fishing, camping. The people ordering gear online became the audience. And the ad said the thing only a local store can say: come in, we'll show you how it works, and we'll be here when it breaks.
**V2 — Scene:** *"I've been buying this stuff online for years. I didn't know you carried it nine minutes from my house."*
**V1 — Guide:** — and that's the whole play. Amazon's data found the buyers. The local business made the case that a box can't. *(Illustrative — not a promise of specific numbers.)*

### SEGMENT 7 — Recap
**ON-SCREEN:** Recap text — 1: premium and unskippable · 2: purchase-based targeting · 3: layered and managed.

**V1 — Guide:** Three parts. One: premium Prime Video content, full screen, can't be skipped, brand-safe. Two: targeting built on what people actually bought, as aggregated audience groups rather than anybody's individual history. Three: layer geography, demographics, and content placement on top, then manage it weekly.
**V1 — Guide:** And two things to carry in. **Everybody else guesses what people want — Amazon knows what they bought.** And the better question to ask a client: not *who* is your customer, but *what do your customers buy?*

### SEGMENT 8 — Into the Room
**ON-SCREEN:** Objections as animated text. Then a split — *guessing at intent* against *aggregated purchase behaviour* — with a **green box** on the second. Then three sentences. Then *Quiz →*.

**V1 — Guide:** Before the quiz — two questions you'll be asked, the argument I'd lead with, and one thing to settle before you leave.

**V1 — Guide:** First, and it needs precise words rather than enthusiastic ones.

**V2 — Scene:** *"How do you know what people buy?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Say this and stop. **"Amazon builds audience groups from shopping behaviour. Nobody sees an individual's order history — not us, not you."**

**V1 — Guide:** That's accurate and complete. This is the question on this product where overstating capability could cause a real problem, so use the words as written.

**V1 — Guide:** Second, and it comes from anybody already buying streaming.

**V2 — Scene:** *"Isn't this the same as the streaming TV you already sold me?"*

**V1 — Guide:** Same living room, different buy. **"That one spreads across a range of services. This one is Prime Video specifically — and it's the only place you can aim using shopping behaviour."**

**V1 — Guide:** Now the argument I'd lead with.

**V1 — Guide:** **Everybody else guesses what people want. Amazon knows what they bought.**

**V1 — Guide:** Think about how targeting has always worked in your business. Age, income, zip code, what somebody listens to, what they watch. Every one of those is a proxy — a reasonable guess that the person might be in the market.

**V1 — Guide:** This is the one place where the signal isn't a guess. Somebody bought pet supplies. Somebody bought tools. Somebody bought baby products. **That's not an inference about intent. That's a transaction.**

**V1 — Guide:** And it's a genuinely different sales conversation, because it moves the discussion from *who might want this* to *who has already shown they buy in this category.*

**V1 — Guide:** Three sentences to know cold. To open: **"Everybody else guesses what people want. Amazon knows what they bought."** On the privacy question: **"Aggregated shopping groups — nobody sees an individual's orders."** And to place it against the broad buy: **"Same living room. This is the only one aimed by shopping behaviour."**

**V1 — Guide:** And the one thing to settle before you leave — this carries a premium cost, so an underfunded buy spread wide will disappear. **And be clear about what we don't sell.** We do not offer shoppable ad formats. If a client asks whether people can buy directly from the ad, the answer is no, and it's far better to say so now.

**V1 — Guide:** That's Amazon Prime Video. Quick ten-question check.

## SECTION D — Trainer & Writer Reference *(not narrated)*

> **Note on structure.** The objections, lines, and first-meeting warning that reps most need are **narrated in Segment 8**, immediately before the quiz. Everything below is reference for trainers and for the rep card.

**How the advertiser pays.** Bought on a **cost-per-thousand impression basis**. One impression means the ad played full-screen on one screen during programming. Impression arithmetic is taught once, in Targeted Display.

**Where this sits among the streaming TV products.**
- **Streaming TV (Room 23):** the broad OTT/CTV buy across Hulu, Tubi, Pluto and the networks. ACR, Census, and audience targeting. Zip-code geography.
- **Social OTT/CTV (Room 17):** same streaming delivery, social-style creative instead of a commercial.
- **YouTube TV (Room 24):** the live channel lineup — local news, live sports.
- **Amazon Prime Video (this, Room 25):** Prime Video content specifically, and **the only one targeted on actual purchase behavior.**

If a client asks why this one costs more than a general streaming buy, the honest answer is the data — you're paying for purchase-based targeting and premium inventory, not just for a TV screen.

**Budget reality.** Alongside YouTube TV, this is at the top of the catalog for cost per thousand. **An underfunded campaign here is invisible.** If the budget won't carry it, recommend Room 23 instead and keep the relationship. That recommendation earns more trust than the sale would.

**How it's sold.** The opener is a question no other product lets you ask: *"What do your customers buy?"* Not who they are — what they buy. Owners answer that instantly and precisely, and every answer is a targetable audience. For any retailer losing business to online ordering, follow it with: *"Those people are your customers. Let's go get them."*

**Bundles with:**
- **Streaming TV (Room 23)** — broader reach at a lower cost per thousand, with this as the precision layer. Common combination.
- **Geofencing and mobile audience targeting** — the offline visit measurement conversation pairs naturally.
- **Website / landing pages** — the ad drives searches and direct visits rather than clicks, so the site has to hold up.
- **Reputation management** — somebody who sees a full-screen premium ad will look the business up. A weak star rating wastes an expensive impression.

**Objections and responses:**
- *"How do you know what people buy?"* → Careful and exact: Amazon builds aggregated audience groups from purchasing behavior. No names, no individual order histories, nothing about one specific person. Never imply otherwise.
- *"Isn't this the same as the streaming TV you already sold me?"* → "Same kind of screen, different data. That one targets on viewing and geography. This one targets on what households actually purchase."
- *"Why does it cost more?"* → Be straight: premium inventory plus the purchase data. If that precision isn't worth it for their category, recommend the broader streaming buy instead.
- *"Everybody pays to remove the ads."* → Most Prime Video subscribers watch with ads — it's the default experience, and removing them costs extra.
- *"I compete with Amazon. Why would I advertise there?"* → This is the best objection on the product and the answer is strong: *those are your customers, buying your category, right now. This is the one way to talk to them — and you can say the thing a box can't.*
- *"Can people buy from the ad / add it to their cart?"* → **No. We do not sell shoppable formats.** Amazon offers ad formats that let a viewer add a product to their cart from the ad, and Amazon markets them heavily, so a client may well have read about them. Our product is a full-screen non-skippable video ad. Say that plainly rather than guessing, and never imply the ad is transactional.
- *"Can you target my specific zip codes?"* → **Don't answer this from memory.** Geographic targeting is available, but the granularity on this platform is not confirmed to match the zip-code precision of our standard streaming TV product. Check before you quote it — see the caution below.

**Lines for the room:**
- *"Everybody else guesses what people want. Amazon knows what they bought."*
- *"What do your customers buy? Not who they are — what they buy."*
- *"'People who fish' isn't a demographic you can buy anywhere else. 'People who bought fishing gear' is."*
- *"Those people ordering online are your customers. This is how you talk to them."*

**Retention note.** Two risks. **Budget mismatch**, which is the main one and preventable by being candid about cost per thousand upfront. And **measurement** — there's no click on a television, so agree at the first meeting on what you'll watch together: site traffic, branded search, store visits, and staff hearing customers mention it.

**Writer's notes:**
- **Statistics sources:** roughly two-thirds of US households using Prime Video, and the large majority of subscribers remaining on the ad-supported default, come from 2025–2026 industry reporting on Prime Video's ad tier. Prime Video's US ad-supported reach of well over 100 million viewers is reported by **EMARKETER** (over 315 million globally as of late 2025). Its share of total US TV viewing is tracked in Nielsen-based reporting. **Attribute as "industry reporting shows" and re-verify before recording — this product launched its ad tier recently and the figures are still moving quickly.**
- **Deliberately excluded:** the CPM figure, the monthly minimum, the contract term, and the production quote. Completion-rate figures were excluded per the standing rule; the script conveys the same point through "non-skippable."
- **⚠️ Geographic granularity — confirm, and until then treat it as unresolved.** Room 23 offers zip-code targeting. This sheet says only "geographic targeting," so the script deliberately says geography is "set with the client" and never names a level of precision. **Reps must not assume zip-code targeting carries over from the streaming TV product.** Confirm the actual available granularity with the platform and add it to Part 1; until that happens, the objection response above tells reps to check rather than quote.

**Shoppable formats: confirmed not sold.** Amazon offers ad formats that let viewers add products to a cart from the ad. **We do not offer that.** It's deliberately absent from the script — no reason to teach an absence on camera — but it's in the objection list above because Amazon promotes those formats heavily and clients will have read about them.
- **Privacy language is a genuine compliance item.** Reps say "aggregated audience groups built from purchasing behavior" and never anything implying access to an individual's purchase history. Q8 tests this, and it's the same discipline used in the geofencing and CRM modules.
- **Payoff vertical (outdoor and sporting goods store)** is new. Updated list: diner, roofer, flooring co., hearing clinic, insurance agency, storage co., garden center, medical clinic, auto repair shop, pest control co., RV dealership, used-car lot, physical therapy clinic, wedding/event venue, veterinary clinic, private school, clothing boutique, brewery/taproom, home builder, paint store, coffee drive-thru, technical college, senior living community, farm equipment dealer, community credit union, furniture store, **outdoor/sporting goods store**.
